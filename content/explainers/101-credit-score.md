# Credit Score & Credit Bureaus (CICs) — Complete 101 Guide

*Last updated: November 2026*

## What is a Credit Score?

A credit score is a three-digit number that summarises your credit history — how you have borrowed and repaid loans and credit cards — into a single measure of creditworthiness that lenders use when deciding whether to give you credit and on what terms. In India, all licensed credit information companies (CICs, commonly called credit bureaus) must calibrate consumer credit scores on a common scale of 300 to 900, as mandated by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI); the closer the score is to 900, the higher the perceived creditworthiness. [^1]

The score is derived from the "Accounts" and "Enquiries" sections of your credit information report (CIR) — the detailed record of your loans, credit cards, repayment history, and lender enquiries maintained by each bureau. [^2]

Credit bureaus in India operate under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005 (CICRA), with the Rules and Regulations of 2006 framed under it, and are licensed and regulated by the RBI. [^3] The oldest and best known is TransUnion CIBIL (formerly Credit Information Bureau (India) Limited), set up in 2000, which launched India's first generic credit score in 2011. [^4]

### Key features

- **Scale**: 300–900 across all four CICs (mandated common calibration) [^1]
- **Scope**: Consumer (retail) credit data; bureaus also maintain commercial/MSME and microfinance databases
- **Mandatory participation**: Every credit institution — banks, NBFCs, housing finance companies, asset reconstruction companies and others — must be a member of all registered CICs and report credit information to each of them [^5]
- **Consumer access**: One free full credit report including the score, once every calendar year, from each CIC [^1]

## How it Works

### 1. Lenders report your credit data

Banks and other lenders submit account-level data (loan type, sanctioned amount, outstanding balance, days past due, written-off/settled status, and more) to all four CICs in a standardised Uniform Credit Reporting Format. Because the same file goes to all four bureaus, an error at the lender's end propagates to all four. [^5]

### 2. Reporting frequency has sharply increased

| Period | Reporting cycle | Effective date |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Legacy | Monthly | — |
| Fortnightly | As on the 15th and last day of each month, submitted within 7 days | January 1, 2025 [^6] |
| Four reference dates a month | As on the 9th, 16th, 23rd and last day of the month | July 1, 2026 [^7] |

The July 2026 regime was originally proposed for April 1, 2026; after industry feedback, RBI deferred it to July 1, 2026 and eased some operational requirements. [^8] (Note: several press reports in January 2026 described the change as "weekly updates from January 1, 2026"; RBI's final amendment directions instead prescribe four reference dates from July 1, 2026 — RBI's own text never uses the word "weekly". [^8] [^5]) The practical effect for consumers: repayments, closures and defaults now reflect on credit reports far faster than the old 30–45 day lag.

### 3. Bureaus validate and compute the score

CICs validate incoming data and reject records with errors; lenders must rectify rejected data and resubmit it within seven days of the rejection report. [^1] Each bureau then computes its score from the Accounts and Enquiry sections of the report. [^2] Four key factors influence the CIBIL score: payment history, credit mix and duration, credit utilisation, and other factors such as multiple recent enquiries. [^9]

### 4. Lenders pull your report when you apply

When you apply for a loan or credit card, the lender retrieves your credit report and score from one or more bureaus as a "Specified User" for credit appraisal. Which bureau a lender pulls varies — CIBIL is the primary bureau for most banks, Experian is common among fintech platforms, Equifax in microfinance/rural lending, and CRIF High Mark in MSME and two-wheeler finance. [^10]

### 5. Enquiries: hard vs soft

Enquiries recorded by lenders when you apply for credit ("hard pulls") appear on your report; multiple applications in a short window can lower your score. Checking your own score or report is a "soft enquiry" and does not affect your credit score. [^11]

## Types/Categories

### Consumer vs commercial credit information

- **Consumer credit reports/scores**: For individuals — loans, credit cards, enquiries, repayment history (300–900 scale). [^1]
- **Commercial/MSME reports**: Bureaus also maintain business credit databases and scores; CRIF, for example, provides both personal and business credit scores through its portal. [^17]

### Report types

- **Credit Information Report (CIR)**: The standard report supplied to lenders. [^1]
- **Comprehensive Credit Information Report (CCIR)**: An additional report format combining consumer, commercial and microfinance data on a borrower. [^1]

### Scores across bureaus

All four bureaus' scores are equally valid; each is computed on the bureau's own file and model, so a spread of tens of points across bureaus is normal, driven by differences in lender coverage, scoring models, and update timing. A spread of ~100 points usually signals an error present in one bureau's file but not the others. [^18] [^19]

## Regulatory Framework

1. **CICRA, 2005** — The parent law regulating CICs; RBI registers and supervises CICs under it, with the CIC Rules and Regulations, 2006 framing operational requirements. [^3]
2. **Master Direction – RBI (Credit Information Reporting) Directions, 2025** — Issued January 6, 2025 (Circular RBI/DoR/2024-25/125), consolidating all instructions on credit information reporting; covers data submission, consumer access (including the free annual full credit report), alerts, and a compensation framework for delayed updation/rectification. [^20] [^1]
3. **Credit Information Reporting (Amendment) Directions, 2025** — Issued December 4, 2025; moves lenders to four monthly reference dates from July 1, 2026, adds data-quality commitments (file-level Data Quality Index within 3 days of data receipt), and requires rectification of rejected data before the next cycle. [^7] [^8]
4. **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025** — Notified in November 2025; CICs processing personal data are "data fiduciaries" under this regime, with phased compliance: Data Protection Board constituted (Nov 2025), consent-manager framework operational November 13, 2026, and full substantive obligations by May 13, 2027, with penalties that can reach ₹250 crore. [^21] [^22]
5. **RBI – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS), 2026** — Effective July 1, 2026, replacing the 2021 scheme; covers complaints against CICs along with banks, NBFCs and PPI issuers. [^23] [^24]

**Conflicting information note**: Early-2026 press coverage (e.g., new-year rules round-ups) stated bureaus would update data weekly from January 1, 2026; RBI's final amendment directions set the operative date at July 1, 2026, and describe four reference dates rather than a strict weekly cycle. [^8]

## Consumer Rights Analysis

RBI's 2025 Master Direction gives borrowers specific, enforceable rights: [^1]

1. **One free full credit report (FFCR) including the credit score, once every calendar year** (January–December), in electronic form, from each CIC — and each CIC must prominently display the FFCR access link on its website homepage. [^1] Free report channels: CIBIL's MyCIBIL portal (Free Annual Credit Report), [^25] Equifax's online free-report flow, [^26] CRIF's free annual report, [^17] and Experian's annual free report. [^27] Free-report entitlements are long-standing RBI mandates; extra reports beyond the free one are paid. [^27]
2. **Alerts**: CICs must send SMS/email alerts when your CIR is accessed by lenders; lenders must alert you when submitting default/days-past-due information about you — an early-warning system against fraudulent loans in your name. [^1]
3. **Correction within 30 days**: Under Section 21(3) of CICRA, you can ask a lender or CIC to update/correct your credit information, which must be done within 30 days of the request. [^1]
4. **₹100/day compensation**: If your complaint is not resolved within 30 days of filing with a lender/CIC, you are entitled to ₹100 per calendar day of delay, shared between the responsible lender and/or bureau. [^1]
5. **Reasons for rejection**: Lenders must inform you of the reasons for rejecting your data-correction request. [^1]

### Gaps the consumer should know

- **Compensation is not automatic** — you must complain, and enforcement has been weak: RBI penalised TransUnion CIBIL (₹26.82 lakh), CRIF High Mark and Equifax on August 31, 2026 precisely for failing to credit compensation to eligible complainants within the prescribed period. [^15] [^16] The penalty validates the right; it does not deliver it. Claim the ₹100/day yourself.
- **An error fixed at one bureau may persist at others** — the same lender file feeds all four bureaus, so dispute at the source (the lender) and verify corrections across all four reports. [^5]
- **Which bureau a lender pulls is the lender's choice, not yours** — a clean CIBIL score does not protect you from an error on your Equifax or CRIF file. [^10]
- **Third-party "free score" apps add intermediaries** — prefer the bureaus' own free annual channels, and read what you consent to when using aggregator apps. [^19]

## Privacy Implications

- **What is held**: Your identifiers (name, date of birth, PAN, addresses, phone, employment/income details as reported) plus account-level credit data, repayment/delinquency history, and enquiry records. [^2]
- **Who can access**: Only registered "Specified Users" (member lenders) for permissible purposes such as credit appraisal; sharing credit information received under the Act with any other person violates Section 17(4) of CICRA and Regulation 9 of the CIC Regulations, 2006. [^1]
- **Access transparency**: You must be alerted by SMS/email when your report is accessed by a lender. [^1]
- **DPDP regime**: CICs are data fiduciaries under the DPDP Act/Rules, subject to consent, security-safeguard, breach-notification and data-principal-rights obligations on the phased 2025–2027 timeline. [^21] [^22]
- **Litigation watch**: A public interest litigation on CIBIL data privacy is reported to be pending before the Telangana High Court, where the court has been pressing the Union finance ministry for a response — a signal of unresolved systemic privacy concerns around bureau data. [^28]

## Safeguards

1. **Pull your free report from all four bureaus every year** (soft enquiries, no score impact) — this is the single most effective defence against errors and fraudulent loans. [^1] [^11]
2. **Keep alerts on**: Ensure your current mobile number/email is registered with lenders so access and default-reporting alerts reach you. [^1]
3. **Mind the levers that move the score**: Pay EMIs/card dues on time; keep credit utilisation low; maintain a healthy mix of secured and unsecured credit; avoid clustered loan/card applications. [^9]
4. **Fix errors at the source**: Complain to the lender first when its data is wrong (with evidence like payment receipts and NOCs), and to the bureau for cross-lender or bureau-side issues; track the 30-day clock and claim ₹100/day on delay. [^1]
5. **Use your DPDP rights**: Request access, correction and grievance redress from bureaus as data fiduciaries as the regime's obligations come into force through 2026–27. [^21]
6. **Escalate deliberately**: If unresolved, take the complaint to the RBI Ombudsman (below) rather than letting it lapse.

## Complaints & Grievance Redressal

**Step 1 — Complain to the lender or bureau.** Register the complaint in writing (keep the acknowledgement) with the credit institution whose data is wrong, or with the CIC if the issue spans multiple lenders or is bureau-side; the CIC coordinates with all concerned lenders when multiple lenders' data is involved. [^1]

**Step 2 — Watch the 30-day clock.** The lender/CIC must update/rectify within 30 days of your request; beyond that, compensation of ₹100 per calendar day accrues until resolution. [^1]

**Step 3 — Escalate to the RBI Ombudsman.** If the entity rejects your complaint, replies unsatisfactorily, or does not respond within 30 days, file with the RBI Integrated Ombudsman (RB-IOS, 2026) — which covers CICs — via the CMS portal at cms.rbi.org.in, by email to crpc@rbi.org.in, or by post to the Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre, RBI, Chandigarh; the helpline is 14440. Complaints must be filed within one year of the entity's reply (or one year and 30 days if no reply). [^29] [^23] [^24]

**Step 4 — Claim your compensation explicitly.** The Aug/Sep 2026 penalties on all three penalised bureaus show that compensation owed to complainants was not always credited; treat the ₹100/day as your claim to make and track, not a benefit that arrives on its own. [^15] [^16]
