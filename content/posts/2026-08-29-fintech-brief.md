# Fintech Brief — August 29, 2026

## Allahabad HC Issues Notice to RBI, NPCI on Plea for UPI "Accept/Decline" Option

The Allahabad High Court's Lucknow Bench has issued notice to the RBI, NPCI, and the Union Ministry of Finance over a PIL seeking a mandatory "Accept/Decline" mechanism for incoming electronic payments. A Division Bench of Justice Rajan Roy and Justice Manjive Shukla heard the petition filed by advocate Anupriya Agarwal on August 24. [^1]

The petitioner argues that the absence of any consent mechanism for incoming UPI and other electronic credits undermines customer autonomy, privacy, and protection against misuse. The plea seeks directions for the RBI to frame guidelines requiring payment service providers to offer recipients the ability to accept or decline incoming payments before they hit the account.

This is significant. India's UPI ecosystem processed 23.66 billion transactions worth ₹29.88 lakh crore in July 2026 alone — a record. Yet the recipient has zero say in whether an incoming credit lands in their account. In a context where bank accounts are being frozen over alleged cyber-fraud transactions as small as ₹105 or ₹200, giving users agency over incoming credits is a consumer-protection question the RBI has so far sidestepped.

## ESDS Software IPO Fully Subscribed on Day 1; ₹720 Crore Issue Draws Heavy Demand

Nashik-based cloud and data centre company ESDS Software Solution saw its ₹720 crore IPO fully subscribed within hours of opening on August 28, with overall subscription hitting 102% by midday. The company had already raised ₹216 crore from anchor investors including Motilal Oswal MF, Bandhan MF, and Quant MF at the upper price band of ₹429 per share. Grey market premium signals a potential 76-77% listing gain. [^2]

The IPO is a pure fresh issue of 1.68 crore shares with no offer-for-sale component. ESDS provides managed cloud hosting, e-Governance solutions, and AI-driven data centre services — positioning itself in the India Stack ecosystem. The strong demand reflects continued investor appetite for infrastructure-adjacent tech IPOs, even as broader market conditions remain cautious.

## Merchant Digital Payments Rise 19.6% YoY to ₹11.7 Lakh Crore in July

Digital payments to merchants across India rose 19.6% year-on-year to approximately ₹11.7 lakh crore in July 2026, underscoring the deepening penetration of QR-code and POS-based digital payments at the retail level. [^3]

This merchant-payment figure is a subset of UPI's overall July numbers — the 23.66 billion total UPI transactions include P2P transfers, bill payments, and merchant payments. The standalone merchant data suggests that the Paytm-PhonePe-Google Pay competitive battle for offline merchant onboarding continues to translate into real volume growth, even as MDR on UPI merchant transactions remains zero for sub-₹2,000 payments.

The PIB's UPI factsheet released this week also highlighted that UPI now accounts for 84% of India's total digital payment transactions by volume in FY2025-26, up from 15% in FY19. Cross-border UPI linkages are now live in 11 countries, with the Maldives' Favara system going live on July 30. [^4]

## Stripe-Advent Consortium Abandons $53 Billion PayPal Takeover Bid

The proposed $53 billion acquisition of PayPal by a Stripe-Advent International consortium is officially off, according to sources cited by Reuters and FinTech Magazine. PayPal shares fell on the news. [^5]

While this is a global payments story, it has India-relevant implications. PayPal operates a significant cross-border payments business in India, and Stripe has been aggressively expanding its India presence. The collapse of the deal means PayPal continues under CEO Enrique Lores's standalone turnaround plan — the company recently raised its 2026 profit forecast and outlined cost-cutting measures. For the Indian fintech ecosystem, the key takeaway is that the global payments consolidation wave continues to stutter, keeping the competitive landscape fragmented.
