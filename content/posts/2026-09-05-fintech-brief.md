# Fintech Brief — September 05, 2026

## RBI Pulls Out a Record 30-Day VRRR to Drain a ₹10.3 Lakh Crore Liquidity Flood

The Reserve Bank of India's liquidity plumbing is working overtime. With banking-system surplus hitting a record ₹10.32 lakh crore on September 3 — the residue of $130+ billion flooding in through the special FCNR(B) window and subsequent dollar-rupee swaps — RBI absorbed ₹6.02 lakh crore across two three-day variable rate reverse repo auctions on Friday, accepting ₹5.41 lakh crore in one at a 5.24% cut-off against a ₹7 lakh crore notification.

Now it has announced a record 30-day VRRR for ₹7 lakh crore on September 7, with reversal on October 7 and a premature-reversal option (two working days' notice) — a flexibility signal that banks may need the money back sooner. Overnight call money is already trading at 4.95%, below the 5.25% repo rate, and the central bank separately sold ₹32,000 crore of government bonds, with the new five-year paper cut off at 6.53%. Forex reserves touched a record $740.80 billion on the back of the swap dollars.

Why it matters for consumers: this is the expensive clean-up after the FX-swap party. Business Standard notes the longer-duration absorption raises the odds of a rate-hike debate at the October policy — and the cost of sterilisation eventually shows up in the RBI's surplus transfer to the government, i.e., the public exchequer.

## SEBI Clears the NSE IPO — India's Biggest-Ever Public Issue Is Days Away

The nine-year wait is over. SEBI issued its observation letter on September 4, clearing the National Stock Exchange's ₹30,000 crore IPO — an entire offer-for-sale of 14.89 crore shares that would comfortably beat Hyundai Motor India and LIC to become the largest Indian public issue ever. Reuters reports the exchange targets a listing in the week of September 21: RHP filing by Tuesday, price band around September 11, and the issue opening around September 15, with shares listing on BSE — the NSE cannot list on itself.

The clearance follows the Supreme Court's dismissal this week of SEBI's own appeals in the long-running NSE co-location and dark-fibre cases — the governance disputes that stalled the exchange's listing ambitions for nearly a decade after the June 2026 DRHP.

Also on Friday: SEBI signed an MoU with Europe's markets regulator ESMA on cooperation and information exchange over central counterparties (CCPs), replacing their 2017 pact and letting ESMA rely on SEBI's supervision of Indian clearing houses. And a consultation paper on revising the new CAS closing-price methodology — which currently doubles as the derivatives settlement price — is expected within a week.

The consumer lens: the market's chief referee becomes a profit-seeking listed company. Its revenue depends on the very trading activity it polices. Watch how conflict-of-interest safeguards hold once shareholders demand growth.

## WhatsApp Becomes a Biller: 22,722 Bill Payments Land Inside a Chat App

WhatsApp switched on Bill Payments in India on September 3, plugging its ~500 million users into Bharat Connect (the rebranded BBPS). Users tap the rupee icon on the home screen, pick from 22,722 billers across 30 categories — electricity, gas, water, FASTag, insurance, credit card dues, loan EMIs — and pay via UPI, debit or credit card, with fetched bills and one-tap repeats for regular billers. Rollout is gradual over the coming weeks.

The regulatory plumbing is more interesting than the UI: WhatsApp India operates as an Agent Institution onboarded through a Customer Operating Unit under NPCI Bharat BillPay's framework — Meta doesn't touch the biller network directly. Bharat Connect processed 270.72 million transactions worth ₹1.49 lakh crore in July, and since July 2024 RBI has mandated credit card bill payments via third-party apps route through it.

The timing rides a record: UPI notched 24.51 billion transactions worth ₹29.82 lakh crore in August — the first month above 24 billion, up 22% in volume and 20% in value year-on-year, with daily volumes at 791 million. The battleground is distribution: PhonePe and Google Pay command over 80% of UPI, and NPCI's 30% market-share cap has been deferred yet again to December 31, 2026 — never once enforced.

Why it matters for consumers: paying the electricity bill inside a chat app is genuinely convenient. But recurring household bills are the richest behavioural data in payments, and each launch deepens the funnel into a single US-owned platform riding on India's public rails. Convenience is real; so is the concentration.

## Ultrahuman's $70 Million Round Is a Third of the Week's Entire Funding

Health-tech startup Ultrahuman raised $70 million led by Qualcomm Ventures, with an unusual strategic mix: US diagnostics giant Labcorp alongside Alpha Wave Global, Blume Ventures, Nexus Venture Partners and Alteria. The Bengaluru company is pitching an evolution from smart-ring maker to "human-computer interface" — a Qualcomm-powered ring is planned, and a September software update will let existing Ring Air/Pro devices interact with AI apps and even act as game controllers.

The fundamentals are notably less frothy than last week's Slice down round: $140 million annualised revenue run rate (up ~45% year-on-year), targeting $200 million by January 2027, roughly 800,000 rings sold, and 12% of users paying for PowerPlugs subscriptions. The reported valuation sits near $365 million. Founder Mohit Kumar says IPO ambitions wait on eight quarters of profitability — earliest window 2028.

Context: Indian startups raised just $196 million across 24 deals this week (August 29–September 4), down from $245 million the prior week — so Ultrahuman alone accounts for over a third of it. Elsewhere: baby-tech firm Cradlewise raised $12 million from 3one4 Capital and Prudent Investment Management, sneaker brand Comet ₹100 crore (~$10.2 million) from Verlinvest and Elevation Capital, and spacetech startup Kepler Aerospace $8 million.
