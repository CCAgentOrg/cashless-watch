# Fintech Brief — September 06, 2026

## NPCI Hands AI Agents the UPI Purse Strings — "Unified Agent Protocol" Lands at GFF

India is preparing to let artificial intelligence spend your money — within limits. NPCI is expected to unveil the **Unified Agent Protocol** at the Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai this week (September 8–11), allowing AI agents to execute UPI payments without the user manually approving each transaction, according to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-preparing-rollout-agentic-payments-upi-sources-say-2026-09-01) and [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/npci-to-launch-agentic-payments-on-upi-report). NPCI has not commented.

The plumbing leans on two existing UPI features: UPI Circle, which lets a primary account holder delegate payment authority to a secondary user — in this case, an AI agent — and Reserve Pay, which lets customers block funds for multiple debits. Banks currently cap such blocks at ₹10,000 for up to 90 days, limits that may be revisited for agentic use. The first rollout targets low-value, high-frequency spending like groceries, with merchants getting direct integration infrastructure and customers setting rule-based instructions on when and how much agents may pay — with spending limits, audit trails and identity checks built in. Also expected at GFF: UPI AutoPay interoperability, letting users port recurring-payment mandates across apps.

Context: NPCI demoed agentic payments with Razorpay and OpenAI at GFF 2025; Pine Labs has its own P3P protocol, and Visa and Mastercard are building similar frameworks globally. But India would be first at population scale — 24.5 billion monthly transactions.

The consumer lens: India's dispute, chargeback and fraud rules were designed for humans pressing buttons. Spending caps and audit trails are welcome guardrails, but liability when an agent pays the wrong bill, misreads an instruction or gets tricked remains untested. Watch who bears that loss — the customer, the bank or the AI platform.

## PayPal Cuts India Jobs — 600 by Report, 220 by the Company

PayPal has laid off around 600 employees in India — roughly 10% of its 6,000-plus local workforce — across technology, engineering, operations, payments and finance teams in Chennai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad, [Moneycontrol reported](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/paypal-lays-off-around-600-employees-in-india-amid-global-restructuring-14021296.html). PayPal disputes the figure, telling [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/paypals-global-mass-layoffs-impact-220-indian-employees) that about 220 employees were impacted. Either way, the process was abrupt: employees were told on a Monday morning call (August 31) and had system access revoked right after, with severance reported at one month's salary plus placement assistance.

The cuts are part of a restructuring announced in May to shrink PayPal's global workforce by 20% over two to three years, targeting at least $1.5 billion in gross run-rate savings. In Ireland, around 164 employees were cut the same day — about 12% of that headcount.

Why consumers should care: PayPal is a key rail for Indian freelancers and small exporters receiving cross-border payments. As global fintechs chase AI-driven cost discipline, the things that get cut first — support headcount, operations depth — are exactly what Indian users touch when a payment stalls or a dispute needs filing.

## Weekly Funding: $303.7 Million, and Slice's Down Round Sets the Tone

Indian startups raised $303.7 million across 25 deals in the week ended September 5, up from about $218.7 million the prior week, per [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-aug-31-sep-05-12495074) — five growth-stage deals worth $201.9 million and sixteen early-stage rounds worth $101.8 million. The week also logged 250 layoffs and two shutdowns.

The headline: digital banking startup **Slice raised $100 million at a $450 million valuation** — a marked-down round led by Neo Wealth with Kado Global, Moore Strategic Ventures and Raise Financial ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/slice-raises-100-million-from-neo-wealth-kado-global-and-moore-strategic-ventures-at-lower-valuation-of-450-million-14021886.html)). The former unicorn's markdown is the week's clearest signal that fintech capital now prices profitability, not promise.

Also notable: EV financing and battery-as-a-service player **Yuma Energy raised $35 million** in Series A funding led by auto-parts giant Magna International; **NextGen Finance raised ₹215 crore** led by Beams Fintech Fund. Beyond startups, PUBG maker **Krafton committed another $250 million to India** over the next three to four years, separate from its $670 million growth fund ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/pubg-maker-krafton-to-invest-another-250-million-in-india-over-next-three-to-four-years-109830.htm)).

## UPI Goes Abroad: Flood Relief for Nepal, UZQR Deal for Uzbekistan

Two cross-border stories show UPI maturing beyond tourist QR codes. First, Nepal: after devastating flash floods near the Nepal–Tibet border killed hundreds and left hundreds missing, UPI International is now channelling donations from India into Nepal's Prime Minister Disaster Relief Fund — the Nepal embassy is actively urging Indian donors to activate UPI International. As [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/built-for-tourists-upi-s-nepal-link-now-carries-flood-relief-donations-126090101331_1.html) notes, it is the first time the rail is being used for inbound collections for a foreign government, not tourism.

Second, Uzbekistan: NIPL signed a commercial agreement on August 30 with the country's HUMO payment system, signed during PM Modi's bilateral visit and cleared by both the RBI and the Central Bank of Uzbekistan, letting Indian travellers scan the national UZQR code with their UPI apps. No rollout date yet; Uzbekistan would become UPI's 11th country ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-upi-payments-uzbekistan-indian-travellers), [Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/industry/banking/finance/banking/nipl-and-uzbekistans-humo-sign-agreement-for-indian-travelers-seamless-transactions/articleshow/133652889.cms)).

The consumer lens: the Nepal case is the more meaningful precedent. Public payment rails that can move money across borders instantly, at near-zero cost, matter most in a disaster — not at a duty-free counter.
