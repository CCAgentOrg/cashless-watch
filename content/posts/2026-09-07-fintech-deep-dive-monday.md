# Fintech Deep Dive — Monday | September 07, 2026

This is the last full week before Global Fintech Fest 2026 opens in Mumbai (September 8–11), and the pre-GFF announcements tell you exactly what the fest will be about: re-architecting India's payment stack for actors that aren't human. The rail itself is getting an agent protocol (NPCI's Unified Agent Protocol). The storefront in front of the rail is getting an AI discovery layer (Juspay Breeze Universal). The merchant back office is becoming a conversation (Razorpay RAY). And the largest UPI processor in the country is engineering around the one device class the app economy skipped — the feature phone. A Zeta survey released this week supplies the backdrop: 70% of Indian banks now run AI in production, with security and control — not ROI — as the scaling constraint. Here are the five stories that matter for developers and architects.

## 1. NPCI's Unified Agent Protocol: The Rail Gets an Agent API

The biggest story of the week broke on September 1, when [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-preparing-rollout-agentic-payments-upi-sources-say-2026-09-01) that NPCI is preparing a **Unified Agent Protocol (UAP)** — a framework that would let AI agents execute small UPI payments without per-transaction human approval. The protocol is expected to be unveiled at Global Fintech Fest 2026, which runs September 8–11 at the Jio World Centre in Mumbai. NPCI has not formally confirmed the timeline or specifications, but the reporting is detailed and consistent across outlets.

The architecture matters more than the headline. UAP is not a new rail; it is a delegation and authorisation layer composed from two mechanisms UPI already ships. **UPI Circle** lets a primary account holder delegate payment authority to a secondary user or profile — and NPCI has already extended Circle to software profiles, including AI profiles, under a controlled framework. **Reserve Pay** lets customers block funds for multiple debits; banks currently cap such blocks at ₹10,000 for up to 90 days, and sources indicate those limits and validity windows are being reconsidered for agentic use. In effect, UAP composes existing primitives into something new: a standing, bounded mandate under which software can initiate payments.

The control surface is where developers should focus. Under the proposed model, customers define rule-based instructions — when an agent may pay, how much, and at which merchants or merchant categories. The framework reportedly includes spending ceilings, agent and customer identity checks, transaction-level audit trails, and direct merchant integration infrastructure so platforms don't have to route agent payments through consumer apps. NPCI is also preparing a liability framework, though details of how responsibility is allocated among user, agent, bank, and merchant have not been disclosed. First use cases are expected to be low-value and frequent — groceries, utilities, e-commerce — with more complex flows (discount-triggered purchases, threshold-based investments) coming later.

Scale context is what makes this more than a press-release story: UPI processed **24.51 billion transactions worth ₹29.82 trillion in August 2026 alone**, with Google Pay and PhonePe together carrying roughly three-fourths of volume. NPCI piloted agentic payments at GFF 2025, and its FiMI payments-domain AI model (announced February 2026) already powers the UPI Help Assistant. On the build side, Razorpay, Pine Labs, and Cashfree are reportedly already tooling up for agentic payments.

**Why it matters for developers:** This converts UPI from a human-initiated rail into a machine-facing one — the first real-time payments system at this scale to attempt it. Expect a new integration surface distinct from today's PSP APIs: agent registration and identity, mandate and rule management endpoints, idempotency requirements that assume no human is watching for duplicates, and audit-trail APIs built for liability disputes rather than reconciliation. The open question that should worry every integrator is liability allocation: until NPCI publishes that matrix, anyone building agent-initiated flows is engineering against an unpriced risk. [Analysts have already flagged](https://government.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/digital-india/who-pays-when-ai-gets-it-wrong-the-governance-challenge-of-agentic-payments-on-upi/133723153) that delegated judgement — not just delegated payment — is the hard governance problem.

## 2. Juspay Breeze Universal: Making Brands Discoverable Inside AI Conversations

On September 2, [Juspay launched **Breeze Universal**](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/juspay-launches-breeze-universal-to-help-brands-become-discoverable-across-chatgpt-claude-and-other-ai-platforms-article-14020927.html), a global pilot that makes brand catalogues discoverable across AI platforms including ChatGPT and Claude, plus merchants' and banks' own first-party surfaces. The pitch: shoppers discover, compare, and purchase products within a single AI conversation, instead of bouncing between a chatbot's recommendation and a separate checkout.

Juspay COO Sheetal Lalwani framed the thesis bluntly: discovery and payments can no longer be treated as two separate entities — for consumers they are becoming one continuous experience. Technically, that makes Breeze Universal a catalogue-syndication and agentic-checkout layer. Brands push structured, agent-readable product data into the platform; Juspay handles the handoff from an AI conversation into an actual payment, presumably onto Indian rails through its existing Breeze checkout stack, with the "universal" framing signalling ambition beyond India.

**Why it matters for developers:** This is the storefront layer that sits logically above the Unified Agent Protocol. UAP answers "how does software pay?" — Breeze Universal answers "what can software see, and where does it check out?" For backend teams, agent-readable catalogues are a new integration surface with real design decisions: product schemas optimised for LLM consumption, price and inventory freshness guarantees, and checkout handoffs that preserve merchant identity and consumer consent. The pattern to watch is protocol standardisation — if every AI platform demands a different catalogue format, Juspay is betting that a neutral syndication layer becomes the integration point, the way payment orchestration did a decade ago. There's also a consumer-protection angle hiding in the UX: when discovery and payment merge into one conversation, the deliberate friction of a checkout page — cart review, price display, payment-method choice — disappears. Whoever specs this layer is specing consumer consent, whether they admit it or not.

## 3. Razorpay RAY: The Merchant Dashboard Becomes a WhatsApp Thread

On September 3, [Razorpay launched **RAY**](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/startup-news-and-updates-daily-roundup-september-2-2026), a conversational AI account manager on WhatsApp built in partnership with IndusInd Bank, with Bajaj Finance among the first businesses live on the platform. Founders and finance teams can send a text or voice note to get payment summaries, analyse settlement and transaction data, and access support — through conversation rather than dashboards. Razorpay says businesses have already logged more than 20,000 conversations with RAY in the pre-launch period.

The architecture is the interesting part. RAY is built on Razorpay's intelligence layer spanning payments, risk, and business operations — the same data plane that powers its routing and fraud systems. A bank partner (IndusInd) sits inside the loop, which is the compliance architecture talking: for regulated account-level services, the bank must be a principal, not an afterthought. And WhatsApp is the interface, which means RAY rides the messaging platform where Indian merchants already live rather than asking them to install another app.

**Why it matters for developers:** Two shifts are visible here. First, the dashboard is becoming a legacy interface for a class of business users — conversational output, generated on demand, replaces the fixed-report model, and that changes how teams design analytics APIs: they need to serve open-ended natural-language queries with governed data access, not just render canned charts. Second, this is the merchant-side mirror of agentic payments. If NPCI's UAP lets software pay, RAY is an early example of software operating the account — answering questions, initiating actions, escalating to humans. The companies building both sides (Razorpay is named in UAP tooling reports and ships RAY) will define what a coherent agent-native merchant stack looks like. Watch the permissioning model: what RAY may *answer* versus what it may *do* is exactly the boundary UAP's mandate system will have to formalise.

## 4. PhonePe's UPI 123Pay Push: Engineering for the Device the App Economy Skipped

On August 31, [PhonePe launched its own **UPI 123Pay** service for feature phones](https://www.newindianexpress.com/business/2026/Aug/31/digital-payments-without-internet-phonepe-launches-upi-service-for-feature-phones), targeting the more than 200 million Indians still using them. The service supports P2P transfers and P2M payments, balance checks and transaction history — with no internet connection. It runs on an SMS-based architecture on a proprietary stack designed for limited 2G connectivity, and ships pre-bundled on Nokia, HMD, Lava, and itel handsets. Camera-equipped feature phones can scan merchant QR codes. Bill payments, recharges, and Aadhaar-based onboarding are on the roadmap.

Worth stating plainly: UPI 123Pay itself is not new — RBI and NPCI launched it in 2022 with four routes (app, IVR, missed-call, sound-based). What's new is distribution. PhonePe is the first major PSP to industrialise the channel — pre-installing it at the handset layer rather than waiting for users to find it — and it has the scale to matter: over 71.5 crore registered users, more than 5 crore merchants, and over 10 billion UPI transactions in July 2026. CEO Sameer Nigam framed it as onboarding the "next 300 million users."

**Why it matters for developers:** This is constraint-engineering as a discipline: protocol design for unreliable, high-latency, session-less transports, with store-and-forward semantics and careful authentication over a channel — SMS — that was never designed to carry financial messages. Every Indian payment team that files SMS under "legacy" should note that the country's largest UPI processor just bet a product line on it; the same offline-tolerance patterns apply to IoT payments, which NPCI is separately enabling through UPI Circle device profiles. The consumer-advocacy caveat: SMS is also the fraud channel Indians know best. Smishing and SIM-swap risks don't disappear because the UX is simpler, and PhonePe's security disclosures around session handling, PIN entry, and transaction confirmation on this stack deserve close scrutiny as volume grows.

## 5. Axis Bank Puts Guardrails in the App: UPI Limit Management and Travel Declaration

On September 1, [Axis Bank launched **UPI Limit Management** and an **International Travel Declaration** capability](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/axis-bank-s-safety-tool-set-upi-limit-declare-overseas-travel-before-trip-126090100667_1.html) as part of its Safe Banking proposition — billed as an industry first. Customers can now set their own UPI transaction and daily limits directly in the app, and pre-declare overseas travel so foreign transactions aren't fraud-flagged or blocked mid-trip.

It's the least flashy item this week and possibly the most instructive. Granular, user-set transaction policy — caps, categories, context flags — is exactly the control plane that agentic payments will require. NPCI's Unified Agent Protocol needs a limits-and-consents UX that lets a user say "my agent can spend ₹500 a day at grocery merchants and nothing else." Axis is shipping the human-facing version of that interface first, and the rest of the banking system should treat it as a rehearsal.

**Why it matters for developers:** Default-down is the right product instinct — the user starts from maximum restriction and loosens deliberately, which is the opposite of how most payment limits work today (start permissive, tighten after fraud). If banks expose these controls programmatically, third-party apps and future agent frameworks get a standards-backed way to enforce user policy instead of inventing their own. Watch whether NPCI generalises this into limit APIs beyond UPI Circle, and whether other banks ship equivalents before GFF closes on September 11.

## The Pattern: A Stack Re-Layered for Non-Human Actors

Set the five stories side by side and the week's architecture is visible. At the rail, UAP defines how software pays. In front of the rail, Breeze Universal defines what software can see. In the merchant's back office, RAY shows how software operates an account. Meanwhile, 123Pay and Axis's controls serve the humans — the 200 million without smartphones, and everyone who needs a bound on what their software does on their behalf.

The Zeta survey [released this week](https://ffnews.com/news/7-in-10-indian-banks-run-ai-in-production-security-and-control-not-roi-is-now-th-9e1063fc) completes the picture: 70% of Indian banks now run AI in production, but security and control — not ROI — are the stated scaling constraints. That is precisely the tension UAP's liability framework, IndusInd's presence inside RAY, and Axis's app-level guardrails are all trying to resolve. India isn't just adopting agentic fintech; it's building the governance scaffolding around it in public, at population scale, a week before the world's largest fintech gathering opens in Mumbai.

For developers, the practical takeaway: the pre-GFF window is when integration roadmaps get set. Whatever gets demoed September 8–11 will define a meaningful slice of FY27 integration work. Read the UAP documentation the day it drops — and read the liability annex twice.
