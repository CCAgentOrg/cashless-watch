# Fintech Brief — September 08, 2026

## Global Fintech Fest Opens Today — Agentic UPI Is the Main Event

Global Fintech Fest 2026 kicks off in Mumbai this morning (September 8–11, Jio World Centre) under the theme "Potential to Impact: Trusted, Connected, Global Systems for Inclusive Finance", with agentic AI, tokenisation and quantum on the marquee ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/industry/banking/finance/banking/global-fintech-fest-how-adyen-powers-payments-for-global-ambition-and-expansion/articleshow/133864168.cms)). The announcement everyone is waiting for is NPCI's **Unified Agent Protocol** — the framework reported by [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-preparing-rollout-agentic-payments-upi-sources-say-2026-09-01) that would let AI agents execute small UPI payments without the user approving each transaction. NPCI has not formally confirmed the rollout, but the reporting is consistent: UAP composes two existing primitives — UPI Circle for delegated payment authority (already extended to software profiles) and Reserve Pay for blocked funds — into a standing, rule-based mandate with spending ceilings, identity checks and audit trails. Banks currently cap Reserve Pay blocks at ₹10,000 for up to 90 days; those limits are reported to be under revision for agentic use.

Also expected at the fest: interoperability for **UPI AutoPay**, letting users port existing mandates across UPI apps and letting merchants move mandates between payment providers ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/npci-to-launch-agentic-payments-on-upi-report)). That's a genuinely consumer-positive change — today, switching your UPI app means re-setting every recurring mandate from scratch, which is exactly the kind of lock-in that keeps users captive. The context is a rail at record scale: UPI processed 24.51 billion transactions worth ₹29.82 lakh crore in August ([Press Insider](https://pressinsider.com/technology/india-readies-upi-for-payments-made-by-ai-agents-report)). The open question for the consumer: when an agent mispays, who bears the loss? NPCI is reportedly preparing a liability framework, but the allocation among user, agent, bank and merchant is undisclosed — watch for that detail before celebrating.

## SEBI Extends Angel Funds' Accredited-Investor Deadline to March 2027

SEBI has extended the deadline for angel funds registered on or before September 10, 2025 to comply with the accredited-investor mandate — now March 31, 2027, per its latest circular, granted after industry representations ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/stocks/news/sebi-extends-deadline-for-angel-funds-to-comply-with-accredited-investor-mandate/articleshow/133885413.cms)). Funds registered after that date must onboard only accredited investors; all other Chapter 8 provisions of the June 3, 2026 AIF Master Circular are unchanged. The extension is a two-year softening of a rule meant to restrict angel investing to investors who can demonstrably absorb the risk. For consumers, this cuts both ways: accreditation protects unsophisticated retail from high-risk startup bets, but every extension also delays the day the angel market becomes a cleaner, disclosure-heavier place. Meanwhile, SEBI's revised **ETF pricing rules** — new base-price, band and close-out norms for equity, debt and commodity ETFs — came into force yesterday (September 7) after a week's deferral, with the T-1 NAV transition due by April 1, 2027 ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/sebis-new-etf-pricing-rules-from-7-september-whats-changing-and-why-investors-should-care-experts-explain-11788457928760.html)); the fallback pricing for illiquid ETFs should stop retail investors from staring at nonsensical band limits on thinly traded funds.

## PB Fintech's PB Pay Starts Onboarding Merchants as an RBI-Licensed Aggregator

PB Fintech — the Policybazaar and Paisabazaar parent — has begun merchant onboarding on PB Pay, its payment aggregator platform, after receiving RBI's Certificate of Authorisation in February 2026 ([Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/bankingfinance/PB-Fintech-Subsidiary-PB-Pay-Enters-Payment-Aggregator-Market/6a9e586040007e01b83c8147)). The board approved a ₹20 crore capital injection into the subsidiary in July, and the company is explicit that this is a diversification play beyond insurance distribution and lending marketplace revenues ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/pb-fintech-to-enter-payment-aggregator-business-post-rbi-approval-4026-PE1_COR)). The aggregator market is already crowded — Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU and a queue of RBI-licensed entrants — so PB Pay's edge is presumably bundling payments with its insurance and lending funnel. The consumer note: PB Fintech's FY26 sustainability report disclosed a ₹5 crore IRDAI penalty paid by a subsidiary, a reminder that mis-selling scrutiny is the reputational baggage this brand carries into payment flows.

## Funding: BFSI Voice AI and Quick Commerce Get Fresh Capital

Voice AI startup **Navana.ai raised ₹40 crore** in a Series A led by Ronnie Screwvala, with Antler India, Sharad Sanghi, Sandeep Singhal and Paula Mariwala participating; the capital goes toward BFSI deployments and speech models spanning 12 Indian languages and 45 dialects, on top of the 100 million+ voice AI minutes it already handles ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/voice-ai-startup-navana-ai-raises-rs-40-crore-from-ronnie-screwvala-others/articleshow/133874691.cms)). Banks automating customer calls in vernacular languages is a genuine access win — provided consent and disclosure keep pace. Quick food delivery platform **Swish is raising ₹224.54 crore (~$24 million)** in an extended Series B led by Bertelsmann India, its second raise this year after a $38 million round in March ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/swish-to-raise-rs-225-crore-led-by-bertelsmann-india)). The weekend's headline round — Pixxel's $100 million Series C — was covered in yesterday's brief.
