# Fintech Brief — September 09, 2026

## GFF 2026 Day 1: Modi Sets the Agenda — UPI Goes Global, and a Consumer Protection Index Is on the Table

Opening the seventh Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai on Tuesday, PM Narendra Modi announced that UPI is now live in 11 countries, and framed the next chapter as connecting India's payment rails directly with other countries' domestic systems — prioritising corridors with large Indian diasporas, high trade volumes, or willing partners ([PM India](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/pm-inaugurates-global-fintech-fest-2026-in-mumbai), [News18](https://www.news18.com/india/upi-live-in-11-countries-why-pm-modi-is-pushing-for-global-payment-system-linkages-10317075.html)). The pitch is remittances: Indians abroad are among the world's largest remitters, and routing transfers over UPI rails can save on transaction costs while money reaches families faster ([New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Sep/08/pm-modi-urges-fintech-sector-to-expand-upi-globally-cut-remittance-costs)). Worth noting what was *not* announced: no new bilateral link, no deadline — the Singapore PayNow connection remains the flagship live example ([The420.in](https://the420.in/modi-upi-global-expansion-cross-border-remittance-costs)).

The line this newsletter cares most about: Modi asked that cyber security be "top notch" in India, that the industry build "its own ethical data protection standards", and — remarkably — that it create **"a fintech consumer protection index to rate every company transparently"** ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/pm-modi-says-upi-now-live-in-11-countries-pushes-for-further-global-reach-101788885696468.html)). An exhortation is not a policy — there is no methodology, owner or timeline — but if such an index actually materialises, it would give consumers their first at-a-glance trust signal across fintechs. Hold the industry to it. Modi also pushed fintech beyond payments into credit, insurance, savings and pensions, and flagged agentic AI, tokenisation and quantum as the next frontiers, against the backdrop of 7.8% Q1 GDP growth. RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra, also at the fest, called fintech "a valuable partner in building the financial system of the future", crediting it for MSME credit delivery via cash-flow-based lending, account aggregators and the Unified Lending Interface ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/fintech-a-valuable-partner-in-building-financial-system-of-future-rbi-guv-126090801433_1.html)).

## RBI Fines the Big Credit Bureaus for Not Paying Consumers Their Compensation

RBI has imposed a combined ₹34.90 lakh penalty on TransUnion CIBIL (₹26.82 lakh), CRIF High Mark (₹6.89 lakh) and Equifax (₹1.19 lakh) — orders dated August 31 — for failing to credit compensation to eligible complainants within the prescribed timeline ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/transunion-cibil-crif-high-mark-equifax-penalised-3490-lakh-for-delayed-compensation/81579.html)). The compensation in question is the ₹100-per-day payout owed when a bureau takes more than 30 days to update or rectify credit information under RBI's framework ([The420.in](https://the420.in/rbi-penalties-transunion-cibil-crif-equifax-sammaan-hinduja-leyland-finance)). Read that again: the companies that hold your credit history were penalised because they did not pay consumers the money those consumers were already owed. The penalty hits the companies, not your rights — if you have a pending or past delayed-correction dispute, the ₹100/day claim still stands and is worth pursuing with your bureau and bank. In the same batch of orders, RBI also fined Hinduja Leyland Finance and Sammaan Finserve a combined ₹10.40 lakh over CRILC reporting, microfinance pricing and securitisation lapses ([The420.in](https://the420.in/rbi-penalties-transunion-cibil-crif-equifax-sammaan-hinduja-leyland-finance)).

## SEBI Drops Investor-Group Disclosure for G-Sec-Only Foreign Investors

SEBI has exempted foreign portfolio investors that invest exclusively in government securities from furnishing investor-group details, via a circular issued September 7 that applies across all investment routes into the G-Sec market ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-eases-compliance-for-fpis-investing-only-in-govt-securities/81584.html)). The change is housekeeping following RBI's June 5 withdrawal of the concentration limit for the general route — the disclosure existed to monitor that limit, and with the limit gone, SEBI says tracking investor groups is "no longer relevant" ([NiftyTrader](https://www.niftytrader.in/markets/fpi-g-sec-rules-sebi-key-disclosure-change)). Low drama by design, and one less friction point for foreign money into government bonds.

## Deals: Bajaj Finance Backs TrueFan AI; Wealth-Tracker Safebox Scales

Bajaj Finance has acquired a 5% stake in AI video-generation platform TrueFan AI — the first investment under the group's new "Finserv Intelligence" innovation vehicle, which will back startups from seed to Series B across AI, cybersecurity, quantum and fintech ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/bajaj-finance-buys-5-of-truefan-ai-in-first-finserv-intelligence-bet)). Family wealth-protection platform **Safebox** — which tracks assets, liabilities, insurance and critical records across 55 categories via SEBI-registered Beyondco Technologies, and says customers have recorded over ₹6,000 crore in assets since June 2026 — is riding the GFF spotlight ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/startup-news-and-updates-daily-roundup-september-8-2026)). Also raising: **Fundly.ai** ($4 million for B2B pharma distribution) and **DigitalPaani** (₹22 crore led by Navam Capital) ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/startup-news-and-updates-daily-roundup-september-8-2026), [ET Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/funding/digitalpaani-raises-22-crore-in-funding-led-by-navam-capital/133917626)). Separately, the Bombay High Court set aside the ~₹100 crore PMLA freeze on Coda Payments India's bank and payment-gateway accounts, holding the Adjudicating Authority failed to record the mandatory Section 8(2) finding that the property is involved in money laundering ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/bombay-high-court/bombay-high-court-sets-aside-100-crore-pmla-freeze-548587)) — a due-process guardrail worth noting as PMLA action against payment intermediaries multiplies.
