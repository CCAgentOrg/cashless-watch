# Fintech Brief — September 11, 2026

## UPI Learns to Tap: NFC Payments and AI-Powered Support Land at the POS

Closing out Global Fintech Fest 2026, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra unveiled two UPI capabilities: 'Tap & Pay' on NFC-enabled POS terminals and MyUPI, an AI-powered customer support layer ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/upi-gets-tap-and-pay-ai-powered-customer-support-as-rbi-unveils-two-new-capabilities-at-gff-2026/134010564)). Tap & Pay works by unlocking your NFC-enabled phone and tapping it on the terminal — and because the transaction rides on the POS machine's connectivity, it functions even when your phone has no data ([Fortune India](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/sebi-launches-corporate-bonds-tokenisation-npci-unveils-new-upi-capabilities/158604)). Reuters frames the timing: this lands ahead of an expected full Apple Pay launch in India ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-launches-tap-and-pay-option-real-time-payments-ahead-full-apple-pay-2026-09-10/)). MyUPI, built on NPCI's small language model FiMI as part of an expanded UPI Help framework, is meant to handle complaints and controls conversationally. The rails are already shifting from typed PINs to device-native auth — biometric-authenticated UPI transactions crossed 6.29 billion as of August 31, 2026 ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/upi-gets-tap-and-pay-ai-powered-customer-support-as-rbi-unveils-two-new-capabilities-at-gff-2026/134010564)). The consumer stake: every convenience layer is also a new fraud surface. A tap is faster than a QR scan — so is a mistake — and per-transaction limits and dispute timelines for tap payments deserve the same visibility as the convenience.

## SEBI Flips the Switch on Demat 2.0: Tokenised Bonds That Settle Instantly

Announced jointly by RBI Governor Malhotra and SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey at GFF, Demat 2.0 is a new market infrastructure that issues, holds, trades and settles corporate bonds as digital tokens on a distributed ledger — a shared record owned by the depositories ([Fortune India](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/sebi-launches-corporate-bonds-tokenisation-npci-unveils-new-upi-capabilities/158604)). The headline feature is atomic settlement: "the bond and the money move instantaneously," as SEBI put it, with settlement happening in central bank digital currency inside the existing regulated market infrastructure — India claims a global first in issuing corporate bonds natively on DLT while keeping ownership records with statutory depositories ([PSU Watch](https://psuwatch.com/newsupdates/npci-adds-upi-features-sebi-launches-instant-bond-settlement-pilot)). REC, L&T and IIFL have already issued ₹1,025 crore of tokenised bonds under the phased pilot ([Fortune India](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/sebi-launches-corporate-bonds-tokenisation-npci-unveils-new-upi-capabilities/158604)), including REC's ₹500 crore first issue under SEBI's regulatory sandbox. Why consumers should care: settlement risk — the gap between paying and receiving — is a hidden cost baked into everyone's market returns, and compressing it to an atomic instant removes an entire class of failure. The CBDC leg also quietly hands the digital rupee its first real capital-market workload.

## NPCI Wants a Registry for AI Agents That Spend Your Money

NPCI is building a registry to verify and monitor AI agents making transactions on its rails, three sources told Reuters — part of the planned Unified Agentic Protocol for agentic payments ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-plans-ai-registry-it-looks-roll-out-agentic-payments-sources-say-2026-09-10/)). The backdrop is uncomfortable: heightened attention on rogue behaviour by agents at leading AI firms such as OpenAI, Meta and Anthropic. Once an AI agent can initiate payments, "know your customer" quietly becomes "know your customer's agent" — and the liability question we flagged earlier this week remains unanswered. A registry is a reasonable first step: identity for agents is a precondition for accountability. But verification is not liability. Consumers should be able to see, revoke and cap what any agent is authorised to spend, in one place, before agentic payments scale to anything resembling mainstream use.

## Deals: Swish Grabs $24M, Theater Raises ₹75 Cr — and a ₹45,000-Crore IPO Traffic Jam Forms

Ten-minute food delivery startup **Swish** raised $24 million led by Bertelsmann India Investments, with monthly orders crossing 1 million and volumes tripling since March 2026 as it expands its kitchen network across Bengaluru, Gurugram, Noida, Delhi and Ghaziabad ([CXO Digital Pulse](https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/swish-raises-24-million-in-funding-led-by-bertelsmann-india-investments)). Fashion brand **Theater** raised ₹75 crore in Series A led by Niveshaay, with Physis Capital and Prath Ventures participating, at a ₹410 crore post-money valuation ([Startup Story](https://startupstorymedia.com/niveshaay-leads-rs-75-cr-series-a-round-in-fashion-brand-theater)). On the IPO conveyor: 2026 fundraising is on course to breach ₹1 lakh crore — 81 IPOs have mobilised about ₹83,062 crore so far — and the **NSE** issue, expected to raise around ₹23,000 crore, is likely to open next week, with Indian Bank selling its 17.91% NSE stake via OFS in it ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/ipos/fpos/indias-ipo-fundraising-poised-to-breach-rs-1-lakh-crore-in-2026/articleshow/134036093.cms)). **RentoMojo's** IPO was subscribed 1.97x on day one, with the non-institutional segment leading at 3.87x ([Startup Story](https://startupstorymedia.com/rentomojo-ipo-subscribed-1-97x-on-day-1-nii-portion-leads-at-3-87x/)). Two mega-issues landing within weeks will absorb a lot of primary-market appetite; retail investors chasing allocation should check how much of the cash actually reaches the company versus exiting shareholders — RentoMojo's OFS-heavy structure being the cautionary example from yesterday.
