# Fintech Brief — September 13, 2026

## RBI Drafts Debit-Freeze Rules for Suspected Money Mule Accounts

The Reserve Bank has released draft [Know Your Customer (KYC) Amendment Directions, 2026](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-proposes-sop-suspected-money-mule-bank-accounts.html) for public comment, acting on a Supreme Court order dated August 4, 2026 that directed it to adopt and circulate a Standard Operating Procedure for bank action on temporary debit holds linked to money-mule activity and cyber-enabled financial fraud. The draft inserts a new Annex III into the KYC Directions, 2025: on flagging a suspected money mule account or transaction, a bank must place a temporary debit hold immediately and notify the account holder, who generally gets 20 days to explain or justify the activity. The bank must then either remove the hold, continue it and report the account to the jurisdictional police through the NCRP-CFCFRMS channel, or comply with a direction from a law-enforcement agency or competent authority. Absent contrary instructions, the hold cannot exceed 60 days, and the SOP prescribes record-keeping, continued STR filing, enhanced monitoring and a grievance-redressal mechanism. It covers commercial banks (including small finance, payments, regional rural and local area banks) and urban cooperative banks — but not nodal, pool or escrow accounts. Comments are due by **October 2, 2026**; the rules take effect April 1, 2027, or earlier if a bank chooses. The consumer stake: account freezes in fraud chains have so far been a black box — victims' and intermediaries' accounts locked up for months with no uniform timelines or appeal route. A 60-day cap and a grievance mechanism are floor rules worth having, but watch the escape hatch ("absent contrary instructions") and the fact that the initial burden sits on the account holder to respond within 20 days.

## SEBI Rethinks Expiry-Day Settlement After Closing-Auction Jitters

In a Saturday consultation paper, SEBI proposed [overhauling the closing auction session (CAS)](https://www.financialexpress.com/market/cas-concerns-sebi-proposes-2-options-for-fo-settlement-trade-timing-after-expiry-day-volatility-4337893) it had switched on August 3 for derivative-eligible stocks, after expiry-day volatility tested the new mechanism. On settlement prices it offers two alternatives: a "Blended VWAP" combining trades from the last 30 minutes of continuous trading and the 10-minute auction, weighted by actual traded value (across exchanges for single-stock derivatives), or a temporary return to the pre-CAS method of using only the last 30 minutes of continuous trading. The paper also floats changes to market timings and order rules, with public comments due by **October 3, 2026** ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/sebi-proposes-cas-changes-across-settlement-market-timing-order-rules-19989774.htm)). The consumer stake: expiry-day settlement prices determine derivatives payouts for the millions of retail F&O accounts SEBI has spent years trying to protect from themselves — how the closing print is calculated is not an arcane plumbing detail, and averaging over a wider window cuts both ways.

## Primary Market: Rentomojo Closes 72x, Arcil 20x — and NSE Looms

RentoMojo's IPO closed with a [72.28x overall subscription](https://www.indiaipo.in/daily-reporter/india-ipo-daily-market-ipo-updates-12th-september-2026), a sharp acceleration from the 1.97x day-one figure we flagged on Friday. Arcil's ₹733-crore issue was subscribed 20 times, driven by QIBs bidding 52.65x their quota, and Mann Fleet Partners received SEBI approval for its IPO. All eyes stay on the **NSE** issue — expected to raise around ₹23,000 crore with Indian Bank selling its 17.91% stake via offer-for-sale — likely to open this coming week ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/ipos/fpos/indias-ipo-fundraising-poised-to-breach-rs-1-lakh-crore-in-2026/articleshow/134036093.cms)), in a year when 81 IPOs have already mobilised about ₹83,000 crore. Retail investors piling into oversubscribed issues should remember the arithmetic of allocation: at 72x, most retail applicants get a token lot or nothing, and the OFS-heavy structures seen this cycle mean much of the money raised never reaches the company.

## Funding: Pixxel's $100M Caps a Heavy Week

Weekend reading from the deal desk ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-sep-07-sep-12-12523070)): early-stage startups raised $174.8 million across 15 deals this week. Space-tech startup **Pixxel** led with a $100 million Series C co-led by Temasek and Seraphim Space. Cybersecurity firm **QNu Labs** raised ₹200 crore in a Series A1 co-led by the National Quantum Mission and Speciale Invest. Travel-lifestyle brand **Mokobara** raised ₹170 crore (~$18 million) in Series C funding led by Sauce.VC with Peak XV, AYRA Ventures and Niveshaay ([Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/funding/travel-and-lifestyle-brand-mokobara-raises-rs-170-crore-in-funding-from-saucevc-others-12524738)). Fintech-adjacent: cardless fleet-fuel payments platform **Piston** — US-headquartered with its engineering centre in Kolkata — raised $15 million in Series A led by FPV Ventures with Spark Capital and Pear VC, taking total funding to $22.5 million ([DealStreetAsia](https://www.dealstreetasia.com/stories/piston-graph-ai-494857)), and fleet-management platform **Carrum Mobility** raised $10 million in Series B led by Uber ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/funding/weekly-funding-roundup-the-startup-deals-you-may-have-missed-in-week-of-september-13-14028355.html)). Note the pattern in QNu's round: a national mission co-leading a private round is deeper state participation in frontier-tech financing — worth watching for where DPI money meets deep-tech cap tables.
