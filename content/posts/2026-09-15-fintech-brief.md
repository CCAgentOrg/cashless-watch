# Fintech Brief — September 15, 2026

## Government Draws the Line: UPI Up to ₹2,000 Is Now Statutorily Free

The Finance Ministry's Department of Financial Services on Monday [notified that UPI transactions up to ₹2,000 and RuPay debit card payments cannot attract any charges](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/upi-stays-free-finance-ministry-bars-charges-on-transactions-up-to-rs-2000-555475-2026-09-14), direct or indirect, under Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/upi-stays-free-finance-ministry-bars-charges-on-transactions-up-to-rs-2000-555475-2026-09-14)). The notification converts an assurance into law: banks and payment providers are barred from imposing charges on the named modes, and the government had promised exactly this listing when the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill — passed on August 6 — removed the blanket zero-MDR restriction and left it to the Centre to notify which payment modes stay protected ([Convergence](https://www.convergence-now.com/fintech/lok-sabha-passes-bill-enabling-upi-charges-paving-way-for-future-mdr)). Read together, the protected list ends where ₹2,000 begins: UPI above that threshold to large merchants remains the candidate for a merchant discount rate, which [the Financial Express reports won't exceed 0.5%](https://www.financialexpress.com/money/upi-above-rs-2000-may-attract-0-5-mdr-for-large-traders-4293962/lite), with businesses up to ₹1.5 crore turnover exempt — small value indeed, since only about 4% of person-to-merchant UPI transactions cross ₹2,000. The actual MDR structure now goes to an NPCI-headed committee to finalise ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/mdr-on-upi-transactions-worth-rs-2-000-and-above-likely-in-two-weeks-report-14011983.html)). The consumer stake: person-to-person payments stay free regardless, and Finance Minister Sitharaman has said any merchant charge cannot be passed on to customers — the committee's design has to make that enforceable, not just promised.

## RBI Drafts Money-Mule Rules: Targeted Holds, Not Blanket Freezes

The RBI has placed in the public domain the draft [Reserve Bank of India (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/bs_viewcontent.aspx?Id=5170), prescribing a uniform SOP for banks to act on suspected money-mule accounts and cyber-enabled financial fraud ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/Bs_Connect2RegulateDetails.aspx?prid=505)). The draft — [issued September 11, with comments open until October 2](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/rbi-issues-draft-sop-for-temporary-debit-holds-on-money-mule-cyber-fraud-accounts/article71457627.ece) and in force from April 1, 2027 — follows the Supreme Court's August 4 order directing the central bank to standardise how banks handle flagged amounts. The mechanics as reported: transactions of ₹1,000 and above flagged by AI-based monitoring can attract a temporary debit hold for up to 60 days, but [only the disputed amount can be frozen, not the entire account](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/rbi-only-disputed-amt-can-be-frozen-under-new-norms/articleshow/134229725.cms); customers get 20 calendar days to establish legitimacy, banks get 10 days to assess, and the process must link to the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal's fraud-management system ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/wealth/save/rbi-proposes-60-day-temporary-debit-hold-on-suspicious-money-mule-transactions-key-things-you-need-to-know-about-rbis-kyc-directions/articleshow/134241278.cms)). The consumer stake is real: harassment via blanket account freezes after a single cybercrime complaint has been a chronic grievance, and a codified, time-bound, amount-specific SOP is a genuine fix — provided banks actually follow the clock. Read the draft before October 2 and comment; this is the rare consultation where individual account holders are the direct beneficiaries.

## NPCI's Surplus Falls 32% — The Economics of a Free Rail Get Tighter

NPCI's FY26 accounts, reported Monday, show the cost of running the world's largest real-time payment system on goodwill: [net surplus fell 32% to ₹989.4 crore even as revenue rose 22% to ₹4,240 crore](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-npci-profit-989-cr-fy26-bhim-loss) ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-npci-profit-989-cr-fy26-bhim-loss)). Marketing was the biggest cost centre at 47% of expenses (₹1,420 crore, up 27%), while data-centre rental jumped 20x and power costs climbed 51% — server demand from AI infrastructure is not free either. The subsidiary scorecard is starker: NPCI BHIM Services, the for-profit entity created to grow the BHIM app, widened its loss 5.7x to ₹390.6 crore on a turnover of just ₹27.8 crore; NIPL, the international arm, lost ₹19.4 crore on ₹83 crore of turnover. This is the quiet backdrop to Monday's MDR notification: the incentives and cashbacks that keep zero-cost UPI running are paid out of a shrinking surplus, and the debate over who funds the rail — government subsidy, merchant MDR, or scale — is now unavoidable. Anyone reading the MDR committee's work should read NPCI's books alongside it.

## Philippines Signs Up for UPI Technology

The Philippines will adopt India's UPI stack, following a September 12 meeting in New Delhi between President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. and NIPL MD & CEO Ritesh Shukla. Trade Secretary Cristina Roque said the deal will let state-run LandBank "[distribute government financial aid much faster](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/14/phl-taps-india-to-upgrade-digital-payments)" and make everyday digital transactions cheaper for Filipinos ([Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/14/phl-taps-india-to-upgrade-digital-payments), [Newsbytes](https://newsbytes.ph/2026/09/15/ph-eyes-indias-upi-technology-for-faster-digital-payments)). UPI's international count keeps growing — 11 countries by the industry's own count at Global Fintech Fest — but NIPL's FY26 loss of ₹19.4 crore is a reminder that exporting payment infrastructure is a volume game that hasn't yet paid. Watch whether Philippines deployment converts into live corridors and remittance savings, or joins the long list of MoU-stage expansions.
