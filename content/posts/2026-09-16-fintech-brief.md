# Fintech Brief — September 16, 2026

## NPCI Notifies the MDR: 0.4% on UPI Above ₹2,000, From October 15

The wait ended in numbers: NPCI has [formally notified a merchant discount rate of 0.4% on person-to-merchant UPI transactions above ₹2,000, effective October 15, 2026](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/mdr-on-upi-notified-npci-says-0-4-fee-applicable-on-person-to-merchant-transactions-above-rs-2000-effective-october-15-2026/articleshow/134265294.cms), with person-to-person transfers untouched and a per-transaction cap of ₹300 for payments of ₹75,000 and above ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/economy/policy/upi-to-introduce-fees-on-transactions-above-rs-2000-from-october-15-mdr-merchant-payment/articleshow/134265271.cms)). This lands two days after the Finance Ministry statutorily protected transactions up to ₹2,000 and RuPay debit card payments from any charge, completing the two-piece structure the August amendment to the Payment and Settlement Systems Act anticipated: free below the line, chargeable above it. The scale context is what makes this manageable — UPI moved 2,451 crore transactions worth ₹29.9 lakh crore in August alone, and [NPCI's FAQ estimates about 96% of P2M transactions will remain unaffected](https://m.economictimes.com/news/economy/policy/upi-charges-from-october-15-faqs-on-who-will-pay-0-4-mdr-and-what-consumers-small-vendors-large-merchants-need-to-know/articleshow/134266293.cms) because they sit below ₹2,000. The open question is the one that matters to consumers: who actually bears the fee at checkout. Finance Minister Sitharaman has said merchant charges cannot be passed on to customers, but the operational design — [a 22-member UPI & Services Steering Committee including the Indian Banks' Association and Payments Council of India](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/banks-panel-to-decide-on-upi-merchant-charges-on-transactions-above-2-000-126091500672_1.html) — now has to translate that assurance into enforceable rules, and [NPCI is also proposing a dedicated fund](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-payments-up-to-rs-2000-remain-free-what-it-means-for-users-126091500150_1.html) to subsidise acceptance infrastructure in Tier 3-6 centres, the Northeast, Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh, with the framework to be finalised with the RBI within three months. Watch the pass-through: a ₹25,000 purchase would carry up to ₹100 of MDR, and whether that surfaces as a surcharge line or quietly inside the price is the difference between a merchant fee and a consumer tax.

## Moneyview Halves Its IPO Fresh Issue to ₹750 Crore

Fintech unicorn Moneyview has [cut the fresh issue component of its IPO in half to ₹750 crore from the ₹1,500 crore in its draft papers, and trimmed the offer for sale to 10.04 crore shares from 13.61 crore](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/moneyview-halves-ipo-fresh-issue-to-rs-750-cr-after-sebi-nod-623713), per a notice to investors dated September 14 ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/ipo/digital-lending-platform-moneyview-cuts-ipo-size-fresh-issue-halved-to-rs-750-crore-14029305.html)). The mechanics were enabled by an April 2026 SEBI rule letting issuers shrink a fresh issue by up to 50% without refiling the DRHP — Moneyview applied on September 3, [SEBI signed off on September 10](https://entrackr.com/news/moneyview-cuts-ipo-fresh-issue-to-rs-750-cr-investors-trim-ofs-12533665), and the revised allocation sends ₹325 crore toward loan disbursals under default-loss-guarantee arrangements and ₹250 crore into NBFC subsidiary Whizdm Finance. A halved primary raise from a digital lender two months after approval is a signal worth reading: either the growth plan needs less capital than the March 2026 draft assumed, or the window for richly-valued lending IPOs is tighter than founders hoped. The company has not disclosed a reason or a listing date; investors will get their answer in the price band.

## Fibe Clears SEBI for Its ₹750 Crore IPO

The lending IPO pipeline is not thinning — it is queuing. [Fibe, the Pune-based digital lender formerly known as EarlySalary, has received SEBI approval for an IPO comprising a ₹750 crore fresh issue and an offer for sale of 40.07 million shares](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/startups/fibe-gets-sebi-approval-for-rs-750-crore-ipo/articleshow/134268824.cms) by backers including TPG's The Rise Fund, Norwest Venture Partners and Eight Roads Ventures ([Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/ipo/Social-Worth-Technologies-Fibe-Receives-SEBI-Nod-for-indian-rupee750-Cr-IPO/6aa9a8920416c068539d9d9d)). Founded in 2015 by Akshay Mehrotra and Ashish Goyal, Fibe graduated from small-ticket salary advances to longer-tenure unsecured personal loans and purpose-linked financing across healthcare, education and insurance. The numbers on the table: FY26 operating revenue of ₹1,585 crore, net profit of ₹257 crore, and assets under management of ₹8,603 crore, with ₹562.6 crore of the fresh proceeds earmarked to capitalise its NBFC arm, EarlySalary Services. With Moneyview and Fibe both moving in the same week, public-market investors are about to price digital lending as a sector, not a story — credit-quality disclosures in the coming prospectuses will set the terms for everyone behind them in the queue.

## Flam Raises $40 Million Series B — With Shah Rukh Khan on the Cap Table

AI content startup Flam has [raised $40 million in a Series B led by QED Investors, with Shah Rukh Khan, Claypond Capital, Martin Chavez, Olivier Pomel and Venky Harinarayan joining](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/flam-raises-40-mn-shah-rukh-khan-joins-funding-round/articleshow/134268019.cms) alongside existing investors RTP Global and Dovetail ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/startup-news-and-updates-daily-roundup-september-15-2026)). The San Francisco-and-Bengaluru company, founded in 2021 by Shourya Agarwal, Malhar Patil and Amit Gaiki, builds interactive video, 3D product experiences and conversational visual agents for enterprise customers, and [will put the money into R&D, product expansion and enterprise sales](https://technode.global/2026/09/15/flam-raises-40-million-series-b-interactive-ai-content); it previously raised a $14 million Series A led by RTP Global in May 2025. No valuation was disclosed. For consumers, the interesting bit is where this tech points: ad formats that respond and transact inside the frame — engaging, certainly, and one more reason the rules on dark patterns and disclosure in advertising need to keep pace with the formats.
