# Fintech Deep Dive — Thursday | September 17, 2026

## The week global ambitions met settlement realities

International and cross-border fintech owned the week. Global Fintech Fest 2026 (September 8–11, Mumbai) set the tone with the Prime Minister's call to push UPI into more countries and cut remittance costs, and the BRICS New Delhi Summit (September 12–13) tested how much of that ambition survives contact with geopolitics. In between, the RBI handed out cross-border aggregator licences, a US payments bridge quietly took shape, and the gap between UPI's 11-country map and its near-zero cross-border volume became impossible to ignore. Five stories that define the week.

## Story 1: The UPI–Zelle bridge — India's biggest remittance corridor may finally get a retail rail

The most consequential cross-border news of the week may be the quietest. Bank of America is working on a pilot to connect Zelle — the US bank-network P2P rail operated by Early Warning Services — with India's UPI, a development discussed publicly at GFF and reported on September 10 ([India Today](https://www.indiatoday.in/business/story/zelle-upi-bridge-bank-of-america-indians-in-usa-nris-remittances-national-payments-corporation-of-india-2991410-2026-09-10); [Indian Eagle](https://www.indianeagle.com/traveldiary/upi-zelle-bridge-india-us-digital-payments)). The US is India's largest remittance source by far, and most of that money still moves over correspondent-banking and money-transmitter rails priced for a pre-UPI era.

Why this matters structurally: the UPI–PayNow linkage with Singapore was a central-bank-to-central-bank integration between two RTGS operators. A Zelle–UPI bridge would be different — it would put a send-money-to-India button inside the US consumer's *own banking app*, the interface tens of millions of NRIs already use daily. No new app, no agent network, no cash pickup. That is exactly the distribution advantage UPI lacks abroad and exactly where remittance cost is concentrated: not the payment leg, but the last-mile interface and the FX markup layered on top.

The open questions are the ones to watch. First, FX transparency — a bridge is only consumer-friendly if the exchange rate and fees are shown up front, not buried in a "no fee, adjusted rate" model. Second, compliance: Zelle's operator network is owned by the largest US banks, and money-transmission and AML obligations on the US side will shape how real-time this actually is. Third, scope: pilots of this kind typically start with limits and eligible accounts. The lesson from UPI–PayNow is sobering — nearly three years after that link launched, it remains a narrow remittance corridor rather than a mass rail. A Zelle bridge would have far larger distribution, but it will live or die on the same variables: pricing, limits, and the banks' willingness to promote it. Consumers should judge the final landed rupees per dollar, not the press release.

## Story 2: GFF 2026's cross-border product wave — PhonePe goes to the Gulf, Visa QR goes to 14 markets

Global Fintech Fest 2026, inaugurated by the Prime Minister on September 8 at Mumbai's Jio World Convention Centre, made UPI's global expansion a headline goal — alongside extending fintech into pensions and insurance, building a Fintech Consumer Protection Index, and framing agentic AI, tokenisation and quantum as the next frontier ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-agentic-ai-products-fintechs-gff-2026)). UPI entered the week live in 11 countries, having processed 24.51 billion transactions worth roughly $314 billion in August ([Reuters via TechReviewAfrica](https://techreviewafrica.com/news/7138/ethswitch-takes-ethiopias-digital-payments-push-to-global-fintech-stage)).

The most strategically interesting announcement was PhonePe's three-part Visa partnership (September 9): Tap to Pay, Cross Border Scan to Pay, and Smart Accept ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-agentic-ai-products-fintechs-gff-2026)). Cross Border Scan to Pay lets PhonePe users scan Visa QR codes in 14 markets including Singapore, Thailand, Japan and South Korea — reaching over 20 million merchants ([Emkay's GFF note](https://investmentguruindia.com/newsdetail/internet-sector-update-gff-2026---indian-fintech-remains-a-hotbed-of-innovation-by-emkay-global-financial-services-ltd563145)). Read that market list carefully: most of those corridors are ones UPI has *not* opened. The crucial detail is that these features run on Visa's card network, deliberately separate from UPI International's 11 countries ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-agentic-ai-products-fintechs-gff-2026)). India's largest payments app is effectively saying: we will not wait for UPI's diplomatic map to fill in.

PhonePe also announced its first overseas market entry with a licence from the Central Bank of the UAE — wallet, remittances and merchant payments for the Gulf corridor ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/business/finance/gff-phonepe-to-enter-uae-first-overseas-expansion-central-bank-licence-19988101.htm)). On the official-rails side, RBI Executive Director Gunveer Singh unveiled cross-border merchant payments via RuPay Credit Card on UPI International — extending the domestic credit-card-on-UPI experience to international QR acceptance points and e-commerce merchants on the Global UPI network ([BW Disrupt](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/rbi-executive-director-unveils-new-rupay-and-upi-payment-innovations-at-gff-2026-623540)), and RBI Deputy Governor Rohit Jain announced multicurrency capabilities for the forex category on Bharat Connect as a controlled-user-group launch ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-gets-new-features-tap-to-pay-unified-window-for-transaction-details-126091001485_1.html)).

The consumer takeaway: the cross-border stack is layering fast, but fee stacks are opaque by default. Domestic UPI is zero-MDR; international spend through card rails or UPI International typically carries FX markups. As choice expands, the regulator's next job is making the *cost* of each rail comparable at the point of sale. An UPI International that is accepted but expensive is choice on paper only.

## Story 3: RBI's cross-border aggregator licences formalize the export funnel

Around GFF week, PayU, Paytm, Razorpay, Juspay and Skydo received cross-border payment aggregator (PA-CB) licences from the RBI, enabling them to facilitate payments between Indian merchants and overseas customers ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-agentic-ai-products-fintechs-gff-2026)). PayU paired the news with Fraud Liability Protect, an AI-powered fraud tool for Indian merchants accepting international card payments — merchants using it saw 4–5% higher international payment success rates, with one online travel merchant reporting a 14% success-rate rise and an 83% drop in its fraud-to-sales ratio; Zomato is an adopter ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-agentic-ai-products-fintechs-gff-2026)).

This is the unglamorous plumbing story of the week, and arguably the most economically important. India's services exports run through freelancers, SaaS companies, D2C brands and online travel — sellers who get paid by foreign cards and wallets. The old OPGSP regime was a patchwork; the PA-CB framework replaces it with licensed, capital-adequacy-checked intermediaries under RBI supervision. Licences convert a regulatory gray zone into a compliance moat: the big five now hold the formal keys to the corridor, and smaller aggregators must either partner with them or exit.

Two things to watch. Concentration: five licensed players handling the bulk of inward cross-border merchant flows is efficient but creates pricing power over precisely the small exporters who have the least negotiating leverage. And the fraud data cuts both ways: AI-driven risk-tiering that lifts success rates by 5% is genuinely good for merchants — but risk-based authentication is also a black box, and declined transactions disproportionately hit legitimate buyers in thinner-file markets. As this corridor formalizes, RBI's periodic PA-CB reviews should demand decline-rate transparency, not just fraud-rate headline numbers.

## Story 4: BRICS — the CBDC pitch meets the Declaration

Bloomberg reported on September 10 that India, chairing BRICS this year, is pushing members to link central bank digital currencies for cross-border payments — while deliberately stopping short of a unified bloc-wide network that could read as a challenge to the dollar; Modi favours bilateral digital-currency settlement between members, and a single system was judged unlikely to be agreed at the summit ([NDTV/Bloomberg](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/india-to-pitch-rbi-backed-cross-border-digital-currencies-for-brics-payments-report-12026672)). RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra has separately confirmed local-currency trade agreements with the UAE, Mauritius, Maldives and Indonesia, with more under negotiation ([NDTV/Bloomberg](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/india-to-pitch-rbi-backed-cross-border-digital-currencies-for-brics-payments-report-12026672)).

The summit then delivered a masterclass in what "strategic ambiguity" looks like in payments. Finance ministers and central bank governors, meeting in Mumbai on September 10, backed continued work on payment interoperability and local-currency settlement but stressed there is "no one-size-fits-all" approach ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/brics-local-currency-trade-settlement-plan-cbdc-framework-de-dollarisation-push-benefits-for-india); [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/brics-finance-chiefs-flag-tariff-risks-back-cross-border-payments-push-ahead-of-leaders-summit-14027888.html)). At the BRICS Business Forum on September 11, Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal pitched exactly that — linking payment systems, with UPI cited as the model ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/business/Economy/brics-treads-carefully-on-de-dollarisation-finance-track-talks-payments-not-a-common-currency/article71459353.ece)). Then the New Delhi Declaration, adopted September 12, kept the word "CBDC" out of its final text entirely; its payments paragraph (para 90) notes the Payment Task Force's work on interoperability and local-currency settlement — phrasing that, read plainly, means consensus was not reached ([CryptoRank's declaration read-through](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/69955-brics-delhi-declaration-2026-i-read-all-120-paragraphs-so-you-dont-have-to); [Al Jazeera's BRICS Pay explainer](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/16/what-is-brics-pay-and-can-it-rival-the-wests-swift-payments-system)).

India's actual playbook is coherent once you see it: build retail rails (UPI) and stitch them bilaterally, trial wholesale settlement via the digital rupee pilot, keep de-dollarisation rhetoric at arm's length, and let the task forces grind. For ordinary consumers the payoff is indirect and distant — cheaper remittances and trade settlement arrive only when interoperability moves from declaration prose to live corridors. Until then, treat every "BRICS Pay will rival SWIFT" headline as a bid for attention, not a settlement standard.

## Story 5: The 11-country map versus the less-than-0.01% reality

The week's applause lines deserve the week's arithmetic. UPI is live in 11 countries, with Uzbekistan the latest — NIPL signed a commercial agreement with Uzbekistan's national payment operator (HUMO/NIPC) on August 30, enabling Indian travellers to pay by scanning UZQR codes ([ET TravelWorld](https://travel.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/technology/indians-can-now-make-upi-payments-in-uzbekistan/133670317)). But cross-border UPI transactions reached only about 14.86 lakh in FY2026 — up 97% from 7.55 lakh in FY2025, and still less than 0.01% of UPI's total volume ([IBTimes India](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/upi-gambit-how-indias-payment-revolution-goes-global-what-comes-next-905362)).

The adoption hierarchy tells you what actually drives volume: Bhutan and Nepal lead on deep economic integration and labour flows; Singapore and the UAE grow on diaspora and tourist traffic; France remains a diplomatic signal more than a commercial corridor ([IBTimes India](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/upi-gambit-how-indias-payment-revolution-goes-global-what-comes-next-905362)). Geography of acceptance is not the constraint — economics of acquisition is. Every UPI QR abroad must be acquired, serviced and settled by a local acquirer who sees thin margins on small-ticket Indian tourists. Without merchant-side economics, MOUs produce launch-day headlines and dormant QRs.

The private sector is moving where diplomacy is slow. Wizz Financial launched its SWITCH app at GFF (September 11) — a multi-country wallet connecting the US, UAE and India, enabling eligible visitors to pay across India under the UPI One World framework and non-residents to move money into UPI accounts in real time, with cross-border payouts to over 80 countries ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/wizz-financial-launches-cross-border-app-connecting-upi-to-global-markets/article71455858.ece); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-news/wizz-financial-launches-the-first-global-cross-border-wallet-for-every-nationality-1.500670801)). Meanwhile the Commerce Secretary's framing at GFF — fintech exports rising from $8 billion to a potential $60–80 billion ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/editors-view/gff-2026-from-innovation-showcase-to-fintech-ecosystem/134101524)) — depends on exactly these corridors being both live and trusted.

For the traveller, the honest advice stands: UPI abroad is a *choice*, not automatically the cheapest one. Independent comparisons of the 11-country footprint conclude that exchange rates, fees and acceptance still determine whether UPI International beats a forex card or cash ([Urban Acres](https://urbanacres.in/international-upi-offers-choice-not-always-the-lowest-cost)). Check the landed cost before you tap.

## The week in one line

India's cross-border strategy is now running on three parallel tracks — diplomatic (UPI's country-by-country map, BRICS task forces), regulatory (PA-CB licences, Bharat Connect multicurrency), and commercial (PhonePe-Visa, Wizz SWITCH, a possible Zelle bridge). The diplomatic track produces headlines; the regulatory track produces infrastructure; the commercial track produces volume. Next year's scoreboard should be judged on the third — cross-border transactions as a share of UPI volume, remittance cost per corridor, and the landed rupee in an NRI's mother's account. Everything else is stagecraft until then.
