# Fintech Deep Dive — Friday | September 18, 2026

## The week regulation stopped hinting and started building

If the last few months of Indian fintech policy were about intent, this week was about architecture. The Department of Financial Services and NPCI converted the MDR debate into statute and a rate card — and were sued within a day. RBI answered a Supreme Court order with the first hard clock on fraud-related account freezes. SEBI and RBI jointly put corporate bonds on a blockchain and settled them in digital rupee. The fintech industry got its second regulator-recognised conscience. And SEBI opened three separate consultations on the resilience of market plumbing. Five stories that define the week in policy and regulation.

## Story 1: UPI gets a price tag — and a courtroom date

The two-piece legal structure nobody was watching for arrived in exactly that order. First, on September 14–15, the Finance Ministry gazette-notified the payments that must stay free: all person-to-person transfers, all UPI transactions up to ₹2,000, and RuPay debit card payments. Then on September 15, NPCI issued its circular completing the other half: a 0.4% MDR on person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000 effective October 15, capped at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 and above ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/mdr-on-upi-notified-npci-says-0-4-fee-applicable-on-person-to-merchant-transactions-above-rs-2000-effective-october-15-2026/articleshow/134265294.cms)). The carve-outs are where the politics live: a flat ₹5 on railways, telecom, insurance, fuel and agricultural inputs above ₹2,000; a concessional 0.02% on capital-market payments; and zero MDR for small merchants in the P2PM category receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month via UPI QR — though those crossing that threshold for three consecutive months graduate into the chargeable category ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-mdr-merchant-fees-small-businesses-transactions-charges-payments-126091600417_1.html); [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/upi-payments-over-rs-2000-to-merchants-will-attract-0-4-fee-10879274)).

The arithmetic makes the design legible. UPI processed 2,451 crore transactions worth ₹29.9 lakh crore in August; NPCI's own FAQ estimates ~96% of P2M transactions sit below ₹2,000 and are untouched ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/economy/policy/upi-charges-from-october-15-faqs-on-who-will-pay-0-4-mdr-and-what-consumers-small-vendors-large-merchants-need-to-know/articleshow/134266293.cms)). But above the threshold you find only ~4% of volume and roughly two-thirds of value — about ₹6 lakh crore a month ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/opinion/upis-next-test-durability-of-mdr-regime/4340732)). This is a tax on value, not on frequency, which is why the kirana with a QR code keeps its free rail while the large merchant funds the ecosystem.

The machinery is now being built in public: a 22-member UPI & Services Steering Committee — banks via the IBA, fintechs via the Payments Council of India — must decide how the MDR pool is split across issuers, acquirers, PSPs and app providers ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/banks-panel-to-decide-on-upi-merchant-charges-on-transactions-above-2-000-126091500672_1.html)), with each participant contributing 5% of its MDR share to an acceptance-infrastructure fund for Tier 3–6 markets, the Northeast, J&K and Ladakh, to be finalised with RBI within three months ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-payments-up-to-rs-2000-remain-free-what-it-means-for-users-126091500150_1.html)).

None of this survived a full day without a challenge. Advocate Anjan Datta filed a PIL in the Supreme Court seeking to quash the notifications, targeting the amended Payment and Settlement Systems Act itself, the differential treatment of UPI and RuPay debit-card transactions, and — the ground every consumer should watch — the argument that charges will ultimately reach citizens despite assurances otherwise ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/plea-in-supreme-court-chances-centres-decision-to-introduce-charges-on-upi-transactions-above-2-000-550329); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/banking/pil-in-sc-challenges-0-4-mdr-on-upi-transactions-above-2-000-in-india-126091601409_1.html)). The government has responded by denying any US pressure behind the move and framing the MDR as the revenue base that lets payment apps beyond the two incumbents finally compete — the 2020 rule capping third-party apps at 30% market share was never enforceable precisely because there was no revenue to compete with ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/business/Economy/govt-denies-foreign-pressure-behind-upi-mdr-says-charge-will-help-indian-upi-apps/article71476075.ece)).

Our read: the sustainability case is real — RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra has said plainly that UPI is "not truly free", with someone absorbing roughly 0.25% per transaction, and a parliamentary committee warned that zero-MDR is not financially sustainable ([Lukmaan IAS summary](https://blog.lukmaanias.com/2026/09/11/global-fintech-fest-2026)). But "merchants pay, consumers don't" is an assurance without yet an enforcement design. A ₹25,000 purchase carries up to ₹100 of MDR; whether that shows up as a surcharge line, a card-network shift, or a quiet price adjustment is the entire consumer question. The three-month rulebook being written now is where surcharge bans, price-display rules and pass-through audits either get built or get lost. Judge the framework there, not at the press conference.

## Story 2: The money-mule SOP — a 60-day clock on the fraud freeze

The least glamorous document of the week may do the most for the most aggrieved class of banking customers. RBI has released the Draft RBI (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026 — a Supreme Court-mandated standard operating procedure for banks dealing with suspected money-mule accounts and cyber-enabled financial fraud, open for comments until October 2 and effective April 1, 2027 ([CorpLaw Updates](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/rbi-draft-kyc-money-mule-sop-2026); [Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/wealth/save/rbi-proposes-60-day-temporary-debit-hold-on-suspicious-money-mule-transactions-key-things-you-need-to-know-about-rbis-kyc-directions/articleshow/134241278.cms)). The August 4, 2026 Supreme Court order directed RBI to formulate and circulate exactly such an SOP, and the draft delivers it with an unusually consumer-protective set of clocks.

The numbers: a temporary debit hold generally cannot exceed 60 days from imposition unless a law-enforcement agency or competent authority instructs otherwise ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/personal-finance/rbi-issues-draft-kyc-proposal-temporary-debit-holds-on-suspected-money-mule-accounts-sets-timelines-14029636.html)). Within that, the customer gets 20 days to submit an explanation; the bank must decide within 10 days of receiving it, or within 30 days if none comes; and if the matter is referred to an LEA, the hold must be lifted on the 31st day from referral absent a contrary instruction ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/personal-finance/rbi-issues-draft-kyc-proposal-temporary-debit-holds-on-suspected-money-mule-accounts-sets-timelines-14029636.html); [BW Businessworld](https://www.businessworld.in/article/rbi-proposes-60-day-cap-on-money-mule-account-holds-623398)). The hold applies to suspected transactions of ₹1,000 and above; amount-level holds are the default, account-level freezes are a last resort for exceptional circumstances; banks must keep filing STRs with FIU-IND (failing to do so for a confirmed mule account is itself non-compliance); and grievances must be resolved within 30 days ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/explained-rbi-proposes-60-day-debit-holds-for-suspected-mule-accounts-126091500610_1.html); [ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/rbi-proposes-60-day-cap-on-temporary-debit-holds-for-suspected-money-mule-accounts/134114446)).

Why this matters: for years, the phrase "your account has been frozen" arrived with no timeline, no explanation and no appeal — a national consumer grievance amplified when cyber-fraud reporting mechanics led to mass debit freezes on accounts that had merely received a tainted payment. This draft converts discretion into a schedule. The escape hatch is the LEA override: a "contrary instruction" can extend a hold indefinitely, so the consumer-protection value depends on instructions being specific, recorded and reviewable. The other dependency is operational — banks must actually build amount-level holds rather than reflexively freezing whole accounts. If you ever face a hold, the draft hands you a script: respond in writing within the 20-day window, demand the reason-for-debit disclosure, and escalate to the RBI Ombudsman at day 30 if the grievance stalls.

## Story 3: Demat 2.0 — bonds born on a ledger, settled in digital rupee

The RBI and SEBI joint announcement at Global Fintech Fest deserves more attention than its slide-deck framing suggests. Governor Sanjay Malhotra and SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey launched Demat 2.0, a pilot combining three things in one flow: corporate bonds issued natively on distributed ledger technology, ownership records retained by statutory depositories, and the funds leg settled in RBI's wholesale CBDC — with smart contracts handling asset servicing ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-sebis-demat-2-0-tokenised-corporate-bonds)). This isn't a sandbox promise; the pilot already has three live issues totalling ₹1,025 crore — REC ₹500 crore on September 7 (7.30% coupon, ~20-month tenure, 18 investors), L&T ₹500 crore on September 9 (7.40%, three years, four investors), and IIFL ₹25 crore (9.10%, two years, a single investor) ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-rbi-launch-demat-2-0-pilot-for-tokenised-corporate-bonds-cbdc-settlement); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-sebis-demat-2-0-tokenised-corporate-bonds)).

The design choices are what separate this from crypto-adjacent theatre. Keeping depositories as the legal record-owners means tokenised bonds live inside securities law, not beside it — there is no parallel system of title to reconcile when something breaks. Settling in wholesale CBDC means delivery-versus-payment in central bank money: atomic finality, no interbank credit exposure in the settlement leg. And smart-contract servicing attacks a real consumer-adjacent failure mode — coupon payments and redemptions that are late or misrouted. SEBI's claim that this combination is a global first is credible on current evidence ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-sebis-demat-2-0-tokenised-corporate-bonds)).

The follow-through question is whether the technology deepens the market that matters. An Economic Times commentary proposes the natural complement: every new listed corporate bond issue of ₹200 crore or more rated AA and below should carry a professional market-making arrangement, with permitted spreads of 75–150 basis points — because India's bond market's real defect isn't settlement speed, it's the absence of liquidity below AAA ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/opinion/et-commentary/oh-lets-look-beyond-aaa-a-new-market-making-framework-could-make-lower-rated-corporate-bonds-more-investible/articleshow/134317930.cms)). Tokenisation without market-makers gives you a beautiful ledger of bonds nobody trades. Watch whether the pilot's next phase pairs with a liquidity mandate.

## Story 4: A second conscience — Unified Fintech Forum recognised as SRO

On September 10, RBI accorded recognition to the Unified Fintech Forum as a Self-Regulatory Organisation in the fintech sector (SRO-FT) — the second entity to receive it after FACE in August 2024 ([Banking Frontiers](https://bankingfrontiers.com/unified-fintech-forum-gets-sro-recognition-from-rbi); [The Banking Updates](https://thebankingupdates.com/daily-news/the-banking-frontline-11-september-2026)). UFF is the former Digital Lenders Association of India, and its 118 members now include NBFCs, lending service providers, technology service providers, credit bureaus and account aggregators, plus nine associate partners. The recognition comes under the May 2024 SRO-FT framework, whose stated job is establishing standards, enforcing ethical conduct, resolving disputes and fostering transparency among members.

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman used the announcement to set the tone for the sector's AI debate — AI as a "double-edged sword" that can detect fraud and perpetrate it, a case for "soft-touch regulation" that doesn't smother innovation, and a suggestion that Indian fintechs need a common industry platform to speak with one voice abroad ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/ai-a-double-edged-sword-fm-sitharaman-flags-innovation-risks-urges-efficiency-without-fragility/articleshow/134061412.cms)).

The honest assessment of SROs: they are conscience, not constabulary. An SRO can write codes, audit members, publish complaint data and expel — it cannot fine, licence or compensate consumers. Two SROs for one sector creates a coverage map worth watching: FACE's base is lending-ecosystem heavy, UFF's spans lenders, TSPs, bureaus and account aggregators — the membership overlap will define which conduct issues fall between two stools. The test that matters is boring and measurable: does UFF publish its conduct standards, member complaint statistics and disciplinary actions within its first year? The urgency is rising because agentic AI is walking into finance now — SBI Chairman C.S. Setty called for a "know your agent" framework covering identity, authentication, consent, transaction limits and audit trails, while RBI Deputy Governor Rohit Jain's formulation was sharper: an institution "may outsource the computation, but it cannot outsource the consequence" ([The Banking Updates](https://thebankingupdates.com/daily-news/the-banking-frontline-11-september-2026); [Ikigai Law](https://www.ikigailaw.com/article/703/fintales-edition-49-gold-tokenisation-ai-accountability-and-mdr-split)). Self-regulation that doesn't grow an AI chapter fast will find itself regulating the last wave.

One rumour worth killing while we're here: viral messages claiming a September 15 deadline for merchant re-KYC under RBI rules were false — no such circular exists, and RBI has set no such deadline ([Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/rbi/No-Official-RBI-September-15-KYC-Deadline-for-Merchants/6a965d99a703e4a8160c3e9b)). Deadlines that arrive via WhatsApp forwards deserve the same verification as payment requests.

## Story 5: SEBI stress-tests the plumbing — three MII consultation papers

While the MDR fight consumed the headlines, SEBI quietly opened a resilience review of market infrastructure institutions — the exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories whose failure would be everyone's problem at once. A September 9 consultation paper proposes governance strengthening for MIIs, including widening the director pool ([ELP Law](https://elplaw.in/leadership/sebi-consultation-paper-on-strengthening-governance-of-miis)). A companion paper on business continuity proposes shrinking disaster-recovery drills to at least four hours including switchover from primary to disaster-recovery site, run on non-working days — replacing the once-a-year full-trading-day ritual with something more frequent and more honest — and requires stock exchanges to build a framework for recovering lost trade data from clearing corporations during replication disruptions ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/sebi-proposes-shorter-dr-drills-stronger-resilience-norms-for-market-infrastructure-institutions/article71468260.ece)).

The third paper may prove the most consequential: extending SEBI's IT and cybersecurity framework to subsidiaries of MIIs, with comments due October 2. The gap it closes is real — subsidiaries running critical market systems and data sit outside the explicit jurisdiction of the MII framework today, a fact SEBI's Technical Advisory Committee flagged as far back as December 2025 ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/stocks/news/sebi-proposes-extending-cybersecurity-rules-to-subsidiaries-of-miis/articleshow/134066718.cms)). Regulation finally following the org chart is usually a sign the regulator has studied how things actually break. For the consumer, the stake is unglamorous and absolute: depository data integrity and settlement finality are what stand between a normal Tuesday and a week of "your shares are temporarily unavailable."

## The week in one line

Policy moved from positions to structures this week — UPI got a price and a plaintiff, frozen accounts got a 60-day clock, bonds got a new birthplace with old legal certainties, fintech got a second conscience, and market plumbing got a drill schedule. The deadlines that matter now are the unglamorous ones: October 2 for the KYC-mule and MII comments, three months for the MDR rulebook, April 1, 2027 for the freeze SOP. That is where the consumer-favourable versions of these rules either get written or get diluted — and showing up in comment periods costs nothing.
