# Fintech Brief — September 19, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### 1. Digital gold may finally get a regulator — and a physical-backing rule

The government has begun discussions to tighten rules for the ~$3 billion digital gold market, including a requirement that every unit sold to investors be backed by physical bullion, with the product brought under the joint oversight of the RBI and SEBI, according to people familiar with the matter. [The Economic Times reports](https://m.economictimes.com/news/economy/finance/digital-gold-may-come-under-rbi-sebi-oversight-physical-backing-proposed/articleshow/134319873.cms) the finance ministry has sought views from regulators, banks and other stakeholders, and is examining whether to formally classify digital gold as a "security" — which would pull it under existing securities law.

This is a product that has lived in a regulatory gap for a decade: sold inside everyday fintech apps, it is neither a deposit, nor a commodity contract, nor a recognised security — SEBI itself warned investors about digital gold platforms back in 2025. Today consumers essentially hold a promise from the platform that bullion sits in a vault. [Industry, notably, is welcoming the move](https://www.indiatoday.in/business/story/buying-digital-gold-rbi-sebi-may-soon-tighten-rules-around-your-investment-2997508-2026-09-18): MMTC-PAMP's managing director Samit Guha said the ecosystem "needs some regulatory oversight urgently," and platforms have already formed a self-regulatory body, the Digital Precious Metals Assurance Council of India, whose members include bullion providers MMTC-PAMP and SafeGold. For consumers, verified vault audits and formal recourse would be a genuine upgrade over trust-me marketing — the details of which regulator does what will decide how much.

### 2. RBI Deputy Governor: UPI MDR won't push India back to cash

One day after the Finance Ministry's political defence of the new UPI merchant fee, the RBI added its own reassurance. Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu said on Friday that fears the 0.4% MDR — effective October 15 on person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000 — would trigger a rush back to cash are unlikely to materialise, calling it only an ["initial apprehension"](https://www.thehindu.com/business/Economy/mdr-apprehension-unlikely-to-spur-higher-cash-usage-rbi-deputy-governor/article71482058.ece). He reiterated that the charge is paid by merchants, not consumers, and is capped at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 or more.

The comment matters because the cash-reversion argument has become the main consumer-facing critique of the fee — and because Murmu used the same platform to flag a second theme regulators keep returning to: as financial institutions move "from human intelligence to machine intelligence," boards must educate themselves on the risks. Watch whether merchants quietly surcharge or steer customers to cash at the margin after October 15; the rulebook forbids passing MDR to customers, but enforcement is the real test.

### 3. NSE IPO fully subscribed on day two

India's second-largest IPO ever crossed the finish line of demand early: the ₹22,568.94 crore NSE issue was [fully subscribed on its second day](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/ipo/nse-ipo-subscription-status-today-latest-nse-gmp-day-2-should-you-apply-or-not-14032691.html), drawing 1.15x bids by 5 PM Friday after a lukewarm day one that saw just 43% subscription. The issue is entirely an offer for sale of 12.64 crore shares at a price band of ₹1,700–1,785, so the exchange itself raises nothing — selling shareholders take the proceeds. Bidding closes September 21, with listing expected around September 24; the grey market premium has hovered at 6–8%.

The slow start had less to do with appetite than with a [brutally crowded IPO week](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/nse-ipo-why-did-indias-second-largest-initial-public-offering-not-get-fully-subscribed-on-day-1/articleshow/134316486.cms) — five mainboard and six SME issues totalling ~₹24,500 crore opened in the same five days. Brokerages are split on value: at a P/E of roughly 42.9x FY26 earnings, Religare calls the valuation fair-but-full, noting FY26 earnings took a hit from regulatory changes on F&O activity — the same rule-tightening that trimmed NSE's most profitable business.

### 4. Funding desk: a slow week, but fintech still lands

Venture inflows [fell sharply in the week of September 12–18](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/weekly-funding-roundup-sept-12-18-sharp-fall-in-vc-inflow), with most of the money at very early stages. The fintech-relevant exceptions: digital-gold savings platform [Jar raised ₹29 crore](https://ascendants.in/funding-feed/jar-raises-rs-29-crore-unitary-fund-series-b2-23-percent-higher-valuation-digital-gold) in a Series B2 extension from existing backer Unitary Fund, at a valuation 23% higher than its previous round — a vote of confidence in gold-savings behaviour just as regulators move to formalise the category (see story 1). MSME lender UGRO Capital raised ₹380 crore (~$39.6 million) from Dutch development financier FMO, keeping foreign development capital flowing into India's lending stack even as generalist VCs turn cautious.
