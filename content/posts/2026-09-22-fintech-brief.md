# Fintech Brief — September 22, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### 1. NSE IPO closes 5.7x subscribed — ₹90,000 crore chased the country's biggest exchange

India's largest stock exchange wrapped up its landmark IPO with bids for about 50.4 crore shares against 8.86 crore on offer — [subscribed 5.69 times](https://www.firstpost.com/business/nse-ipo-subscription-reasons-strong-investor-demand-14047422.html) — worth roughly ₹90,000 crore at the upper price band of ₹1,700–1,785. The ₹22,568.94-crore issue drew over $10 billion in bids, making it India's second-largest IPO after Hyundai Motor India's 2024 issue. Institutional demand dominated: the qualified institutional buyer portion was subscribed 12.68 times and non-institutional 6.55 times — retail just 1.39 times. Shares list on the BSE on September 24. NSE brings a 93% share of India's cash equity market, around 75% of options trading, and 129 million registered investors as of March 2026. The consumer-relevant wrinkle: the exchange that sets the fees investors pay is about to become an investor-owned company — and retail, which got the thinnest allotment, will meet it on listing day at whatever price the institutions' 12.68x hunger sets.

### 2. RBI finalises Basel III market-risk rules: trading-book arbitrage gets harder, structural FX gets relief

The RBI has issued [final directions on minimum capital requirements for market risk under Basel III](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/banks/rbi-issues-final-directions-for-minimum-capital-requirement-for-market-risk-under-basel-iii-14034735.html), tightening how banks classify investments between trading and banking books to prevent regulatory arbitrage. Banks can no longer park trading-book instruments in the banking book to show lower capital — if they do, they must compute the capital difference and hold it. The framework covers interest-rate, equity and foreign-exchange risk, with market-risk capital to be met at the close of every business day. Two offsets soften the blow: banks may exclude structural foreign-currency positions — capital and unremitted surplus in overseas subsidiaries, branches, IFSC Banking Units and SEZ offshore units — from their net open position, case-by-case and for at least six months at a time ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/rbi-allows-banks-to-exclude-structural-fx-positions-from-net-open-position-126092101259_1.html)). A 12% specific-risk capital charge now applies to certain non-equity capital instruments issued by banks, irrespective of external credit rating — relevant for anyone holding bank bonds through debt funds.

### 3. Earned wage access fintech Refyne lines up ~$50 million at unicorn valuation — and eyes broking

[Tiger Global-backed Refyne is in talks to raise around $50 million at a $1 billion valuation](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/tiger-global-backed-refyne-looks-to-raise-50-million-at-unicorn-valuation-may-enter-broking-article-14034906.html), Moneycontrol reports, with existing investors expected to lead — XYZ Venture Capital pegged at about $20 million and QED Investors at $10 million. The Bengaluru earned wage access platform, which lets employees draw salary already earned before payday, would put the fresh capital toward a potential broking entry, deepening offerings to its captive base of 2.5–3 million users. Its last equity round was an $82 million Series B in 2022, so this would be the first check in four years. The consumer question: an EWA firm pivoting to broking is a distribution story — selling investing products to salaried users who were drawing advances — and the line between "access wages early" and "leverage your paycheck" deserves scrutiny as these products converge.

### 4. "Keep UPI free for a few more years": ex-NITI Aayog VC joins the MDR resistance

Rajiv Kumar, former NITI Aayog vice-chairman, has [urged the government to keep UPI merchant payments free](https://m.economictimes.com/industry/banking/finance/upi-charges-could-reverse-digital-payments-gains-push-users-back-to-cash-says-ex-niti-vc-rajiv-kumar/amp_articleshow/134386120.cms) for at least a few more years, warning that even a small fee could reverse digital payments gains and push users back to cash. UPI should be treated as a public good with extensive positive externalities, he told PTI — review only once the currency-to-GDP ratio shows a tendency to decline. It is the highest-profile voice yet against the 0.4% MDR on person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000 effective October 15, capped at ₹300 for payments of ₹75,000 and above. The resistance now has three fronts: petrol pumps refusing UPI above ₹2,000, Jammu traders protesting, and now a former government policymaker making the public-good case. For consumers the immediate risk is behavioural: merchants absorbing the fee by surcharging, nudging cash, or refusing higher-value UPI — the exact reversal Kumar warns about.
