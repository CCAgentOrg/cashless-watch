# Fintech Brief — September 23, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### 1. NPCI calls GST-on-MDR fears "misplaced" — but the resistance isn't buying it

With the 0.4% MDR on person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000 set to kick in on October 15, NPCI has [pushed back on reports that 18% GST on the MDR will burden small merchants](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/npci-clarifies-18-percent-gst-on-upi-payments-over-2000-mdr-merchant-impact-10889118). In a statement on X, the payments body argued the apprehensions are misplaced on three counts: transactions up to ₹2,000 carry zero MDR and therefore no GST; merchants with monthly UPI receipts of up to ₹1 lakh aren't liable to pay MDR at all; and GST-registered merchants can claim input tax credit on the MDR they pay, so they "do not bear the cost of GST" ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/banking/finance/npci-clarifies-no-burden-of-gst-on-upi-mdr-for-small-merchants/articleshow/134406450.cms)). The gap in that argument, as the [Indian Express notes](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/npci-clarifies-18-percent-gst-on-upi-payments-over-2000-mdr-merchant-impact-10889118), is merchants whose turnover is below the GST registration threshold — they pay the GST embedded in MDR but can't claim it back. Meanwhile the political economy of the fee keeps heating up: petrol pumps are refusing UPI above ₹2,000, traders' bodies are protesting, and ex-NITI Aayog vice-chairman Rajiv Kumar has made the public-good case against the fee. On the other side, RBI Deputy Governor S C Murmu said last week the MDR is [unlikely to trigger a shift back to cash](https://www.thehindu.com/business/Economy/mdr-apprehension-unlikely-to-spur-higher-cash-usage-rbi-deputy-governor/article71482058.ece) — calling it "initial apprehension" as the ecosystem adjusts. For consumers the watch-item is unchanged: whether merchants above the exemption line surcharge, nudge cash, or simply stop accepting high-value UPI after October 15.

### 2. Moneyview's ₹1,092-crore IPO opens tomorrow as the fintech listing wave builds

Consumer-lending fintech Moneyview has [fixed its price band at ₹32–34 per share for a ₹1,092-crore IPO](https://www.thefinancialworld.com/moneyview-limited-has-rs-1092-crore-ipo) that opens September 24 and closes September 28, listing on the BSE and NSE — the same day the NSE itself debuts. At the upper band the issue values the company at around [₹6,000 crore](https://boldnewsonline.com/moneyview-sets-ipo-price-band-at-rs-32-34-targets-rs-1092-crore), a marked step down from the ~$1.2 billion investors once paid up for. Axis Capital, BofA Securities, IIFL Capital Services and Kotak Mahindra Capital are the book-running lead managers. Moneyview pitches itself as a digital-first lender for "Middle India" — average customer age around 32, teachers and accountants among them — with digital personal loans its fastest-growing segment. The offering lands in a crowded week: [Fortune India counts six IPOs worth about ₹4,500 crore](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/ipo/acevector-ipo-snapdeal-parent-cuts-issue-size-to-420-crore-price-band-fixed-at-30-32/160448) opening this week, with NSE and Hero Motors among six listings. And the private fintech pipeline behind it is filling fast — used-car platform Spinny [filed confidential IPO papers with SEBI on Monday for a ₹2,500–3,000-crore issue](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/ipo/spinny-files-confidential-ipo-papers-eyes-2500-3000-crore-issue/160456) targeting a 2027 debut, CarDekho's parent [has begun converting into a public company ahead of a reported ₹3,000-crore issue](https://entrackr.com), and PhonePe is eyeing a February–March 2027 listing. The consumer question for Moneyview specifically: a lender going public just as UPI MDR and rising rate expectations test its borrowers' wallets.

### 3. PhonePe gets UAE in-principle nod — its first real step beyond India's borders

PhonePe, India's largest UPI app, has received [in-principle approval from the UAE Central Bank for two payment licences](https://inc42.com/buzz/ipo-bound-phonepe-secures-in-principle-nod-for-uae-payments-business): Retail Payment Services and Card Schemes, and Stored Value Facilities — the latter permitting digital wallets. The approvals don't allow commercial operations yet; PhonePe must still clear final regulatory requirements. Once it does, it plans to work with regional banks and payment providers and to support the UAE's domestic networks, Aani and Jaywan. This is the company's first international foray involving actual domestic operations — its existing UAE presence is limited to letting Indian travellers pay at NEOPAY and Network International terminals via NPCI International. The timing is no accident: Inc42 reported last week that PhonePe paused its IPO in March amid West Asia tensions, and is now [revising plans toward a February–March 2027 listing](https://inc42.com/buzz/with-upi-mdr-clarity-phonepe-eyes-ipo-by-march-2027/) precisely because the MDR framework gives its merchant business a monetisation path. The numbers it carries abroad are sober: a 45.9% UPI volume share on 11.25 billion August transactions worth ₹14.26 lakh crore — against a [net loss that widened 62% to ₹2,792 crore in FY26](https://inc42.com/buzz/ipo-bound-phonepe-posts-%E2%82%B92792-cr-loss-in-fy26-revenue-up-11-5-yoy/) on revenue of ₹7,920.5 crore. For consumers, a Wallet-licensed PhonePe in the UAE could eventually mean cheaper remittances — or one more Indian payments giant exporting its rails instead of fixing its economics at home.
