# Fintech Brief — September 25, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### 1. SEBI's board clears a sweeping rulebook refresh — new PMS and settlement regulations, wider FPI and accredited-investor access

Markets regulator SEBI's board on Thursday approved [a broad package of capital-market reforms](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/sebi-board-meet-outcome-big-pms-revamp-easier-settlement-norms-wider-fpi-access-19997999.htm), headlined by two full regulation rewrites: the [SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2026](https://www.zeebiz.com/market-news/news-sebi-board-meeting-8-key-decisions-on-pms-commodity-derivatives-reits-and-more-402816) replacing the 2020 framework, and the SEBI (Settlement Proceedings) Regulations, 2026 replacing the 2018 version. The PMS overhaul widens what discretionary portfolio managers can invest in — including IPOs and primary-market debt issuances — while [FPIs get access to physically settled, non-agricultural commodity derivative contracts](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/sebi-clears-pms-reforms-expands-fpi-access-to-commodities/54953120260924). The board also [widened the accredited-investor pool](https://www.fortuneindia.com/markets/sebi-clears-pms-fpi-accredited-investor-reforms-broadens-avenues/161093): individuals with ₹5 crore in securities-market assets (₹20 crore for body corporates) can now qualify alongside the existing income and net-worth tests, with manager-led accreditation valid three years and deemed status for FPIs. Other decisions: REITs and listed InvITs may issue depository receipts overseas, research analysts are relieved of recording some institutional calls, a common advertisement code comes in, and first-time NCD issuers get a listing relaxation. The consumer watch-item is the accredited-investor widening — it quietly decides who can be sold riskier unlisted products, so the guardrails around that gatekeeping deserve scrutiny.

### 2. NPCI's MDR reassurance tour: Asbe says volumes won't crack as October 15 nears

With the 0.4% MDR on UPI person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000 taking effect October 15, NPCI CEO Dilip Asbe told the [SBI Banking & Economics Conclave that the fee is unlikely to materially affect transaction volumes](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/upi-mdr-kicks-in-october-15-why-npci-expects-no-hit-to-transaction-volumes-11790238804386.html) — around 96% of transactions stay under ₹2,000 and therefore zero-MDR — and that the revenue will be reinvested in soundboxes and AI-based payment infrastructure. He also put a number on the system's pain: [UPI transactions cost banks about ₹21,000 crore annually](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/upi-transactions-costing-banks-about-21k-cr-annually-says-npci-ceo-asbe/articleshow/134472477.cms), the bill the new fee is meant to start recovering. Meanwhile [NPCI has told NBFCs that UPI loan repayments above ₹2,000 will attract a flat ₹5 fee](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/nbfc-loan-repayments-to-attract-rs-5-flat-fee-npci-tells-shadow-banks-article-14037189.html), putting EMI collections in the capped-fee bucket alongside railways, fuel and telecom. For consumers, the open question remains merchant behaviour after October 15 — whether shops above the exemption line absorb the charge, pass it on, or quietly nudge customers back to cash.

### 3. Moneyview's ₹1,092-crore IPO opens to a 1.4x day-one subscription

Digital lender Moneyview's IPO opened Thursday and was [subscribed 1.44 times by 5 pm on day one](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/ipo/moneyview-ipo-gmp-today-september-24-check-subscription-status-of-moneyview-should-you-apply-or-avoid-14037161.html), with grey-market premium around 42%. The company had raised [₹327.5 crore from anchor investors](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/moneyview-raises-rs-327-5-cr-from-anchor-investors-ahead-of-ipo-625357), allotting 9.63 crore shares at ₹34 — the top of the ₹32–34 band — to 20 funds including SBI, HDFC and ICICI Prudential mutual funds, Goldman Sachs and HDFC Life, with domestic mutual funds taking [72% of the anchor book](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/moneyview-raises-rs-327-5-cr-from-anchor-investors-ahead-of-ipo-625357). The issue — a fresh ₹750 crore plus an offer-for-sale of 10.04 crore shares worth ₹341.6 crore by promoters and investors including Tiger Global, Accel India and Ribbit Capital — values the company at ₹6,000 crore at the upper band. It runs until September 28. The fundamentals on display: 14.03 crore registered users, 48 financial partners and ₹22,520 crore in managed assets as of June 2026, with ₹325 crore of the fresh proceeds earmarked for loan disbursals under default-loss-guarantee arrangements.

### 4. Cross-border payments week: PayGlocal wins GIFT City approval while PhonePe details its UAE buildout

Cross-border payments got two regulatory boosts in 24 hours. Payments firm PayGlocal has received [IFSCA approval to set up a payment service provider in GIFT City](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/personal-finance/story/payglocal-gets-ifsca-approval-to-set-up-payment-service-provider-in-gift-city-557482-2026-09-24), letting its IFSC entity issue accounts including e-money, process cross-border money transfers and do merchant acquisition — part of the steady buildout of GIFT City as India's springboard for global payment operations. In the UAE, PhonePe CEO for International Payments Ritesh Pai [detailed plans to build a unified, full-stack payments ecosystem](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/phonepe-to-build-unified-full-stack-payments-ecosystem-in-uae-ritesh-pai-625365) — local rails, consumer wallets, merchant acquiring and remittances — after last week's in-principle approval from the UAE central bank for two licences. Domestically, PhonePe is [hiring 20,000 people and deploying 50 lakh payment devices](https://money.rediff.com/amp/news/market/phonepe-to-hire-20k-deploy-50-lakh-devices-for-rural-expansion/54925620260924) for small-merchant rural expansion. The pattern to watch: Indian payment firms are simultaneously exporting rails abroad and deepening device-led acceptance at home — both monetisation stories ahead of PhonePe's IPO.
