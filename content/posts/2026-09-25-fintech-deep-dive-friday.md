# Fintech Deep Dive — Friday | September 25, 2026

## Twenty days to go-live: the MDR state gets its ledger, insurers get a new rulebook, and the comment season opens

If last week was when UPI got a price, this week was when every actor in the system took a position on it. The revenue pool got a split, traders got a protest day, the government got a fact-check unit working overtime, and two regulators quietly redrew the economics of insurance distribution and market plumbing while the shouting was elsewhere. Here is the policy week, story by story.

## Story 1: The MDR gets a ledger — and a street movement

The most consequential number of the week wasn't a rate; it was a ratio. The NPCI-led UPI & Services Steering Committee has finalised how the 0.4% merchant discount rate — live from October 15 on specified person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 and above — will be divided: 40% to issuer banks, 30% to merchant acquirers, 20% to UPI apps such as PhonePe, and 10% to the apps' partner banks ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/business/news/phonepe-bets-on-merchant-push-as-mdr-returns/4346791)). Citi estimates the fee creates an annual revenue pool of ₹10,000–20,600 crore across banks, apps and payment aggregators, with about a quarter flowing to app providers. That is the number that ends six years of zero-MDR economics: for the first time since 2020, there is a revenue base on which payment apps beyond the two incumbents can actually compete.

The industry response was telling. PhonePe said the framework lets it "take a long view" and invest in merchant expansion. NPCI managing director Dilip Asbe, speaking at the SBI Banking & Economics Conclave, said the revenue would be recycled into the ecosystem — soundboxes and AI-based solutions — and that he did not foresee a major impact on UPI volumes or value ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/business/news/phonepe-bets-on-merchant-push-as-mdr-returns/4346791)). The scale he is defending: UPI processed roughly 24.9 billion transactions worth about ₹30.15 lakh crore in August, and NPCI's own arithmetic holds that the ~4% of merchant transactions that will attract MDR account for about 67% of merchant value ([Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/rbi-urges-banks-to-treat-technology-failures-as-core-risk); [Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/business/news/phonepe-bets-on-merchant-push-as-mdr-returns/4346791)).

The government spent the week pre-empting the two failure modes it fears most: merchants passing the fee to customers, and merchants fleeing to cash. Finance ministry officials are in talks with payment aggregators and platforms on mechanisms to shield consumers from pass-through ([News18](https://www.news18.com/business/economy/upi-mdr-govt-weighs-steps-to-ensure-merchants-dont-pass-fee-to-consumers-ws-l-10338218.html); [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/economy/upi-mdr-rollout-govt-weighs-checks-to-prevent-merchants-passing-cost-to-consumers-14032701.html)). Government sources told NDTV the MDR is a merchant-side processing charge, "not a tax" — it does not touch the Consolidated Fund — and reiterated that 96% of transactions are unaffected ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/mdr-upi-charge-not-tax-96-transactions-wont-be-unaffected-government-sources-12093798)). On rollback, the message was blunt: none ([PropNewstime](https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MzY5MjI=/upi-mdr-charge-above-inr-2-000-will-not-be-rolled-back-government-says)). Implementation will be monitored daily from October 15, partly to discourage a shift back to cash, and existing QR codes and soundboxes keep working ([Arunachal24/News synthesis](https://arunachal24.in/upi-mdr-from-october-15-0-4-charge-on-specified-p2m-payments-above-%E2%82%B92000)).

Traders did not wait for October 15. Madhya Pradesh trade bodies organised a "No UPI Day", covering QR codes in black cloth and asking customers to pay cash, with participation claimed across Indore, Bhopal and Jabalpur. The Indore Retail Garments Association's president demanded withdrawal of the fee — and promised, if it stood, that traders would "immerse UPI QR code machines in water bodies." A consumer group in Jabalpur added the sharpest procedural critique: the charge was imposed on citizens without inviting objections ([Economic Times/PTI](https://m.economictimes.com/news/india/mp-traders-protest-upi-mdr-with-no-upi-day-urge-customers-to-pay-cash/amp_articleshow/134441969.cms)). In Parliament, BJD MP Santrupt Misra argued the government can simply bear UPI's network cost and proposed funding alternatives ([Rediff](https://www.rediff.com/news/report/bjd-mp-santrupt-misra-govt-can-bear-upi-network-cost/20260919.htm)).

The consumer-design critique is getting sharper too. The Retailers Association of India argues the ₹1 lakh monthly exemption threshold for small merchants is too low and excludes millions of micro-enterprises; Frontline's analysis notes the fee curve is regressive by design — 0.4% on a ₹3,000 payment (₹12) but a flat ₹300 above ₹75,000, a falling effective rate as tickets grow — and argues thresholds should be indexed to CPI so inflation doesn't silently push small merchants into the chargeable category ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/economy/upi-mdr-merchant-transaction-fee-costs-impact-consumers/article71491046.ece)). Meanwhile the rumour economy did its bit: PIB Fact Check debunked viral claims that banks were told to stock ATMs ahead of the fee rollout — no such instruction exists ([PIB Fact Check](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdnnSPdyj8Q)).

Our read: the split (40/30/20/10) tells you who the fee is for — issuers and acquirers take 70%, which is the price of keeping banks invested in a rail they don't profit from today. But the protest and the pass-through talks reveal the same thing: "merchants pay, consumers don't" is still an assurance in search of an enforcement design. Whatever the ministry and payment aggregators agree on before October 15 — surcharge display rules, pass-through monitoring — is the actual consumer policy here. Watch it, not the press conferences.

## Story 2: IRDAI redraws insurance distribution — and the market prices the disruption in hours

The insurance regulator released a draft on September 23 that attempts to fix the industry's oldest consumer failure: mis-selling driven by front-loaded commissions. The paper proposes cutting customer acquisition costs, linking distributor compensation to long-term policy retention while capping early payouts, enforcing phased reductions in expense-of-management limits — down to 12.5% of gross direct premium income for life insurers and 20% for general insurers — and merging the many intermediary categories into streamlined groups. Feedback is open until October 25 ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/banking/six-key-concerns-in-irdai-s-draft-paper-that-spooked-insurance-industry-article-14037459.html)).

The case for the surgery is persistency — the share of policies that stay paid. For some insurers, 10-year persistency drops as low as 8%, meaning nine of every ten long-term policies quietly die early, usually after the agent's commission has been fully paid. Agents and brokers counter that sharp cuts to upfront commissions create immediate cash-flow strain in a distribution force that works on advances ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/banking/six-key-concerns-in-irdai-s-draft-paper-that-spooked-insurance-industry-article-14037459.html)). The regulatory obligation isn't discretionary: after the Insurance Act amendments, IRDAI is legally required to issue new commission regulations within six months, placing the deadline around September 2026 — and commission payouts across life and non-life grew much faster than premium collections in FY25 ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/irdai-commission-rules-due-by-september-2026-ceilings-likely-over-micromanagement-ex-irdai-member-nilesh-sathe/128185577)).

Markets delivered their verdict within a day: PB Fintech (Policybazaar's parent) and Turtlemint crashed up to 28% on September 24, and insurance, banking and NBFC stocks broadly weakened — because bancassurance and online distribution economics sit directly in the blast radius ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/pb-fintech-turtlemint-stocks-crash-20-after-irdai-s-consultation-paper-on-insurance-distribution-14037001.html); [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/stocks/irdai-draft-rules-impact-insurance-bank-nbfc-stocks)). Zerodha's Nithin Kamath used the episode for his recurring sermon: "for any regulated business, the biggest risk is regulatory risk. Today's IRDAI draft on insurance commissions is a good reminder" ([CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/business/nithin-kamaths-regulatory-risk-warning-what-irdais-insurance-commission-draft-means-for-broking-and-fintech-19997868.htm)). Former SBI chairman Dinesh Kumar Khara took the opposite view, calling the overhaul radical and predicting distributor earnings recover 20–25% as higher retention compounds ([CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/irdai-insurance-distributor-earnings-20-25-percent-recovery-dinesh-kumar-khara-pb-fintech-alpha-article-19997226.htm)).

Our read: this is the most consumer-significant draft of the month, more than the louder UPI fee. Front-loaded commissions are why a missold endowment policy feels free at signing and ruinous at surrender. Tying payouts to persistency aligns the agent's income with the customer's actual outcome — the exact incentive inversion consumer advocates have asked for. The two questions that decide whether this works: does the persistency definition reward *suitability* (policies kept because they fit) or merely *surrender-avoidance* (pressure tactics to stop lapsation)? And does the EoM squeeze leave digital distributors enough margin to keep serving small tickets? The industry will lobby hard before October 25; consumers should be commenting too, because the loudest voice in that room usually drafts the exceptions.

## Story 3: SEBI's board day — and the expiry-day price fight

SEBI's board meeting on September 24 cleared a stack of reforms. Portfolio managers get a comprehensive rule overhaul, including permission to invest in IPOs and primary debt. Foreign portfolio investors gain access to non-agri commodity derivatives, with safeguards: FPIs must square off or roll over positions before the delivery period, with arrangements for stragglers. The accredited investor pool is widened; vault manager rules expand; and REIT/InvIT governance shifts voting thresholds to 75% of votes cast rather than 75% of all outstanding units, with complete exit for dissenting unitholders when a sponsor changes ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/sebi-overhauls-pms-rules-widens-fpi-access-to-commodity-derivatives-14037621.html); [Fortune India](https://www.fortuneindia.com/amp/story/markets/sebi-clears-pms-fpi-accredited-investor-reforms-broadens-avenues/161093)).

The quieter board decisions matter more for retail. SEBI amended its settlement regulations to separate "wrongful gains" from the base amount used to compute penalties — killing a double-counting concern — and to make adjudication more institutional and consistent, with whole-time member Kamlesh Varshney stressing that egregious cases still won't be settleable ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/banking-and-finance/sebi-changes-settlement-rules-clears-common-advertising-code-10892849)). The board also approved a 90-day one-time settlement window for specified pending cases under the new framework ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/sebi-overhauls-pms-rules-widens-fpi-access-to-commodity-derivatives-14037621.html)).

Meanwhile the live fight is over how derivative contracts expire. A September 12 consultation proposes changing expiry-day settlement prices for index and single-stock derivatives — currently vulnerable to manipulation in the closing minutes of the cash market — by blending the volume-weighted average price with the closing-auction system. It has drawn more than 3,500 comments and closes October 3. Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on September 22 the regulator intends to address the concerns and will issue amended rules after analysing feedback ([Rediff/PTI](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/sebi-to-address-derivative-settlement-price-concerns/54789720260922); [New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/amp/story/business/2026/Sep/22/sebi-chief-ready-to-address-expiry-day-settlement-price-concerns); [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/banking-and-finance/sebi-changes-settlement-rules-clears-common-advertising-code-10892849)). Reuters Breakingviews offered the necessary skeptical counterpoint: India's trading tweaks target the wrong problem — the real defect is liquidity below AAA in the bond market, not microstructure friction ([Reuters](https://www.breakingviews.com/columns/considered-view/indias-trading-tweaks-target-wrong-problem-2026-09-23)).

Our read: expiry-day manipulation is a retail tax paid by anyone holding index options through settlement week, so fixing the settlement price is consumer work even if it reads as market plumbing. The Breakingviews critique lands, though — a regulator that spends its capital on closing-auction mechanics while bond-market depth withers is optimising the visible dial.

## Story 4: RBI's resilience sermon — boards own the next outage

Deputy Governor Rohit Jain used the SBI Banking & Economic Conclave on September 24 to set out what resilience means after a year of UPI outages and vendor failures: technology risk is now core bank-wide risk, owned by boards and senior management, not delegated to the IT department. Banks may outsource technology, but they retain full accountability for its security, reliability and recoverability — and in the AI era, governance must let innovation be adopted "with confidence" while system trustworthiness stays paramount. He framed the address as expectations-setting, not new regulation ([FinTech BizNews](https://www.fintechbiznews.com/govtregulators/10-requirements-in-area-of-technology-cyber-risk-management); [Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/rbi-urges-banks-to-treat-technology-failures-as-core-risk)).

The speech crystallises a doctrinal line RBI has been drawing all year. The June draft Model Risk Management guidance — with its AI kill switches and board oversight — remains draft, and Governor Sanjay Malhotra has been careful to call it guidance, not regulation ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-singapore-safr-agentic-ai-payments-india-gff)). But the accountability principle keeps hardening: banks "cannot outsource accountability for algorithmic decisions," and risk-based governance, oversight and customer explanations are the expectation ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-banks-accountability-algorithmic-decisions-rbi)). CERT-In's Digital Threat Report 2025-26 goes further, proposing human-in-the-loop controls for agentic AI actions above defined financial thresholds — the first concrete regulatory gesture at the moment when an AI agent, not a human, initiates the payment ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-singapore-safr-agentic-ai-payments-india-gff)).

RBI also quietly finalised market plumbing rules: on September 22 it issued the Novation of OTC Derivative Contracts Directions (RBI/2026-27/263), converting its July 2025 draft into final instructions under sections 45W and 45U of the RBI Act, applicable to novations from the date of issue ([Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-finalises-novation-rules-otc-derivative-contracts.html)). Unsexy, but it is the counterparty-risk hygiene that makes institutional markets survive individual failures — the same doctrine Jain preached, in circular form.

## The comment-season calendar — your to-do list as a consumer

This week settled the week's arguments; next month decides the rules. Four dates matter: **October 2** closes comments on RBI's money-mule KYC SOP — the 60-day freeze clock — and on extending SEBI's cyber framework to MII subsidiaries. **October 3** closes the expiry-day settlement price paper. **October 15** is the MDR go-live, with daily government monitoring. **October 25** closes IRDAI's insurance distribution draft. And within three months, the MDR steering committee must finalise the acceptance-infrastructure fund allocation with RBI. Every one of these drafts will be shaped by whoever bothers to write in. Last week's PIL taught us the courts are already engaged; the comment folders are where the consumer-favourable version of each rule gets written — or quietly diluted.
