# Fintech Deep Dive — Monday | September 28, 2026

Last week this column treated Global Fintech Fest follow-through as the story. This week the story has a deadline attached: October 15, the day the UPI MDR framework goes live, is now seventeen days out, and the Sept 21–28 window was when India's payment ecosystem stopped arguing about whether the fee exists and started building for it. Circular OC-237 turned out to be not a fee but a lookup table — sector, instrument, threshold, mandate-type — and every payment gateway, TPAP, acquirer, broker, and AMC in the country now has to encode it. Meanwhile, two quieter infrastructure stories shipped: NPCI's BHIM app grew a conversational AI support layer that pointedly cannot touch money, and Upstox imported US equity market access over a broker API instead of building it. The through-line: the rail's next phase is less about new rails, more about new logic running on the existing ones.

## 1. The MDR go-live is a lookup table, not a fee

The number everyone knows: from October 15, person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000 attract a 0.4% MDR, capped at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 and above. Person-to-person transfers, transactions up to ₹2,000, and small-merchant (P2PM) collections stay free — [NPCI's FAQ](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/personal-finance/upi-mdr-framework-how-does-it-compare-with-card-charges-and-credit-linked-upi-npci-clarifies-14030477.html) puts 95–96% of UPI merchant transactions below the threshold, and the chief executive reiterated on September 24 that consumer exposure is limited to roughly 10% of overall transaction value, with volumes expected to remain largely intact as the ecosystem reinvests the revenue [^1].

But the engineering reality hiding in [circular NPCI/UPI/OC-No.237/2026-27](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/companies/npci-introduces-0-4-mdr-upi-p2m-above-2-000-oct-15/51076768) is that "0.4%" is not one rate — it is the default cell of a matrix. Payments to mutual funds, securities, stockbrokers and dealers get a concessional 0.02%, capped at ₹300 (the flat cap binds above ₹15 lakh) [^2]. Specified insurance payments, along with sectors like railways, telecom, fuel and agricultural inputs, pay a flat ₹5 per transaction above ₹2,000 instead of the percentage [^3]. Recurring UPI AutoPay mandates are treated differently from one-time pulls — which is why analysts writing in Economic Times concluded that existing mutual fund SIPs on UPI mandates escape the charge while a one-time ₹1 lakh lump-sum investment via UPI generates a ₹20 MDR [^4]. And the funding instrument changes the answer too: RuPay credit cards on UPI and pre-sanctioned credit lines are carved out of this framework entirely and remain under existing card MDR rules. As Nivedita Bhardwaj of King Stubb & Kasiva put it, NPCI is effectively running [two parallel MDR regimes on the same UPI rail](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/personal-finance/upi-mdr-framework-how-does-it-compare-with-card-charges-and-credit-linked-upi-npci-clarifies-14030477.html), depending on the underlying instrument.

For the people who build checkouts, that translates into a concrete shipping list for the next seventeen days. The applicability engine must resolve, at payment time: payer instrument (savings account, RuPay credit line, wallet), payee category (small merchant vs regular vs sector-scheduled), threshold (₹2,000; ₹75,000 for the cap; ₹15 lakh for the capital-markets cap), and transaction type (one-time vs mandate). It must then surface the fee correctly to the merchant, invoice the 18% GST that sits on top — the subject of [NPCI's September 22 clarification](https://thefederal.com/category/business/gst-upi-mdr-wont-affect-small-merchands-npci-257338) after confusion about small-merchant burden — and, for platforms monetising the change, wire up TPAP-side and acquirer-side revenue accounting. [MobiKwik's September 16 exchange filing](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/companies/npci-introduces-0-4-mdr-upi-p2m-above-2-000-oct-15/51076768) is the template: it told investors it expects to earn on both its P2P-app share of eligible GMV and its merchant-acquiring GMV. The [Finance Ministry's separate clarification](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/no-us-pressure-in-upi-mdr-decision-npci-circular-offers-no-advantage-to-foreign-credit-cards-finmin/articleshow/134311342.cms) — that only RuPay credit cards may ride UPI for credit, and no foreign-network card gets an advantage — confirms the routing layer stays as-is: instrument whitelist, not open loop.

The honest engineering read: UPI spent a decade letting merchants treat "accept a payment" as one API call with one cost — zero. On October 15, that call grows four dimensions of state. Systems that hardcoded free will find out at reconciliation.

## 2. The fee lands in exchange filings: Ramco Cement, the ₹21,600 kirana, and the broker carve-out fight

The week's most under-covered artefact is a securities filing. On September 21, [Ramco Cement's outcomes filing to NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/RAMCOCEM_21092026112206_sesdiv26.pdf) carried a paragraph explaining that UPI has so far run on zero-MDR with ecosystem support from government incentives, and that the proposed framework would introduce charges on applicable transactions — making the level of the charge and the exemption thresholds material to businesses that lean on UPI for collections. Payments policy has officially become a disclosure item. Treasury and finance teams at collection-heavy companies are now doing the math that AICPDF, the traders' association, laid out in the same filing's summary: a kirana doing ₹10 lakh a month, half of it through UPI with 90% of those payments chargeable, would incur about ₹21,600 a year in MDR, roughly ₹25,500 with GST. The association's aggregate estimate for affected retail and distribution payments: ₹140–190 crore a month, ₹1,680–2,280 crore annually — against its estimate of ₹20,700 crore in total annual ecosystem operating cost, about 0.06% of the ₹328 lakh crore in annual UPI value.

The capital markets got their own brawl. The framework's 0.02% concessional rate for payments to stockbrokers, mutual funds and securities dealers sounds gentle, but brokers immediately flagged the edge cases: [Firstpost reported](https://www.firstpost.com/business/upi-mdr-capital-markets-brokers-mutual-funds-14047260.html) their complaint that a client merely funding a trading account — no trade executed — still triggers the charge, and that industry discussions have centred on a much lower per-transaction cap of ₹2–₹5 plus a higher free threshold than ₹2,000 [^5]. [Economic Times reported](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/topic/npci-upi-limit) the formal representation seeks a ₹5 cap, a zero-MDR threshold raised to ₹20,000, and — tellingly — reconsideration of the mandatory periodic settlement of unused client funds, a long-standing broker ask now bundled onto the MDR complaint. NSE's chief executive Ashish Chauhan, speaking after the exchange's listing, positioned the government as uninvolved: "no discussion on MDR with the government… it seems to be an issue between the broker and client. They will find a way out" [^6].

For builders in the capital-markets stack, the technical takeaway is the mandate-vs-one-time split. UPI AutoPay SIPs are outside the prescribed MDR; broker wallet top-ups and one-time lump-sums are inside it. Every investing app that lets users "add money" via UPI must now decide who eats a 0.02% fee on funding flows — and disclosures from at least one large AMC suggest the industry plans to absorb rather than pass through [^7]. Watch for that absorption to become selective the first time a fee line item shows up in a competitor's checkout.

## 3. BHIM MyUPI: the rail grows a conversational support layer — minus the money

While the agentic-payments protocol (UAP) sits parked at the RBI's door, NPCI quietly shipped the version of conversational finance it can defend. [BHIM MyUPI](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/bhim-myupi-9-upi-services-ai-assistant), launched by NPCI BHIM Services Limited at GFF and analysed in coverage landing September 23, packs nine UPI support services into an AI assistant inside the BHIM Payments App: transaction status checks, complaint raising with a reference number and in-app tracking, mandate management, de-linking a UPI number, and fraud-awareness help — available 24/7 in English, Hindi, Telugu, Tamil and Bengali, with more languages planned [^8].

The design line matters more than the feature list: payment actions still require authentication through the user's UPI PIN. The assistant can read, explain, draft, and route — but it cannot move money. That is the same doctrine NPCI's chairman articulated at GFF when UAP was pulled hours before its unveiling: agents may recommend; decision-making and execution stay separate [^9]. MyUPI is that principle applied to the least glamorous surface of the rail — support. And support is a rational place to start: the moments when UPI trust actually breaks are failed payments, mystery mandates, and scam messages, and those are exactly the workflows the assistant targets with complaint reference numbers and fraud-awareness answers rather than balance-checking party tricks.

The multilingual detail is the sleeper. A user who cannot navigate a settings menu but can ask a question in Tamil or Telugu now has a support channel that did not exist before. If NPCI extends the pattern — conversational dispute filing feeding the existing complaint API, conversational mandate review ahead of AutoPay debits — the assistant becomes the accessibility layer for the rail's governance features, not just its help desk. The thing to watch is whether complaint volumes shift from Twitter to the assistant, and whether resolution times move.

## 4. Upstox × Alpaca: US equities arrive as an API import

On September 23, [Upstox launched US stock and ETF trading](https://alpaca.markets/blog/upstox-launches-us-equities-trading-through-partnership-with-alpaca) for its 20 million-plus customers, with fractional investing from $1 (roughly ₹95). The notable part for this column is what Upstox did *not* build: no US brokerage entity, no clearing stack, no custody rails. The entire backend — brokerage, clearing, and custody — is [Alpaca's Broker API](https://www.tipranks.com/news/private-companies/alpaca-backed-upstox-launches-u-s-stock-access-for-indian-investors), the same "agent-first" infrastructure the company rents to fintechs across dozens of markets.

The enabling move happened earlier: in April 2026, Alpaca closed its acquisition of Zincmoney, an IFSCA-regulated broker-dealer operating from GIFT City, giving it India-adjacent licences, IFSC-listed products, and payment infrastructure to anchor these partnerships. That is the pattern worth naming — cross-border retail investing is consolidating into B2B2C API rails, where an Indian broker differentiates on interface, onboarding, and price while clearing and custody come from a commodity API. It rhymes with what UPI itself did to payments: the differentiated part moved up-stack, and the plumbing became rentable.

Two technical footnotes. First, fractional-from-$1 implies notional trading and internal aggregation on the API provider's side — a materially different order-management problem than whole-share routing, and one where the API vendor's execution quality becomes the Indian broker's user experience. Second, the "agent-first" label on the clearing infrastructure is not marketing noise in a week when India is actively designing agent-permission primitives for money movement. Brokerage APIs designed with programmatic clients in mind — entitlements, sub-accounts, granular permissions — are the same shape as the delegated-payment primitives UPI Circle standardized. When agentic investing arrives in India, it will likely arrive through exactly these rented rails.

## The week in one line

The rail is not growing new tracks this month; it is growing a pricing engine, a conversational front desk, and rented global access — and October 15 is the date the first of those stops being optional.
