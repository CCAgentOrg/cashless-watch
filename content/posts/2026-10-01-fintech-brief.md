# Fintech Brief — October 01, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### 1. Traders call off 'No UPI Day' after meeting Sitharaman — but October 15 stands

The merchant revolt against UPI's new Merchant Discount Rate has blinked first — for now. [Leading traders' bodies withdrew their call to observe October 2 as 'No UPI Day' after a meeting with Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman on Wednesday](https://www.socialnews.xyz/2026/09/30/traders-withdraw-no-upi-day-protest-after-meeting-fm-sitharaman), days after [AIM — the All India Mobile Retailers Association — had urged policymakers and NPCI to scrap the 0.4% MDR on larger UPI merchant payments](https://m.rediff.com/amp/business/report/mobile-retailers-protest-against-upi-mdr-charges/20260930.htm). The legal route is exhausted too: [the Supreme Court declined to stay the framework on September 28 in Anjan Datta v. Union of India](https://www.indianpaycalculator.in/govt-news/upi-mdr-supreme-court-no-stay-october-15-2026), leaving the October 15 start date intact. The scale of what's actually being charged matters: [NPCI says payments of ₹2,000 and under — more than 95% of UPI merchant transaction volume — stay free, merchants with up to ₹1 lakh a month in receipts owe nothing, and the fee cannot be passed to the customer](https://www.latestly.com/india/news/traders-call-off-no-upi-day-what-changed-after-meeting-fm-nirmala-sitharaman-7627247.html). What the protests actually changed is unclear — no rollback was announced after the meeting — but [Business Standard reports UPI usage has held steady through September despite the backlash](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/upi-usage-holds-steady-in-september-despite-backlash-over-new-mdr-norms-126093001390_1.html). Consumer take: your P2P transfers and small-ticket payments stay free, but [the 0.4% fee on planned purchases — appliances, electronics, fees, travel — is capped at ₹300 per transaction from October 15](https://m.economictimes.com/wealth/save/fee-on-upi-p2m-payments-how-this-small-0-4-fee-can-take-a-bigger-bite-out-of-your-money/amp_articleshow/134585025.cms), and the real watch-item is whether any of it migrates into sticker prices despite the pass-through ban.

### 2. Moneyview lists today after a 101.87x rush; StockGro's parent takes the confidential IPO route

A fintech listing day and an IPO filing in the same news cycle. [Moneyview's ₹1,092-crore IPO closed subscribed 101.87x overall — QIBs at 230.54x, NIIs at 120.37x, retail at 20.41x — and the stock lists on NSE and BSE today](https://www.ndtvprofit.com/markets/stock-market-today-all-you-need-to-know-before-going-into-trade-on-oct-1-12122081), after [the issue priced at ₹32–34 a share and finished at 98.46x when subscriptions closed](https://ipowatch.in/moneyview-ipo-gmp-grey-market-premium). Meanwhile [AssetGro Fintech, parent of stock research platform StockGro, has filed confidential pre-filed IPO papers with SEBI to raise ₹2,000–2,500 crore — a fresh issue of about ₹800 crore plus an offer for sale](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/stockgro-parent-assetgro-fintech-files-confidential-pre-filed-ipo-papers-with-sebi/article71523102.ece), with [the pre-filed DRHP submitted on September 26](https://www.siliconindia.com/news/startups/stockgro-parent-files-confidential-ipo-papers-for-rs-2500-crore-nid-242504-cid-19.html). The confidential route keeps draft offer documents out of public view until SEBI's review advances — a growing habit among Indian issuers. Consumer note: StockGro sells stock advice and trade ideas from SEBI-registered analysts to retail users; when the draft goes public, the disclosures worth reading are revenue mix (advisory subscriptions vs. anything else) and what the OFS sellers are cashing out.

### 3. October 1 arrives: RBI's bulk-FD transparency is live, but international card reporting is a grey area

Two RBI changes kicked in today. The clean one: [banks must now disclose bulk fixed-deposit rates — deposits of ₹3 crore and above — on their websites daily by 10 am, announced in advance, with identical rates for similar bulk deposits; the rule covers commercial banks, small finance banks, RRBs, payment banks and urban co-operative banks](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/wealth/invest/fd-interest-rate-rules-change-from-october-1-what-depositors-need-to-know/fd-interest-rate-rules-change-from-october-1-2026/slideshow/134583814.cms). Retail depositors are untouched — this is about pricing discipline on large institutional money. The messy one: [new reporting requirements for international credit card transactions take effect October 1, but the framework doesn't clearly say how individuals are supposed to report routine foreign spends — subscriptions to Claude, The New Yorker or Bandcamp could land in a compliance grey zone since card spends fall under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme's reporting ambit](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/stocks/news/claude-new-yorker-bandcamp-rbi-rules-leave-international-card-payments-in-grey-area/articleshow/134605646.cms). Until the banks and the regulator clear the air, the practical advice is mundane: [October's other changes — SBI's free ATM limit dropping to five for salary accounts, revised NPS charges, LPG Aadhaar-KYC — are the ones with unambiguous mechanics](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/5-money-rule-changes-from-october-2026-bulk-fd-rates-upi-mdr-starts-above-rs-2000-sbi-atm-free-limit-falls-to-5-lpg-aadhaar-kyc-new-nps-charges/articleshow/134587809.cms), and nobody should panic-pre-report a $20 subscription.
