# Fintech Deep Dive — Thursday | October 01, 2026

## The week the world's wallets arrived — and India's payment stack left home

Cross-border fintech runs on two currents: money and infrastructure moving in, and Indian-built rails moving out. This week both flowed hard. Apple Pay went live in India after a decade of waiting, picking a bank partner and card networks but pointedly skipping UPI. The Reserve Bank's cross-border card authentication deadline landed today, changing what happens when an Indian card is used on a foreign website. Trinidad and Tobago moved to become the first Caribbean nation to adopt UPI's technology. In the Gulf, Checkout.com wired the UAE's Jaywan scheme — built on India's own RuPay stack — into global acquiring. And Bharti Airtel's African money arm set course for a London listing at up to $9 billion. Five stories, one pattern: the borders in payments are being redrawn in both directions at once.

## Story 1: Apple Pay lands in India — cards first, UPI deliberately last

On Wednesday (September 30), Apple launched Apple Pay in India, with Axis Bank as its first banking partner and support initially limited to Axis-issued Visa and Mastercard credit cards ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/apple-pay-launches-india-with-axis-bank-partnership-2026-09-30); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/technology/apple-pay-goes-live-in-india-with-axis-bank-issued-visa-and-mastercard-cards/article71526771.ece)). Customers add eligible cards to Apple Wallet and pay in stores, apps and online with iPhone, iPad or Apple Watch, authenticated by Face ID, Touch ID or passcode; Mac support is promised later. Axis is India's fourth-largest credit card issuer with 16.3 million cards outstanding as of August — real scale, but a fraction of the 124 million cards nationwide ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/apple-pay-launches-india-with-axis-bank-partnership-2026-09-30)). Apple has reportedly held talks with HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank without yet agreeing commercial terms ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/apple-pay-launches-india-with-axis-bank-partnership-2026-09-30); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/apple-pay-set-to-launch-in-india-with-axis-bank-today-sources-say)).

The deliberate omission is the story within the story. UPI is excluded from the launch: wiring it in requires NPCI approval and a sponsor bank, and reports since August have been consistent that UPI support will come later, if at all in the first phase ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/technology/apple-pay-could-launch-in-india-by-october-without-upi-support-report-1978120); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/apple-pay-in-india)). That means Apple is entering the world's largest real-time payments market through its smallest door. UPI accounts for roughly 84% of India's digital payment volumes by transaction count, and NFC contactless acceptance — Apple Pay's native habitat — remains thin outside large retail chains ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/apple-pay-launches-india-with-axis-bank-partnership-2026-09-30); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/technology/apple-pay-launches-in-india-initially-with-axis-bank-card-support-heres-how-to-set-it-up-and-use-4150494)). Apple is effectively betting that premium iPhone users' credit-card spend at NFC terminals is a monetisable niche: TechCrunch reports Apple will earn a transaction fee on the flow, small in share but meaningful against its growing India installed base, where it held 28% of smartphone market value in 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/apple-pay-set-to-launch-in-india-with-axis-bank-today-sources-say)).

For consumers, the near-term experience is narrow but real: tokenised cards (the actual number is never stored on device or Apple servers), tap-to-pay without PIN or OTP at enabled terminals, and in-app/online checkout ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/technology/apple-pay-goes-live-in-india-with-axis-bank-issued-visa-and-mastercard-cards/article71526771.ece)). The consumer-rights questions we will be watching: which merchants get the premium NFC positions, whether any fees get pushed into prices, and whether UPI integration — the only version of Apple Pay most Indians could actually use daily — ever arrives. A decade of rumours ended; the useful question now is whether the product stays a status accessory or becomes infrastructure.

## Story 2: RBI's cross-border card deadline lands today — your foreign checkouts are about to change

Today (October 1, 2026) is the compliance deadline in the Reserve Bank of India's (Authentication Mechanisms for Digital Payment Transactions) Directions, 2025 for the cross-border provisions: card issuers must have in place a mechanism to validate non-recurring, cross-border card-not-present (CNP) transactions when an overseas merchant or acquirer requests authentication, plus a risk-based mechanism covering all cross-border CNP transactions, and must register their Bank Identification Numbers (BINs) with the card networks ([RBI via Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-issues-rules-digital-payment-authentication.html); [Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/wealth/save/rbi-issues-directions-for-digital-payment-transaction-authentication-mechanism/articleshow/124115819.cms)).

The directions, issued on September 25, 2025, made two-factor authentication with at least one dynamic factor the floor for domestic digital payments from April 1, 2026. But the cross-border half has a quieter, equally consequential purpose: today, when you pay a foreign website with an Indian card, whether you are challenged with an additional factor depends largely on the merchant's side. Under the new framework, overseas merchants and acquirers gain a standard way to request Indian-style additional authentication for one-time (non-recurring) international payments on Indian-issued cards, and issuers must be able to respond — with risk-based controls across the whole cross-border CNP book ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-rbi-two-factor-authentication-digital-payments); [ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/rbi-mandates-two-step-authentication-for-digital-payments-by-2026/124113771)). Notably, the directions also repeal the December 2016 relaxation that waived additional authentication for small CNP payments up to ₹2,000, closing a decade-old gap ([ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/ecorpit-rbi-afa-authentication-retrofit-service-india-2026)).

Why this belongs on the cross-border beat: Indian cards have long had an asymmetry abroad — no AFA was demanded on many foreign checkouts, which made paying overseas frictionless but also meant fraud disputes hung on weaker evidence. The directions put issuers on the hook explicitly: they are responsible for the integrity of authentication and must compensate customers for losses arising from non-compliance ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/rbi-mandates-two-step-authentication-for-digital-payments-by-2026/124113771)). Expect the practical effects to show up over the coming weeks: some international merchants will start triggering OTPs for Indian cards, some previously "just worked" flows may decline until BIN registration and routing are sorted, and recurring foreign subscriptions (SaaS, streaming) stay largely outside the change since the mandate targets non-recurring transactions ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/09/223-rbi-two-factor-authentication-digital-payments)). The consumer test is whether issuers implement this as safety or as lazy blanket-declining — the RBI has left the burden of proof with them.

## Story 3: Trinidad and Tobago moves to bring UPI to the Caribbean

Trinidad and Tobago is working to implement India's UPI under an agreement between NPCI International (NIPL) and the country's Ministry of Digital Transformation, and would become the first Caribbean nation to adopt the technology, the country's High Commissioner to India Chandradath Singh said in remarks reported this week ([India TV News](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/world/upi-goes-global-again-as-trinidad-and-tobago-becomes-first-caribbean-nation-to-adopt-india-s-online-payment-system-2026-09-29-1055556); [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/world/trinidad-and-tobago-set-to-become-first-caribbean-nation-to-adopt-indias-upi)). The underlying agreement dates to 2024; the rollout is now underway, with the planned platform supporting person-to-person and person-to-merchant payments, interoperability among financial institutions, and technical expertise and capacity building from the Indian side — with Trinidad and Tobago retaining ownership of its payment platform ([India TV News](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/world/upi-goes-global-again-as-trinidad-and-tobago-becomes-first-caribbean-nation-to-adopt-india-s-online-payment-system-2026-09-29-1055556)).

This is the "UPI as technology export" model in its purest form. Unlike corridor launches where UPI apps gain acceptance abroad for Indian travellers, this is NIPL supplying the reference architecture for a sovereign national payments system — the same playbook behind its work in Namibia and the broader set of countries building UPI-like rails. The diaspora logic is unusually strong here: people of Indian origin are roughly 35–40% of Trinidad and Tobago's population, the two countries signed six MoUs during Prime Minister Modi's visit, and the payment platform is explicitly framed as part of a wider digital-public-infrastructure partnership ([Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/world/trinidad-and-tobago-set-to-become-first-caribbean-nation-to-adopt-indias-upi); [India TV News](https://www.indiatvnews.com/news/world/upi-goes-global-again-as-trinidad-and-tobago-becomes-first-caribbean-nation-to-adopt-india-s-online-payment-system-2026-09-29-1055556)).

Two caveats keep this honest. First, reported remarks describe active implementation, not an operational launch — the gap between agreement and go-live has historically run into years (Bhutan took over three years from MoU to live QR payments). Second, ownership terms matter: T&T keeping platform ownership is the right consumer-protective architecture, but the governance details — data residency, dispute rules, pricing caps — will decide whether this is DPI cooperation or vendor lock-in with extra steps. For now, note the geography: NIPL's map now has a Western Hemisphere deployment in progress beyond the US-focused PayPal World route.

## Story 4: Checkout.com wires Jaywan into global acquiring — the RuPay descendant goes to work

On September 28, Checkout.com announced it has added Jaywan — the UAE's domestic card scheme — to its UAE acquiring platform, letting merchants accept Jaywan cards through local connectivity with automatic recognition and routing behind a single integration ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/checkoutcom-adds-jaywan-domestic-card-scheme-acceptance-in-the-uae); [Disrupts](https://disrupts.disruptsmedia.com/fintech/checkoutcom-adds-jaywan-uae-acquiring-platform)). The integration helps merchants comply with the UAE's mandate on domestic scheme acceptance without changing their checkout setups. Checkout.com was the first global payments provider to obtain a UAE acquiring licence in 2023, and holds in-principle approval for a Stored Value Facilities licence — its regional merchants include SHEIN, talabat, Tamara and Careem ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/checkoutcom-adds-jaywan-domestic-card-scheme-acceptance-in-the-uae)).

The India connection runs through the scheme's DNA: Jaywan is operated by Al Etihad Payments, a subsidiary of the UAE central bank, and was built on the card-scheme technology stack licensed from NPCI International — making it, functionally, RuPay's Gulf descendant ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/checkoutcom-adds-jaywan-domestic-card-scheme-acceptance-in-the-uae)). Its acceptance footprint has been compounding fast: Emirates began accepting Jaywan for flight bookings on September 16, Etihad became the first airline with full-network Jaywan checkout, and international acceptance has expanded under a new arrangement with UnionPay International ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/al-etihad-payments); [Digiflow](https://digiflow.ae/fintech/uae-jaywan-card-explained)).

Read alongside PhonePe's UAE in-principle approvals two weeks ago, the Gulf is quietly becoming the proving ground for every India-built payment asset: UPI acceptance for travellers, a locally licensed PhonePe in progress, and a RuPay-derived card scheme embedded into the acquiring stacks of global processors. The consumer angle is straightforward — every acquirer that auto-routes Jaywan domestically instead of forcing it onto international rails lowers cost and keeps transactions (and data) onshore, per the UAE's own digital-sovereignty agenda ([Disrupts](https://disrupts.disruptsmedia.com/fintech/checkoutcom-adds-jaywan-uae-acquiring-platform)). The quieter question for Indian policymakers: when RuPay's stack succeeds abroad as someone else's national scheme, India earns strategic influence but not the interchange, data or brand. That trade is worth making consciously, not by default.

## Story 5: Airtel Money points at London — an Indian group's African fintech heads to market

Airtel Money, the mobile-money arm of Bharti Airtel's Airtel Africa, is preparing a London Stock Exchange listing targeting a valuation of $8–9 billion, with existing investors expected to sell at least $800 million of shares and the company providing further offer details — share count and pricing — in October ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/fintech/silver-lining-for-uk-equity-market-airtel-money-targets-usd-9-billion-london-ipo); [tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/09/24/airtel-money-will-list-in-london-and-the-ipo-raises-no-new-money-for-the-business)). Notably, the IPO raises no new money for the business — it is an exit liquidity event for early backers, not growth capital. The listing had been postponed from the first half of 2026 amid market volatility; earlier ambitions of raising $1.5–2 billion were scaled back after investor feedback ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/fintech/silver-lining-for-uk-equity-market-airtel-money-targets-usd-9-billion-london-ipo)).

The disclosed shape of the listed entity is unusual and worth consumer attention: Airtel Money plans to pay out at least 80% of net profit as dividends twice a year, is guiding to mid-20s-percent constant-currency revenue growth for the year to March 2027, and expects EBITDA margin to fall by up to two percentage points because it has revised its agreements with other Airtel Africa companies ([tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/09/24/airtel-money-will-list-in-london-and-the-ipo-raises-no-new-money-for-the-business)). Mastercard bought 25% of the business for $100 million in 2024 and will mark a large gain if the valuation holds. That margin hit from re-priced related-party agreements is, on paper, good news for the mobile-money customers whose fees fund those margins — the thin-margin reality of African mobile money has long been that the telecom parent's economics and the consumer's interests do not always align.

Why this belongs on an Indian fintech page: the group is Indian, the listing is in London, the customers are in fourteen African markets — the full cross-border stack in one corporate structure. It is also a reminder that India's biggest fintech exports are not always UPI: sometimes they are balance sheets and operating models. If the listing prices near target, expect a fresh wave of telecom-led money businesses elsewhere to cite it as their template.

## The week in one line

Apple Pay arrived to serve India's credit-card minority, the RBI moved to make Indian cards safer at foreign checkouts, and the UAE and the Caribbean absorbed Indian-built payment infrastructure — the cross-border ledger for the week balances neatly: what India imports is competition and consumer choice; what it exports is rails.
