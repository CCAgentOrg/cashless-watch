# Fintech Brief — October 4, 2026

## Today's Top Stories
1. RBI Governor warns that financial stability can hide accumulating risk.
2. A Saturday funding roundup puts Indian startup capital at $411 million; fintech deals remain a smaller slice.
3. CoinSwitch reports adjusted-EBITDA profitability; PayMe says September loan disbursals crossed ₹200 crore.

**Coverage window:** 8:00 AM IST, October 3 to 8:00 AM IST, October 4. Sunday’s official and market news flow was thin. The RBI’s fresh policy signal was Governor Malhotra’s speech, not a new rule. ### 1. RBI Governor: resilience today is no guarantee against tomorrow’s shock

In a special address at the Fifth Kautilya Economic Conclave on October 3, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said India’s banks, financial institutions, governments and households have strong balance sheets, but warned against treating a long stretch of stability as immunity. Extended calm can itself encourage risk-taking and leverage. He identified five vulnerabilities in the global system: high public debt, stretched asset valuations—especially around AI—rising leverage outside banks, private credit, and cyber threats amplified by AI. He said he saw no imminent signs of stress; the point was preparedness, not a prediction of crisis. The [RBI’s full speech](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1582) sets out the official view, while [Business Standard’s report](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/economy/news/rbi-sanjay-malhotra-financial-stability-risks-kautilya-conclave-126100300415_1.html) summarises the risk list.

For fintech, the warning is about system connections: a technology or cyber failure can travel through banks, non-bank lenders and payment infrastructure rather than stay within one firm. Malhotra called for resilient institutions, better data, deeper markets, credible safety nets, effective resolution tools, and proactive but proportionate supervision. This was a stability speech, not a circular or a new compliance deadline for fintechs. Consumers should read it as a reminder that convenience depends on dependable providers, recovery plans and clear recourse when a digital service fails.

### 2. Startup funding rebounds in the week’s tally, but the biggest cheques went elsewhere

Entrackr’s [weekly funding report, published October 3 at 10:36 AM IST](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-sep-28-oct-3-12618694), counted $411.46 million raised by 24 Indian startups during September 28–October 3, against about $188.49 million in the preceding week. Five growth-stage deals accounted for $357 million. Simple Energy’s $182 million Series C and SiMa.ai’s $150 million round made up most of that amount, so the headline rebound was driven chiefly by electric mobility and AI—not fintech. The report does not give valuations for these rounds.

The fintech-specific funding item in the roundup was Seeds Fincap securing more than ₹100 crore in Series B funding. Entrackr did not disclose a valuation. That round was reported earlier in the week, so it is week-to-date context rather than a new Sunday deal. The distinction matters: a large ecosystem total can look like a fintech funding boom even when the largest rounds are in unrelated sectors. The roundup describes funding activity across fintech alongside healthtech, AI, e-commerce and other sectors, but does not establish a comparable fintech-only total.

### 3. Two operating signals: crypto-platform margins and digital-credit growth

The same Saturday report says CoinSwitch’s FY26 revenue rose 150% to ₹324.19 crore and adjusted EBITDA reached ₹86.38 crore, versus ₹1.65 crore in FY25. This is adjusted EBITDA profitability, not a claim of positive statutory net profit; operating costs still rose 37%. The figures suggest stronger operating leverage, but the adjustment basis and cash generation remain important checks for investors.

For lending, Entrackr reports PayMe crossed ₹200 crore in monthly loan disbursals in September, nearly twice the ₹100-crore mark it had reached earlier this year. It says PayMe disbursed more than ₹1,300 crore in FY26 and is targeting over ₹2,000 crore in FY27, with a ₹500-crore monthly run-rate goal by year-end. Those are company growth figures and targets, not evidence on repayment quality or borrower outcomes. For consumers, the useful follow-up is whether rapid disbursal growth is matched by transparent pricing, suitable underwriting and effective complaint handling.
