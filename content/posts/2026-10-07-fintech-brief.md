### 1. NBFC retail lending grew faster; RBI rate decision still ahead

The RBI's October 6 [release on NBFC credit deployment](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63739) reports year-on-year credit growth at 15.8% in August, up from 10.0% a year earlier. Retail lending accelerated to 22.0% from 13.6%, and agriculture and allied credit to 17.4% from 5.1%; services slowed to 16.2% from 24.0%. The data cover major NBFCs and housing finance companies—not app-based lending alone—and say nothing about repayment quality.

For digital-credit watchers, retail loan demand is expanding within this sample while services lending has cooled. Separately, [Moneycontrol's pre-decision report](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/india-set-for-first-rate-hike-in-almost-4-years-policy-guide-14045276.html) said 34 of 40 Bloomberg-surveyed economists expected a 25-basis-point repo increase to 5.50%. That was a forecast, not an RBI signal; no policy decision had been announced by the 8:00 AM cutoff.

### 2. SEBI may revise how derivatives settlement prices are set

On October 6, [Moneycontrol reported](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/sebi-likely-to-partly-reverse-cas-rules-bse-nse-other-capital-market-stocks-rise-up-to-3-14045416.html), citing Reuters sources, that SEBI may pause use of the closing auction session (CAS) for derivatives settlement for at least a year. The reported alternative is a volume-weighted average price over the final 30 minutes; closing auctions would remain for less-liquid cash-market shares. SEBI reportedly received 20,000 consultation suggestions.

This is a report, not a SEBI order. If adopted, separate cash-close and derivative-settlement prices could alter expiry-day basis risk. Investors should wait for a formal notice before treating settlement rules as changed.

### 3. UPI MDR: the October 15 framework is narrower than a blanket fee

A [Mint explainer published October 6](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/upi-mdr-charges-when-do-the-provisions-take-effect-how-does-it-compare-with-international-payment-systems-11791275551392.html) sets out the October 15 start date. The Finance Ministry's [September 15 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2310586) says a 0.4% MDR applies only to specified person-to-merchant (P2M) UPI payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 or more. P2P, merchant payments up to ₹2,000 and eligible small merchants remain at zero MDR; about 96% of P2M transactions are unaffected. NPCI's [MDR FAQ](https://www.npci.org.in/uploads/FA_Qs_Merchant_Discount_Rate_MDR_on_Select_UPI_P2_M_Transactions_58dba1d39e.pdf) lists lower rates for specified rail, telecom, insurance, fuel and agricultural-input payments (₹5) and securities-related payments (0.02%, capped at ₹300).

The MDR is borne within the merchant-side payment ecosystem, not charged directly to UPI users. NPCI says merchants cannot pass the fee on to customers, and the government says UPI apps cannot add platform or hidden charges. The FAQ says consumers pay only the posted price.

### 4. Oolka's proposed round signals investor interest, not a closed deal

[Moneycontrol's Oolka report, updated at 9:02 AM IST on October 6](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/fintech-startup-oolka-in-talks-to-raise-45-million-from-bessemer-prosus-and-others-14045092.html), says the AI credit-management startup is negotiating a $45 million (about ₹430 crore) round. Sources cited by the outlet put Bessemer's possible investment at $20 million, Prosus at $10 million and existing backers at about $15 million. The proposed valuation is around $200 million (₹1,900 crore), versus about $90 million in April. These are negotiations, not a closed round; Oolka, Bessemer and Prosus did not respond. Moneycontrol says the startup had previously raised about $21 million, citing Tracxn.

Investor interest is not evidence that an AI credit product improves outcomes; users should watch data use, accuracy and the terms of linked credit offers.
