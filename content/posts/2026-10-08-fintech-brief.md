# Fintech Brief — October 8, 2026

## Today's Top Stories
1. RBI raises the repo rate 25 basis points and announces account-data interoperability measures.
2. SEBI orders colour-coded credit-risk disclosures for debt securities.
3. NPCI is reportedly weighing a January start for UPI MDR; no deferral is official yet.
4. Navi is in reported talks for $40 million at a $1.4–1.5 billion valuation.

**Coverage window:** 8:00 AM IST, October 7 to 8:00 AM IST, October 8. The RBI decision and SEBI circular are official actions. The UPI timing change and Navi round remain reported negotiations, not completed decisions or deals.

---

### 1. RBI tightens policy as it opens a path to more portable financial data

The RBI’s Monetary Policy Committee unanimously raised the policy repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% after its October 5–7 meeting, and changed its stance to “calibrated tightening.” The central bank projected FY27 CPI inflation at 5.2%, including 6.0% in the December quarter and 5.7% in the March quarter; August inflation had risen to 4.8% from 4.5% in July. The RBI said rate cuts are off the table in the near term, with future action depending on the inflation and growth outlook. Its [policy resolution](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63742) says two MPC members favoured retaining a neutral stance.

For fintech lenders, tighter rates can raise funding costs, but this is not an automatic 25-basis-point change to every customer’s loan. Floating-rate borrowers should check their lender’s benchmark and reset schedule; deposit rates also depend on each bank’s decisions.

In a separate part of the same policy package, the RBI set a December 31, 2026 target for interoperability among NBFC Account Aggregators, so customers can share financial information through an AA of their choice. It also plans to let depositories include bank-deposit information in demat holders’ Consolidated Account Statements through AAs. That is a useful step toward a more complete financial overview; it does not remove the need to understand and control each data-sharing consent. ([RBI development and regulatory statement](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63743))

### 2. SEBI adds a visual credit-risk cue to debt offers and bond platforms

SEBI’s October 7 circular makes a colour-coded “Credit Risk-o-Meter” mandatory for listed and proposed-to-be-listed non-convertible securities, commercial paper, securitised debt, security receipts and structured or market-linked debentures. It must appear in offer documents, private-placement memoranda, issuer and online bond platform advertisements, and those platforms’ web and mobile interfaces. The meter maps existing credit ratings into six visual risk levels; the requirement takes effect 45 days after issuance. ([SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2026/introduction-of-credit-risk-o-meter-as-an-additional-disclosure-mechanism-for-debt-securities_105081.html))

This should make a rating easier to notice, not turn it into a safety guarantee. SEBI’s own circular says the meter represents credit risk only—not market or liquidity risk—and is not investment advice. Investors still need to read the rating, security terms and redemption conditions, especially for unsecured or perpetual bonds.

### 3. UPI MDR may move to January—but the published date is still October 15

A [Business Standard report](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/festival-breather-upi-merchant-fee-may-be-deferred-till-january-1-126100800036_1.html), updated shortly after midnight, says NPCI’s UPI and Services Steering Committee is considering deferring the MDR start from October 15 to January 1, 2027. Sources also told the paper that the committee is discussing a broader small-merchant exemption, potentially for businesses with annual turnover up to ₹40 lakh. These are proposals: the report says a final decision is expected in the coming days, and NPCI had not responded by publication. The [NPCI MDR FAQ](https://www.npci.org.in/uploads/FA_Qs_Merchant_Discount_Rate_MDR_on_Select_UPI_P2_M_Transactions_58dba1d39e.pdf) still states October 15 as the start date for the finalized framework, including 0.4% on specified merchant payments above ₹2,000. Until NPCI publishes a change, merchants and payment firms should plan against the stated date—not treat a possible postponement as settled.

### 4. Navi’s next funding round is proposed, not closed

Navi is in advanced talks to raise about $40 million (roughly ₹380 crore) from Elevation, two months after Prosus invested $100 million, [Moneycontrol reported](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/sachin-bansal-s-navi-in-talks-to-raise-40-million-from-elevation-two-months-after-prosus-100-million-round-14046812.html) on October 7. People cited by the publication put the proposed round’s valuation at $1.4–1.5 billion. Navi is also reported to be preparing a roughly $300 million IPO that could value the company near $2 billion; those figures are not the terms of a completed listing or this private round. Navi and Elevation had not responded to Moneycontrol’s queries. The distinction matters: reported fundraising interest is a signal, not cash received or proof of future performance.
