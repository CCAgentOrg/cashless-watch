# Fintech Brief — October 9, 2026

## Today's Top Stories
1. RBI restricts withdrawals at two cooperative banks and penalises a third over delayed KYC uploads.
2. NPCI's UPI Steering Committee is due to discuss a possible MDR delay today; no revised date is official yet.
3. NPCI chief Dilip Asbe joins SWIFT's supervisory board, a governance milestone rather than a new payment link.
4. NABVENTURES closes ₹450 crore of its planned ₹1,500 crore rural and agri-focused fund.

**Coverage window:** 8:00 AM IST, October 8 to 8:00 AM IST, October 9. RBI and NABVENTURES actions are published developments; the UPI MDR delay remains a reported proposal. SEBI's official circular index showed no new October 8 circular; its latest entries were dated October 7.

---

### 1. RBI's cooperative-bank actions have direct consequences for depositors

From the close of business on October 8, the RBI barred both Sri Mahatma Basaveshwar Co-operative Bank in Afzalpur and The Tiruvalla Urban Co-operative Bank in Kerala from allowing withdrawals from savings, current or other depositor accounts. The directions also restrict fresh deposits, lending and other activity without RBI approval, and run for six months subject to review. These are serious restrictions, but the RBI says they do not by themselves cancel either bank's licence. Eligible depositors may claim DICGC insurance up to ₹5 lakh in the same capacity and right, after submitting willingness and completing verification; it is not an automatic release of an entire balance. Customers at either bank should contact its officials and follow the DICGC claim process rather than assume funds are immediately accessible. ([Afzalpur order](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63764); [Tiruvalla order](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63763))

Separately, RBI imposed a ₹5 lakh penalty on The Monghyr-Jamui Central Co-operative Bank for failing to upload customer KYC records to the Central KYC Records Registry within the prescribed timeline. RBI said this supervisory penalty does not determine the validity of customer transactions. ([RBI penalty notice](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63765))

### 2. UPI MDR postponement is still a proposal, not a policy change

NPCI's published framework sets October 15 as the start date for a 0.4% merchant discount rate on specified person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000. That is a merchant-side processing fee, not an automatic 0.4% surcharge to the customer. On October 8, Moneycontrol reported that NPCI had called the UPI Steering Committee to consider pushing implementation to January 2027 after merchants and payment firms sought a delay. The committee was expected to meet on October 9, with an announcement anticipated later in the day; at this brief's 8:00 AM cutoff, no decision had been published. ([Moneycontrol report](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/npci-likely-to-announce-upi-mdr-postponement-tomorrow-afternoon-after-steering-committee-meeting-article-14048016.html); [NPCI's published MDR FAQ](https://www.npci.org.in/uploads/FA_Qs_Merchant_Discount_Rate_MDR_on_Select_UPI_P2_M_Transactions_58dba1d39e.pdf))

Merchants and payment providers should plan against the published October 15 date until NPCI issues a formal revision. A possible festive-season reprieve is not the same as cancellation of the fee framework, and customers should be wary of any attempt to present the reported delay as an immediate change to their UPI terms.

### 3. NPCI's chief joins SWIFT's supervisory board

NPCI managing director and CEO Dilip Asbe was appointed an independent director to SWIFT's 15-member supervisory board, Business Standard reported on October 8. The board oversees strategy for the global financial messaging network, so the appointment gives India's domestic payments operator a place in an international infrastructure-governance forum. It does not connect UPI to SWIFT or change how consumers make cross-border payments today; those require separate arrangements and implementation. ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/banking/npci-chief-asbe-joins-swift-s-supervisory-board-as-independent-director-126100801407_1.html))

### 4. NABVENTURES brings a larger capital pool into rural technology

NABVENTURES, NABARD's venture-capital arm, announced a ₹450 crore first close for its second fund, which is targeting ₹1,500 crore including a greenshoe option. NABARD is the anchor investor. The fund plans to invest across agritech, food and agribusiness, rural fintech, climate-smart solutions, and supply-chain and logistics businesses serving rural markets. The first close is capital available to the fund, not ₹450 crore already invested in one startup; no company-level valuation was reported. For fintech, the relevance is the dedicated pool for rural financial inclusion and the agriculture value chain, where distribution and borrower income seasonality remain hard constraints. ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/nabventures-marks-first-close-of-%E2%82%B91500-cr-fund-at-%E2%82%B9450-cr/))
