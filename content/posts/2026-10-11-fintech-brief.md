### 1. RBI tightens INR-derivative controls; oil-company dollar support stays narrow

Two RBI directions dated October 10 tighten the rules for INR-linked foreign-exchange derivatives. For hedging contracted exposures, the threshold for transacting without separately establishing the underlying exposure falls from USD 100 million to USD 5 million across all authorised dealers. The comparable threshold for exchange-traded INR currency derivatives also falls to USD 5 million across recognised exchanges. These are aggregate thresholds, not an allowance that can be claimed anew at each bank or exchange. Dealers must also stop users from rebooking INR-linked contracts cancelled after the directions take effect; rolling an eligible contract over at maturity remains permitted. Clients hedging contracted exposures must confirm that the same exposure is not hedged with another dealer. ([RBI’s summary](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63783); [Circular 25](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13737&Mode=0))

The more consequential liquidity detail is the new Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve. For qualifying INR-linked derivative contracts above USD 2 million used to hedge a current-account exposure where the client buys foreign currency against rupees, the authorised dealer must keep cash with RBI equal to 20% of the contract’s rupee-equivalent notional. The circular places that reserve obligation on the dealer, not directly on the customer. It could affect dealers’ funding costs or the terms they offer for some large hedges, but RBI has not announced a retail surcharge or a change to ordinary remittance pricing. ([RBI Circular 26](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13738&Mode=0))

A separate RBI measure starts October 12: the central bank will meet the full daily dollar requirements of Indian Oil Corporation, Hindustan Petroleum and Bharat Petroleum through designated banks, until further notice. This is a targeted supply route for three public-sector oil marketers—not a general facility for importers, payment firms or consumers. RBI’s announcement states no volume cap. The two measures together show targeted management of currency-market demand, but do not establish how much either will change the rupee’s market rate. ([RBI press release](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63784))

### 2. Fintech draws about $24 million in the first-week funding tally

Inc42’s October 10 weekly roundup counts $122.9 million raised across 25 Indian startups from October 5–9, with fintech receiving $24 million of that total. Its named fintech rounds include StockGro’s $10.4 million Series B tranche (part of a larger round), NeoGrowth’s $8.7 million in a mix of equity and debt from FMO and LeapFrog Investments, Zomint’s $3.7 million seed round led by Lightspeed and Prime Venture Partners, and Credfix’s $1.6 million seed round. Inc42 lists no company valuation for these deals; the round sizes should not be read as valuations. ([Inc42’s funding tracker](https://inc42.com/buzz/from-dailyobjects-to-stockgro-indian-startups-raised-about-123-mn-this-week))

The most notable lending transaction is NeoGrowth’s ₹85 crore financing, confirmed separately in Entrackr’s weekly report. Its debt-and-equity mix matters: the headline amount is not all fresh equity, and the report does not disclose a post-money valuation. Entrackr’s roundup covers October 5–10, while Inc42’s covers October 5–9; their total startup-funding counts differ, so this brief uses Inc42’s fintech-category total and cites Entrackr only for NeoGrowth’s rupee amount. ([Entrackr’s October 5–10 roundup](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-oct-5-oct-10-12662210))

### Regulatory watch: no UPI MDR change confirmed

NPCI’s FAQ continues to state a 0.4% MDR for specified person-to-merchant UPI transactions above ₹2,000, effective October 15; it also caps MDR at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 or more. The fee is merchant-side under the framework, not a blanket charge on UPI users. Moneycontrol reported that the committee meeting expected to consider a deferment was called off and no decision had been made. Until NPCI publishes a formal change, merchants should plan against the published date and customers should distinguish a merchant’s own surcharge from NPCI’s MDR. ([NPCI FAQ](https://www.npci.org.in/uploads/FA_Qs_Merchant_Discount_Rate_MDR_on_Select_UPI_P2_M_Transactions_58dba1d39e.pdf); [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/upi-mdr-decision-in-limbo-as-steering-committee-meeting-called-off-article-14048737.html); [SEBI circular index](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/home/HomeAction.do?doListing=yes&sid=1&smid=0&ssid=0))
