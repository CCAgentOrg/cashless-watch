# Fintech Brief — October 12, 2026

1. SEBI signals near-term AI rules and a derivatives expiry-price decision.

### 1. SEBI's AI framework is coming; expiry-day settlement rules are next

SEBI chair Tuhin Kanta Pandey said the regulator would issue guidance shortly on responsible AI and machine-learning use in capital markets. As reported from the Capital Market Confluence, the proposed framework would use tiered requirements, with clear accountability, controls over data, kill switches and human oversight. The central point is that outsourcing a decision to a model will not outsource responsibility: regulated firms remain accountable for AI outcomes, data quality, model governance and cyber resilience. ([ETLegalWorld report, October 11](https://legal.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/regulators/sebi-to-issue-ai-machine-learning-guidelines-shortly-eyes-expiry-day-settlement-rules-within-a-week-tuhin-kanta-pandey/134874158))

For brokers, exchanges and wealth platforms using automated recommendations, surveillance or customer-facing AI, the signal is to build auditability and a human escalation path before the circular arrives. “Tiered” may allow obligations to scale with risk, but SEBI has not yet published the thresholds, scope or compliance timetable. This is an announcement of intended guidance, not a rule already in force.

Pandey also said SEBI expects to issue guidance within about a week on how derivatives are settled on expiry days. The regulator is reviewing comments on its consultation about the Closing Auction Session and expiry-day settlement prices. That could matter to traders and brokers because the settlement reference affects derivatives exposure at a point when activity can be concentrated; the announcement itself does not settle what final method SEBI will adopt. Both developments now warrant watching for the actual circular, not treating a speech as implementation.

### 2. RBI's account-aggregator changes: broader visibility, not a universal dashboard

An October 12 ETBFSI explainer revisited the RBI's October 7 announcement: interoperability among NBFC Account Aggregators (AAs), plus a route for SEBI-regulated depositories to include bank-deposit information in the Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) through AAs. RBI set December 31, 2026 as the expected implementation date for both measures. ([ETBFSI explainer, October 12](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/revolutionary-changes-to-bank-deposits-and-investments-in-one-statement-what-changes-for-customers/134875513); [RBI's October 7 statement](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63743))

AA interoperability is intended to let a customer use an AA of their choice to access and share information across participating financial information providers, rather than maintaining separate relationships with multiple AAs. The CAS change could bring bank-deposit data alongside demat holdings in one statement. This is useful plumbing for consent-based financial-data sharing and may reduce document gathering when applying for services such as credit.

The limits matter as much as the convenience. The RBI release sets a deadline, not a promise that every bank account will appear immediately. Coverage depends on participating institutions and the information they make available; sharing remains consent-based. Nor does the CAS become a payment screen or automatically enable transfers: the statement is for viewing and reporting, not transacting. Customers should keep using official bank statements and certificates for transactions, tax records and interest calculations.
