# Fintech Brief — October 13, 2026

## Today's Top Stories
1. RBI tightens rules for rupee-linked derivatives and adds a cash-backed reserve for banks’ own hedges.
2. RBI schedules a ₹1.5 lakh crore overnight liquidity-absorption auction.
3. An IIT-Bombay study challenges the new UPI merchant-fee design; it is a proposal, not a policy change.

**Coverage window:** October 12, 08:02–October 13, 08:02 IST.

---

### 1. RBI tightens rupee-derivative rules; hedging gets more balance-sheet intensive

An [RBI circular issued October 12](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13739&Mode=0) makes two material changes. Authorised dealers may offer non-deliverable derivative contracts involving the rupee to non-resident users only for hedging. Separately, an authorised dealer must maintain a Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) for rupee-involving foreign-exchange derivatives it enters into to hedge currency risk on its own assets and liabilities, where it buys foreign currency against rupees. The reserve is cash held in India with RBI, equal to 20% of the rupee-equivalent notional amount, maintained daily until the contract ends. It applies to contracts entered into after the directions were issued.

The scope is important: this is not a 20% charge on consumers, UPI payments, or every derivative traded by every market participant. It raises the cost of specified bank hedges and may change the economics of some dealer quotes; RBI has not quantified any pass-through. The separate [RBI dollar-supply window for public-sector oil marketers](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63784) also became effective on October 12, though RBI announced it on October 10. The measures point to active currency-risk management, not a new retail payment fee.

### 2. RBI to absorb ₹1.5 lakh crore overnight

RBI announced an [overnight Variable Rate Reverse Repo auction](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63793) for October 13, with a notified amount of ₹1,50,000 crore. Banks can submit bids between 9:30 and 10:00 a.m.; the one-day placement reverses on October 14. RBI said the auction followed a review of current and evolving liquidity conditions.

This is a temporary operation to absorb bank liquidity, not a change to the policy repo rate or a new CRR rule. It matters to fintechs indirectly: banks’ short-term funding and liquidity conditions shape the environment in which payment and lending services operate. The auction alone does not establish that customer loan or deposit rates will change.

### 3. IIT-Bombay researchers argue for a smaller UPI merchant fee

A report covered by *The Times of India* on October 12 proposes cutting the specified UPI merchant discount rate from 0.40% to 0.08%. Its authors propose 0.06% for third-party app providers and 0.02% for payment service providers, while removing the banks’ current 70% combined share of the revenue. They argue banks already benefit from low-cost current- and savings-account funding, citing more than ₹4.85 lakh crore in net interest margin from CASA deposits in FY26. The report suggests setting aside about 3%—roughly ₹15,000 crore—of the interest depositors forgo to support UPI operations instead.

The study also argues that fees should be concentrated on large merchants, proposing a ₹50 crore annual-turnover threshold. These are recommendations by the study’s authors, not an NPCI or RBI amendment. The [existing NPCI schedule described in the report](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/iit-bombay-report-recommends-cutting-upi-mdr-from-0-4-to-0-08-says-banks-should-give-up-their-share-as-they-earned-rs-4-85-lakh-crore-from-casa-deposits-in-fy26/articleshow/134885653.cms) puts the specified fee on merchants; that does not rule out merchants passing costs on indirectly. The real policy question is who should fund the payment rail—and whether the chosen model preserves public trust without hiding the cost.

No new Indian fintech startup funding round with a confirmed amount was verified in this 24-hour window, so older deals are not presented as today’s news. The nearest recent example in the sweep is digital lender NeoGrowth’s ₹85 crore equity-and-debt raise from FMO and LeapFrog, reported on October 9—outside this window—with no valuation reported ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/msme-focused-digital-lender-neogrowth-raises-rs-85-cr-from-fmo-leapfrog-12658698)). The date-bounded checks found no new SEBI circular or NPCI decision with a confirmed fintech-facing change. Watch for official UPI implementation updates; the IIT-Bombay recommendation is not itself a change to merchant charges.
