# Fintech Brief — October 16, 2026

## Today's Top Stories
1. **RBI raises banks’ daily CRR floor to 99%** — A tighter operating rule takes effect today, without changing the prescribed CRR ratio.

### 1. RBI tightens daily cash-reserve discipline

A Reserve Bank of India notification raises the minimum daily cash reserve ratio (CRR) that scheduled banks must maintain from 90% to 99% of their prescribed requirement, effective from the reporting fortnight beginning October 16. The change was announced on October 9 and takes effect today; it is not a new CRR announcement in this morning’s window. The [RBI’s notification](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13736&Mode=0) says the decision followed a review of liquidity conditions.

The distinction matters: this is a change to the daily floor, not an increase in the prescribed CRR ratio itself. Banks still have to meet the full requirement on average across the reporting fortnight, but they now have much less room to hold a shortfall on one day and compensate on another. [Business Standard’s report](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/rbi-tightens-liquidity-after-rate-rise-raises-daily-crr-maintenance-to-99-126100901382_1.html) noted that the change reduces banks’ flexibility in managing daily funds. In practice, that could make cash management more exacting and affect overnight liquidity around reporting dates; it does not by itself mean lending must contract.

For fintechs and borrowers, the signal is less about a direct new product rule than about the price and availability of bank liquidity. Payment firms that depend on sponsor banks, settlement accounts or credit lines should watch whether tighter daily buffers are reflected in funding costs. The impact will depend on banks’ liquidity positions and market conditions, not just the headline 99% threshold.

### 2. Paramotor Digital seeks up to ₹1,000 crore through an IPO

Digital-gifting and consumer-spend management company Paramotor Digital Technology has filed an updated draft offer document with SEBI for an IPO of up to ₹1,000 crore, [ETBFSI reported](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/fintech/paramotor-digital-to-raise-up-to-1000-crore-via-ipo-files-updated-draft-with-sebi/134958915). The proposed issue comprises a fresh issue of up to ₹698 crore and an offer for sale of up to ₹302 crore. The company may also consider a pre-IPO placement of up to ₹139.6 crore, which would reduce the fresh issue if completed.

The draft allocates ₹355 crore of fresh proceeds to customer acquisition, retention, branding and promotion; ₹94 crore to technology development, cloud infrastructure and IT assets; and ₹38 crore to office infrastructure, with the balance for general corporate purposes. These are proposed amounts, not funds already raised. The report does not state a final issue price or post-issue valuation, so neither is yet available for comparison. For investors, the key questions will be the economics behind customer acquisition, the quality and repeatability of enterprise revenues, and how the company’s payment-linked products are regulated and disclosed.

### 3. Digital lender Vaibhav Vyapaar’s IPO draws 3.08x bids

The ₹42.1 crore IPO of Vaibhav Vyapaar, which operates the LoanFront digital lending platform, closed on October 15 with subscriptions of 3.08 times the shares on offer, according to [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/ipo/vaibhav-vyapaar-ipo-subscribed-3-08-times-on-final-day-listing-scheduled-for-october-21-14053234.html/amp). The fresh issue comprises 77.96 lakh shares priced at ₹51–₹54 each. The company plans to use about ₹32 crore to strengthen its capital base, with the remainder for general corporate purposes; listing on NSE Emerge is scheduled for October 21.

Subscription is investor demand, not money already received by the company; proceeds depend on allotment and completion. The issue is small beside Paramotor’s proposed offer, but it shows a second route for finance businesses to seek public capital. For a digital lender, investors should look beyond the platform label to the underlying loan book, funding costs, collections and regulatory compliance.
