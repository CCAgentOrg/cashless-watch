# Fintech Brief — October 17, 2026

## Today's Top Stories
1. **RBI clears Emirates NBD’s Indian branches to join RBL Bank** — The transition is set for December 1.
2. **SEBI revises closing-auction and derivatives settlement rules** — Most changes begin December 7; one order-transition rule starts in February.
3. **UPI’s new merchant fee faces its first sustainability test** — Industry voices welcome a revenue path but warn that merchant classification matters.

### 1. RBI approves Emirates NBD’s Indian branches joining RBL Bank

On October 16, the Reserve Bank of India sanctioned the amalgamation of Emirates NBD Bank P.J.S.C.’s Indian branches with RBL Bank under Section 44A of the Banking Regulation Act. From December 1, all of Emirates NBD’s Indian branches will function as RBL Bank branches, the [RBI said in its release](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63820). This is the regulatory integration step after Emirates NBD acquired a 60% stake in RBL Bank for about ₹26,000 crore in June, becoming its promoter, [Mint reported](https://www.livemint.com/companies/news/rbi-approves-merger-of-emirates-nbd-s-india-branches-with-rbl-bank-from-1-december/amp-11792158042439.html).

The move consolidates the two lenders’ Indian operations; it is not a new capital raise. The RBI notice does not specify changes to accounts, fees or service arrangements, so customers should wait for bank communications rather than assume terms change today. RBL and Emirates NBD have until December 1 to complete the transition.

### 2. SEBI changes closing-auction and expiry settlement mechanics

SEBI’s October 15 circular revises the Closing Auction Session (CAS), market timings and derivatives settlement methodology. The cash-market auction will run for ten minutes, from 3:30 to 3:40 p.m. Derivatives generally continue to 3:45 p.m., but expiring index derivatives stop at 3:30 p.m. on expiry days. Expiring stock and index derivatives will use a volume-weighted average price (VWAP) from the final 30 minutes of continuous cash-market trading, rather than relying on the closing-auction price. Most changes take effect on December 7; the new treatment for unexecuted limit and iceberg orders carried into CAS begins February 8, 2027. The dates and mechanics are set out in the [SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1792079571420.pdf).

The circular follows SEBI’s review of CAS operations and consultation. A [Business Standard analysis](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-s-cas-rule-change-bigger-positive-for-brokers-vs-exchanges-jefferies-126101600560_1.html) published October 16 cites Jefferies saying brokers may benefit more than exchanges. For investors, the change alters order execution and expiry-price risk near the close; it does not guarantee lower volatility or a derivatives recovery. Brokers and exchanges must update systems before December.

### 3. UPI MDR debate turns to who pays—and which transaction qualifies

A [Moneycontrol report from October 16](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/who-pays-for-upi-inside-the-mdr-debate-reshaping-digital-payments-14054005.html) captures the first industry debate after the new Merchant Discount Rate framework took effect on October 15, as [Business Standard reported ahead of implementation](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/npci-sets-0-4-fee-on-upi-merchant-payments-above-2-000-effective-oct-15-126091501061_1.html). NPCI’s product chief said person-to-person transfers, P2PM merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month, and P2M payments up to ₹2,000 remain free. The Department of Financial Services–NPCI [official FAQ](https://financialservices.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-09/FAQs---Merchant-Discount-Rate--MDR--on-Select-UPI--P2M--Transactions_0.pdf) sets a 0.4% MDR on eligible P2M transactions above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 from ₹75,000; designated sectors pay a flat ₹5 above ₹2,000, while covered capital-market payments have a 0.02% rate capped at ₹300.

The FAQ says merchants pay MDR and cannot pass it on to customers; P2P transfers remain free. It places broker wallet top-ups and investment payments in the lower capital-market tier. Moneycontrol quotes Zerodha’s capital-markets head warning that a ₹5-lakh trading-account transfer could mean ₹100 at 0.02%, while questioning whether funding one’s own account is a purchase. That is a classification dispute, not proof every transfer will be charged. The fee is an acquiring cost for a merchant, not automatically a charge to the UPI payer.
