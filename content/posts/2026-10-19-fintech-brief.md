# Fintech Brief — October 19, 2026

## Today's Top Stories
- Microfinance portfolios and borrower counts are shrinking even as average loan sizes rise; UPI and account-aggregator data are increasingly part of underwriting.

### 1. Microfinance: larger tickets, fewer borrowers, persistent funding stress

A [Business Standard report published late Sunday](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/mfis-may-have-to-grin-bear-for-some-time-as-funding-pressures-persist-126101800820_1.html) finds a sector still adjusting to tighter credit and funding conditions. Sa-Dhan's June 2026 figures show the microfinance portfolio down 7.6% year on year to ₹3.26 trillion; borrower numbers fell 15% to 65.4 million, and loan accounts fell 19.7% to 100.6 million. Yet the average loan ticket rose 15% to ₹62,962. The report also puts loans more than 180 days overdue at 17.4%, up 440 basis points—evidence that older defaults remain unresolved even as newer originations perform better.

The split matters: this is not simply credit expanding to borrowers with bigger needs. Lenders are retreating from some small-ticket segments, while borrowers try to fit more of their borrowing within the existing lender-count cap. Industry representatives are arguing for a revised ₹3 lakh annual household-income threshold, with proposals ranging from ₹4 lakh to ₹5 lakh or adjustment by state income. Those are proposals, not a new RBI rule. MFI-NBFC portfolios grew 5.2% to ₹46,884 crore, but the article says funding to smaller specialist lenders remains subdued.

One lender quoted by the paper says it uses account-aggregator data—with borrower consent—including cash flows, UPI transaction patterns and utility payments to assess repayment capacity. That may help size loans more realistically than bureau history alone; it also makes meaningful, revocable consent and limits on secondary use of transaction data central consumer safeguards.

### 2. Apple Pay arrives as a card wallet, not a UPI challenger

[Mint's Sunday explainer](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/apple-pay-debuts-in-india-how-it-works-features-and-key-limitations-11792297454132.html) describes Apple's India rollout, which began in late September: eligible Axis Bank Visa and Mastercard credit-card holders can add a card to Apple Wallet and use biometric-authenticated contactless payments, plus Apple Pay at participating online checkouts. The initial launch does not include RuPay, UPI or Apple Cash. Apple Pay therefore adds an iPhone-based interface for existing card rails; it does not replace the QR-and-bank-account payments most people use day to day.

For customers, the convenience comes with the same underlying card credit limit, billing cycle, rewards and foreign-transaction fees. Tokenisation means the merchant does not receive the actual card number, but the card's existing terms and costs still apply. The competitive question is whether Apple broadens bank and network access; the immediate consumer trade-off is smoother checkout, not a new low-cost payment rail.

### 3. Jio's mega-IPO puts UPI mandates in the spotlight

Jio Platforms' anchor-investor bidding is scheduled for today, before the public issue opens on October 21 and closes October 23. The [SEBI-filed prospectus](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/oct-2026/Jio%20Platforms%20Limited%20-%20Red%20Herring%20Prospectus%20and%20Abridged%20Prospectus_p.pdf) sets out an offer of up to 270 million fresh shares; the announced price band of ₹1,065–₹1,119 implies a raise of up to about ₹30,213 crore and a valuation near ₹10.3 lakh crore at the top end, according to [Business Standard's Sunday review](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/ipo/ipo-review-jio-platforms-30-213-crore-mega-offer-is-at-a-minor-premium-126101800494_1.html). This is proposed capital, not money already raised. The prospectus gives retail applicants using UPI until 7 p.m. IST on October 23 to complete the mandate. For a digital-services company of this scale, the IPO is also a high-profile test of the plumbing that connects online applications to bank-account fund blocks.
