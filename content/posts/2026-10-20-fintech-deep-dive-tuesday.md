# Fintech Deep Dive — Tuesday | October 20, 2026

**Focus:** Buzz & Funding — venture rounds, fund formation, IPOs and the market’s test of fintech growth.

**Coverage:** October 13–20, 2026; reporting checked through 8:30 a.m. IST on October 20.

## Executive summary

The week’s clearest funding signal is not a sudden rush of new fintech rounds. It is a narrower market: Tracxn’s first-nine-month tally shows dollars up 14% year on year while the number of deals fell 41%, and nearly two-thirds of capital went to late-stage companies. A newly independent investment firm, BlackLane Capital, says it intends to back startups across fintech and other sectors, but its stated five-year deployment target is a plan—not a fund close. Public-company results add the harder test behind the headlines: Groww is absorbing a regulatory hit to derivatives income while expanding into adjacent businesses; Jio Financial’s core income is rising even as costs and provisions suppress profit; and Super.money’s revenue has tripled alongside a substantially larger loss. More capital is available to proven businesses than to the broad early-stage field, and investors are asking for evidence that customer acquisition and lending can pay for themselves.

## 1. Fintech funding rises; the round count says the recovery is selective

On October 13, Fortune India reported Tracxn’s January–September 2026 fintech data: Indian companies raised **$2.2 billion**, 14% more than the **$1.9 billion** raised in the same 2025 period. But the number of rounds dropped from **260 to 153**—a 41% decline. The averages can mislead: a small number of large cheques are lifting the total while many fewer companies are securing rounds. [The report](https://www.fortuneindia.com/personal-finance/banking/fintech-funding-rises-14-to-22-bn-in-9m-cy26-sector-adds-3-new-unicorns/164321) says five rounds of at least $100 million contributed $1.1 billion, or 48% of the total; CRED’s $540 million Series H alone represented about one-quarter.

The concentration becomes clearer by stage. Late-stage funding—Series C and later, private equity and pre-IPO—reached **$1.5 billion**, up 95%, but across only 25 deals versus 30 a year earlier. It made up 67% of all fintech funding. The headline total remains about 40% of the $5.5 billion peak recorded in the first nine months of 2021. This is not a broad-based return to the boom years; it is capital favouring businesses that can already support scale, large balance sheets or a credible route to liquidity.

There are success signals: Tracxn counted three fintech IPOs in the period—Augmont, Turtlemint and Kissht—and three new unicorns, Square Yards, KreditBee and Neo Group. A company crossing a $1 billion private valuation is not the same as delivering returns to investors, and an IPO is an exit route, not proof of durable profitability. The relevant distinction is between *more dollars raised* and *more companies funded*. So far, the former is improving faster than the latter.

## 2. BlackLane Capital: a new funding channel, but not $100 million in the bank

A second capital story came from Entrackr’s October 12–17 startup roundup. CoinSwitch Ventures has rebranded as **BlackLane Capital**, describing itself as an independent global early-stage investment firm that will expand beyond Web3 into fintech, enterprise infrastructure, consumer applications and digital assets. It says it plans to invest up to **$5 million per startup** and facilitate more than **$100 million of funding over five years**. The firm says it has backed over 26 startups since 2022. [Entrackr’s report](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-oct-12-oct-17-12683867) does not disclose a new fund’s committed corpus or imply that the $100 million has already been raised or deployed; it is a forward-looking target.

That caveat matters in a week when the same roundup counted **28 startups raising $302.85 million** across all sectors—eight growth-stage deals, 21 early-stage deals and one undisclosed round. E-commerce, AI and manufacturing led the sector tally. This is a broad startup snapshot, not a fintech funding total, and it should not be used to claim that fintech captured a share of the $302.85 million. The point is narrower: new capital providers are announcing mandates, but announced intent and closed investments are different stages of the funding pipeline.

BlackLane’s expansion beyond crypto/Web3 could broaden the pool of specialist investors available to financial infrastructure and consumer-finance founders. The test will be its actual portfolio: how much reaches Indian fintech, at what stage, on what terms, and whether a funder with crypto origins can source companies outside that network. Until deal announcements appear, founders should treat the headline as a potential channel—not a financing round they can count on.

## 3. Groww’s Q2: profitability survives a derivatives shock, with a lending trade-off

Billionbrains Garage Ventures, Groww’s listed parent, reported Q2 FY27 revenue from operations of **₹1,501.53 crore**, up 47% year on year, and profit attributable to its shareholders of **₹725.65 crore**, up 54%. Total income was **₹1,540.54 crore**, up 44%. These are results for the quarter ended September 30, not a new funding round; the company’s [October 14 exchange filing and reviewed statements](https://resources.groww.in/web-assets/media-library/2026/10/Q2%20FY27%20Financial%20Results.pdf) are the primary record.

The more revealing detail is the revenue mix. Equity derivatives fell to **44.8% of total income**, from 56.9% a year earlier, and segment revenue declined 14.3% sequentially as the market adjusted to SEBI’s Closing Auction System. Groww’s total revenue was roughly flat against the June quarter, even while year-on-year growth remained strong. It is therefore too simple to read the 54% profit increase as proof that regulatory changes no longer matter. [Moneycontrol’s results coverage](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/groww-expects-f-o-volumes-to-normalise-after-sebi-s-cas-rethink-not-rise-mtf-book-adds-rs-576-crore-14053125.html) describes the derivatives impact and management’s expectation that activity may normalise; that expectation is not a settled outcome.

Groww is building other revenue lines: the company reported higher contributions from margin-trading facilities and commodity derivatives; commodities rose to 6.2% of revenue from 0.2% a year earlier, while its market share in MTF rose to 2.7% from 1.6%. Wealth assets under management exceeded ₹10,000 crore, and its AMC’s AUM reached ₹7,179 crore, up from ₹2,928 crore. Transacting users rose 27% to about 2.4 crore and customer assets by 46% to ₹3.9 lakh crore, according to [Moneycontrol’s Q2 report](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/groww-s-net-profit-rises-by-54-to-rs-725-crore-in-q2-despite-cas-impact-equity-derivatives-contribution-to-revenue-declines-to-45-article-14051859.html).

This is an attractive diversification story, but the replacement engines carry different risks. MTF growth means more customer borrowing against securities; that adds credit, collateral and market-fall exposure, not just commission income. The funding-market question is whether Groww can convert distribution into steadier, recurring revenue without simply shifting dependence from high-volume derivatives to leveraged customers. Watch MTF book quality and costs, not only its market share.

## 4. Jio Financial: operating income expands while reported profit contracts

Jio Financial Services’ Q2 FY27 result, reported October 15, is a useful counterpoint to the Groww growth story. Consolidated operating revenue rose **73% year on year to ₹1,699.3 crore**, but profit after tax fell **51% to ₹340.4 crore**, from ₹695 crore. Net interest income climbed 165% to **₹680 crore**. Those measures point in different directions because the company is expanding its lending and fee businesses while the reported profit line is affected by funding costs, provisions and volatile non-operating income. [Inc42’s results report](https://inc42.com/buzz/jio-financial-services-q2-profit-halves-yoy-to-%E2%82%B9340-cr) notes that the year-ago quarter included ₹269 crore of dividend income, compared with almost none this quarter; the immediately preceding quarter included ₹508.6 crore.

Expenses increased 167% year on year to **₹1,166.1 crore**. Finance costs rose 325% to **₹577.7 crore** as borrowings expanded, alongside higher operating and staff costs. Jio Financial is investing in a broad financial-services stack—Jio Credit, payments, insurance broking and asset-management joint ventures—but that breadth creates a long period in which spending may precede mature earnings. [Business Standard’s market report](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/jio-financial-stock-drop-2-5-as-q2-pat-halves-moFsl-cuts-fy27-estimates-126101600346_1.html) says the share price fell around 2.5% after the result and notes that Motilal Oswal cut its FY27 earnings estimate to reflect higher provisions, even while citing growth in lending and asset management.

The fair reading is neither “the business is failing” nor “revenue growth proves the strategy.” The dividend comparison exaggerates the year-on-year profit decline, but rising finance costs and provisions are real costs of scaling a lender. Investors should separate recurring net interest and fee income from dividends, and track credit quality and cost-to-income as the book grows. A large parent ecosystem can finance expansion; it cannot make the economics of each loan or product disappear.

## 5. Super.money: customer acquisition is growing faster than the profit engine

Flipkart-backed Super.money’s FY26 filings, reported by The Economic Times on October 20, show operating revenue more than tripling to **₹292.4 crore**, from ₹94 crore in FY25. Total income rose to **₹293.3 crore**, but expenses more than doubled to **₹557.1 crore** and net loss widened 78% to **₹263.8 crore**. The report attributes the loss expansion in part to business-promotion spending rising more than threefold. [ET’s account, based on MCA filings](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/flipkart-backed-super-moneys-fy26-revenue-triples-to-rs-292-crore-but-loss-widens-78/articleshow/135044276.cms) says the platform is using UPI to acquire customers and a credit marketplace to monetise them.

This is the classic payments-to-credit wager: low-friction payments can create reach, then credit and commerce are expected to turn that reach into revenue. But a threefold increase in revenue does not settle whether each customer is profitable. The reported expense base was nearly 1.9 times total income, and promotion costs can hide weak repeat usage if new cohorts must continually be bought. Credit adds a second set of questions—who originates the loan, what customers pay, how defaults are shared and whether offers remain clear inside a payments app.

FY26 is an annual account, not a fresh funding announcement in this week’s window. The reporting does not disclose a new round, valuation, acquisition cost per active customer or cohort-level contribution margin. Flipkart’s backing gives Super.money room to invest, but the consumer and investor test remains the same: can it convert payment activity into sustainable, responsible earnings without making borrowing the default monetisation path?

## What to watch

The week offers no evidence of a broad fintech deal rebound. Instead, capital is concentrated in later-stage rounds; a new fund manager is advertising future deployment; and listed and parent-backed firms are being judged on what happens after customer acquisition. **Next signals:** closed BlackLane investments and disclosed terms; Groww’s next-quarter MTF and non-derivatives revenue alongside credit performance; Jio Financial’s provisions and funding costs relative to recurring income; and Super.money’s next filing on promotion spend, repeat use and lending economics. Funding headlines tell us who may get a cheque. These operating measures tell us whether the cheque is buying a durable business.
