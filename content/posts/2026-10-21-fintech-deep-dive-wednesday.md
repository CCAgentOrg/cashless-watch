# Fintech Deep Dive — Wednesday | October 21, 2026

**Consumer fintech | Developments: October 14–21, 2026**

This week’s consumer-finance signal is not a single breakthrough app. It is a more familiar strategy: put a financial product where a customer already shops, rides, or pays—and use a large lending balance sheet or partner network to make the offer work. Four developments show the moving parts: Bajaj Finance’s expanding retail-credit book; a new Eternal–SBI Card tied to Zomato, Blinkit and District; Ather Energy’s entry into insurance distribution; and Kissht’s proposed capital raise for its lending subsidiary.

These are different stages, not equivalent launches. Bajaj has reported completed lending activity. Eternal’s card was announced with applications due to open in phases from October 22. Ather has a regulator’s distribution registration, not an insurance product or underwriting licence. Kissht has exchange in-principle approvals for a proposed share issue, not cash already received. For consumers, that distinction matters: the app or brand on the screen may not be the company setting the credit terms, carrying the lending risk, or paying an insurance claim.

## 1. Bajaj Finance: growth at scale, but the headline metrics answer different questions

Bajaj Finance’s September-quarter results, reported on October 19, provide the week’s clearest snapshot of large-scale consumer credit. Consolidated net profit rose 28.8% year on year to ₹6,367 crore, while assets under management reached ₹5.85 lakh crore, up 26%. The company added ₹37,680 crore to AUM during the quarter. It booked 13.45 million new loans, 11% more than a year earlier, and reported a customer franchise of 128.85 million after adding 4.42 million in the quarter. These are company-level measures, not a count of unique active borrowers or a disclosure of how many people took multiple loans. [CNBC-TV18’s results report](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/bajaj-finance-share-price-q2fy27-results-net-profit-nii-rises-asset-quality-strong-20015184.htm) carries the results and management outlook.

The mix is more informative than any one growth rate. AUM expanded much faster than the number of new loans booked. That can reflect ticket size, the duration of outstanding loans and product mix; it does not, by itself, tell us that more households became borrowers. Nor does a 29% rise in profit mean borrowing became cheaper. Net interest income increased 24%, with the lender’s earning assets and funding both growing.

Credit costs deserve equal attention. Loan losses and provisions rose 4% year on year to ₹2,166 crore. This included ₹337 crore set aside for management and macroeconomic risks; excluding that additional provision, the reported amount was down 12% to ₹1,829 crore. The loan-loss ratio improved to 1.56% from 1.88% a year earlier, or 1.32% excluding the added provision. Those are aggregate portfolio indicators. They cannot tell a borrower whether a particular “easy EMI” or personal-loan offer is affordable, or how a specific product’s delinquencies are behaving.

The consumer test is therefore still at checkout: compare the cash price with the financed total, and read the repayment amount, fees, due dates and consequences of missing a payment before accepting an instalment plan. A broad customer franchise is a distribution advantage for the lender; it is not evidence that every credit offer suits every customer. Watch the next disclosures for product-level loan growth and delinquencies, not just a larger AUM figure.

## 2. Eternal and SBI Card: an everyday-spending discount with a credit product attached

Eternal and SBI Card announced their co-branded card on October 15. Customers could request early access through Zomato, Blinkit and District apps, with invitations to apply expected to begin in phases from October 22. At this report’s cutoff, the offer was announced but the phased application process had not yet begun. [Mint’s launch coverage](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/zomato-eternal-sbi-card-blinkit-district-how-customers-apply-direct-app-online-credit-card-key-features-benefits-explain-11792132467468.html) sets out the initial terms.

The headline benefit is a 10% instant discount on eligible purchases through Zomato, Bistro and District, and on Blinkit electronics and beauty purchases. Other eligible Blinkit categories get 5%, subject to exclusions. Discounts across the Eternal platforms share a ₹2,000 monthly cap. Separately, cardholders can receive 5% cashback—up to ₹1,000 a month—on purchases with Apollo 24|7, ixigo, Uber and Urban Company. The joining fee is ₹499 plus tax; the ₹499 renewal fee is waived after at least ₹2 lakh of spend in the preceding year. Paying the joining fee also brings a six-month Zomato Gold membership.

This is an unusually legible example of commerce becoming a credit-card acquisition channel. Eternal can promote the card inside apps that already capture frequent food, quick-commerce and entertainment purchases; SBI Card supplies the credit-card product. If the customer’s existing eligible spend is high, discounts may be useful. If not, the monthly cap and fee threshold can turn a vivid percentage into a modest realised benefit. And a discount is not a reason to carry a balance: interest and charges on revolving credit can outweigh rewards quickly.

The card’s real test is not how many users tap “request early access.” It is whether eligible cardholders understand which purchases qualify, how the shared cap works, and what happens after an application is approved. The offer also rewards spending inside one corporate ecosystem, which can make the customer’s default choice less neutral. Compare it against a no-fee card or the platform’s ordinary offer using actual monthly spending—not the maximum advertised percentage.

## 3. Ather Insurance: a distribution licence is not insurance cover

IRDAI issued Ather Insurance Limited a Certificate of Registration on October 15 to operate as a composite corporate agent. The subsidiary is wholly owned by Ather Energy, and its registration covers distribution and servicing of life, general and health insurance policies. The distinction is important: Ather Insurance is authorised to distribute policies; it does not underwrite them or become the insurer responsible for paying claims. [The Economic Times reported the approval on October 16](https://m.economictimes.com/tech/technology/ather-energy-gets-irdai-licence-to-distribute-and-service-insurance-policies/articleshow/134984985.cms), citing Ather’s stock-exchange filing.

Ather’s potential advantage is proximity to an existing customer journey. A vehicle purchase or service interaction creates a natural moment to discuss motor cover, while a connected app can keep policy documents and renewal reminders close to the vehicle. In September, Ather recorded 30,479 electric two-wheeler registrations, or 14.71% of the segment, according to Vahan data cited by ET. That is a monthly sales measure, not the size of its insured customer base; it shows market presence, not insurance uptake.

The commercial opportunity is plausible, but the public announcement does not identify insurer partners, product wording, commissions, expected revenue or a customer launch date. That gap is exactly where a consumer should wait for evidence rather than assume an integrated app means better cover. At purchase or renewal, customers should be able to see the actual insurer, premium, exclusions, deductibles, claims channel and the effect—if any—on warranty or service. They should also be able to decline the add-on without losing access to the underlying vehicle service.

The indicators to watch are concrete: named insurer tie-ups, policy documents available before payment, renewal and cancellation flows, and published complaint or claims-handling data. A regulator’s registration is a permission to distribute. It is not an endorsement of any particular policy’s value, nor proof that the distribution will be impartial.

## 4. Kissht’s proposed ₹832 crore issue: more lending capacity is not yet cheaper credit

On October 20, OnEMI Technology Solutions, the company behind Kissht, said the BSE and NSE had granted in-principle approvals for a proposed preferential issue of up to 2.65 crore shares at ₹314.11 each, worth about ₹832.2 crore. The board approved the proposal in September and shareholders approved it on October 14. Entrackr reported that 34 investors are expected to participate. The company’s September filing said 75% of the proceeds would go to its wholly owned lending subsidiary, Si Creva Capital Services, with the remaining 25% for general corporate purposes. [Entrackr’s report](https://entrackr.com/news/kissht-gets-bse-nse-nod-for-rs-832-cr-fundraise-12691676) and [the company’s NSE filing](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ONEMI_18092026103753_Press_Release_Pref_18092026.pdf) describe the proposal.

The wording matters. In-principle exchange approvals are a milestone in the process, not proof that the shares have been issued or the money received. The company says stronger capital could improve credit ratings, lower its borrowing costs and support lending expansion. Those are intended effects, not automatic consumer outcomes. A better-capitalised lender may be able to fund more loans; whether customers see lower rates depends on the lender’s funding mix, pricing, competition and risk costs.

This is a consumer-finance story because capital is the upstream input to digital credit. Kissht’s app can originate demand, but its lending subsidiary’s balance sheet and underwriting determine what credit is actually available. The equity raise is not a customer discount and does not change a loan’s terms by itself. At the time of publication, Entrackr reported no valuation attached to the proposed issue. The useful follow-up will be whether the issue closes, how much reaches the lending subsidiary, and whether growth is matched by transparent pricing and stable repayment performance.

For anyone considering app-based credit, identify the lender named in the offer—not just the familiar app—and compare the annualised cost, fees, repayment schedule and late-payment terms before accepting. More funding can increase access, but access only helps when the loan is suitable and the price is clear.

## The pattern: distribution is growing faster than consumer visibility

Taken together, the four stories describe a shift from standalone financial apps toward financial products distributed through other relationships: a retailer’s ecosystem, a vehicle maker’s customer journey, and a large lender’s existing consumer network. Kissht’s proposed issue sits one layer upstream, supplying capital to the lender behind the digital interface. This can reduce friction at the point of discovery. It can also make the product’s legal and commercial structure harder to see.

The consumer-facing question is not simply “Is this fintech?” It is: who is the regulated provider, who receives the fee or commission, who controls eligibility and pricing, and who handles a complaint or claim? A branded card, an insurance tile and a loan marketplace each put different firms in that chain. Clear screens and useful products do not remove the need to disclose it.

## What to watch next

- **Eternal SBI Card:** whether applications open on October 22 as planned, the full eligibility and fee terms, and whether customers can see the remaining monthly discount cap.
- **Ather Insurance:** named insurers, complete policy wording, customer opt-out and renewal flows, and evidence on claims service—not just the registration.
- **Bajaj Finance:** growth and credit costs by product, alongside the company’s FY27 loan and customer-addition guidance.
- **Kissht:** completion of the proposed issue, cash actually received by Si Creva, and subsequent disclosures on lending quality and pricing.
