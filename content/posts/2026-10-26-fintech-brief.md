# Fintech Brief — October 26, 2026

## Today's Top Stories
1. RBI inaugurates a ₹169-crore data centre and cybersecurity institute in Odisha, with RTGS and NEFT among the systems it will host.
2. YourStory's latest weekly tally puts Indian startup funding at $96 million across 15 deals; a separate IPO pipeline points to continuing public-market fundraising.

### 1. RBI's new data centre puts payment-system resilience in focus

Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the Reserve Bank of India's new Data Centre and Cybersecurity Training Institute in Bhubaneswar on October 25. The RBI says the centre will host mission-critical systems including RTGS and NEFT; the institute is intended to build expertise in cybersecurity, AI and emerging technologies. YourStory, citing officials, puts the project cost at ₹169 crore and says the facility covers 18.55 acres at Jatni, near Bhubaneswar. The stated location rationale includes distance from seismically active zones and high-risk international borders. [RBI's release](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63862) and [YourStory's report](https://yourstory.com/2026/10/pm-inaugurates-rbis-rs-169-cr-data-centre-cybersecurity-training-institute-in-odisha) provide the institutional and project details.

The move matters because payments infrastructure is judged not only by transaction speed but by whether critical services continue through outages, cyber incidents or disruption at a primary site. At the inauguration, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman cited UPI volumes of more than 2,400 crore transactions a month—roughly 80 crore a day, according to [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/india/indias-upi-processes-80-crore-transactions-daily-revolutionizing-digital-payments/articleshow/135190724.cms). That scale raises the value of resilience across the financial system.

One distinction matters: the RBI release names RTGS and NEFT as systems the centre will host; it does not say UPI is moving there. UPI is an NPCI-operated system, so the inauguration should not be read as a UPI product change or a new consumer facility. Nor does the announcement publish service-availability targets or results from disaster-recovery testing. The test of this investment will be operational: whether redundancy, cyber readiness and recovery procedures measurably improve continuity for banks and users.

### 2. Startup funding slows, while IPOs offer another capital route

YourStory's October 26 [Daily Capsule](https://yourstory.com/2026/10/startup-funding-falls-below-100m) reports that Indian startups raised $96 million across 15 deals in the third week of October, down from $180 million the week before. It marks the eighth time in 2026 that the outlet's weekly tally has fallen below $100 million. Even Healthcare's $50 million round, backed by Khosla Ventures and Alpha Wave, was the largest reported deal; the roundup says only 16 funding rounds worth at least $100 million have occurred so far this year. It does not provide a valuation for Even Healthcare, and this is a broad startup-market measure—not a fintech-only funding total.

The same edition points to a different funding channel: five companies were expected to open IPOs between October 26 and 29, targeting nearly ₹2,600 crore in total, according to its [IPO preview](https://yourstory.com/2026/10/five-ipos-to-open-next-week-companies-eye-close-to-rs-2600-cr). YourStory says 104 companies had raised more than ₹1.4 lakh crore through IPOs in 2026 to that point. These figures do not imply that fintech firms are receiving the same access; they show how unevenly capital is being distributed between venture rounds and public offerings. The near-term signal for private startups is still scarce large cheques, while the IPO window remains comparatively active.

## Regulatory watch

The RBI announcement was an infrastructure investment, not a new direction for lenders or payment providers. Our scan of official RBI, SEBI and NPCI release channels did not surface a new consumer-facing rule or UPI change in this 24-hour window. The clearest development is therefore about the back-end capacity needed to keep digital finance dependable—not a change to what customers can do or what they pay.
