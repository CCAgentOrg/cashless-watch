# Fintech Brief — October 28, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### PhonePe's Nigam makes the public case for UPI MDR — and questions the market-share clock

Speaking at Business Standard's BFSI Insight Summit, PhonePe founder and CEO Sameer Nigam argued the return of the merchant discount rate (MDR) is necessary to keep UPI sustainable: "You can't have a vibrant payments market with no money in payments." He disclosed that PhonePe has spent close to **$1.7–1.8 billion over ten years** — all of it losses — building UPI adoption, and brought in **$2.5 billion of foreign capital** while remaining without a life-to-date profit. At the **0.4% cap** NPCI has notified for select person-to-merchant transactions above ₹2,000, he said, India would have "the cheapest MDR in the world." [Business Standard interview](https://www.business-standard.com/specials/bs-events/still-have-600-mn-users-left-to-acquire-on-upi-phonepe-chief-sameer-nigam-126102701780_1.html) · [Companion MDR report](https://www.business-standard.com/specials/bs-events/bfsi-insight-summit-mdr-vital-to-sustain-upi-growth-says-phonepe-ceo-126102701765_1.html)

On the NPCI circular that would cap any single third-party UPI app at 30% of transaction volume from December, Nigam said the rule dates to 2019–20, when WhatsApp's entry spooked an ecosystem that had not yet formed. Today, over a hundred third-party UPI apps operate; Apple Pay is entering, WhatsApp Pay may return, and agentic players like OpenAI and Anthropic will want payments. If PhonePe is barred from onboarding new users in December, he said he would "protest, but comply." Roughly 450–500 million Indians actively use digital payments; in his telling, 600 million more remain to be acquired.

Consumer takeaway: this debate decides who eventually pays for "free" UPI — and the December deadline is the first hard test of whether the regulator protects consumer choice or market-share optics. Yesterday's Paytm estimate (about 2.5 basis points to its processing margin) and today's PhonePe advocacy put the merchant fee at the centre of every platform's planning.

### Retail India gets the keys to Wall Street — through the regulated doors

Groww, which accounts for more than **29% of India's 46 million active broking clients**, launched US equity trading earlier this month with 10,000-plus US stocks and ETFs plus fractional ownership; **Zerodha (15% share)** has begun beta testing ahead of a launch in weeks; **Angel One (6.7 million active clients)** plans its own rollout. CNBC reports Nvidia, SpaceX, Apple, Alphabet, Amazon, Micron and Meta top the early buy lists. The backdrop: the Nifty 50 is down roughly 13% this year against a 12% gain in the S&P 500, with a record **$32.5 billion of foreign investor outflows** from Indian equities since January per NSDL data. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/india-us-stocks-retail-access.html) · [NSDL FPI data](https://www.fpi.nsdl.co.in/web/Reports/Yearwise.aspx?RptType=6)

The RBI's own numbers show the shift. In August, resident Indians sent over **$321 million overseas for equity and debt investing, up 111% year on year**; investment now makes up **11.7% of all outward remittances, nearly double last year's 5.8%**. Kotak Mahindra Asset Management Singapore's chief executive cautioned that as participation grows, the regulator could have "second thoughts" — particularly while the rupee is under pressure. For consumers: the LRS route is legal, but budget for the 20% tax-collected-at-source on investment remittances and for currency swings in both directions. [RBI Bulletin data](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/Bulletin/PDFs/36T_231020262674CC6E9F53484DAB352D0454EF377D.PDF)

### A crypto-shaped side door: Mochatrade raises $5.5 million for US-stock perps

Y Combinator-backed Mochatrade raised a **$5.5 million seed led by CoinFund**, with Coinbase Ventures and Zero Knowledge Ventures participating, taking total reported funding to about $5.56 million including its earlier pre-seed. The platform lets Indian users take long or short **perpetual futures linked to US stock prices** — accounts funded via UPI, balances in rupees, no US brokerage account, no ownership of the underlying shares. It is in beta with a public launch planned in the coming weeks. [Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/india/mochatrade-raises-5-5-million-to-bring-us-stock-trading-closer-to-indian-traders/articleshow/135224076.cms) · [Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/funding/yc-backed-mochatrade-raises-55-million-to-bring-us-stock-perpetuals-to-indian-traders-12756788) · [The Block via Ground News](https://ground.news/article/mochatrade-raises-6m-to-offer-us-stock-perpetuals-to-indian-traders)

The caution writes itself: these are leveraged synthetic products riding crypto-market plumbing, not SEBI-registered broking. The same US-stock hunger that Groww and Zerodha will serve under LRS rules and exchange oversight has a shadow market — and leverage amplifies losses faster than it amplifies gains.
