# Fintech Deep Dive — Thursday | November 05, 2026

**International & Cross-Border | Developments: October 29 – November 5, 2026**

The week's cross-border story is a single system pulling in two directions at once. On one side, India kept building the sanctioned pipes for money crossing its borders: Razorpay got the final licence to run foreign-currency payment infrastructure out of GIFT City, SEBI opened a depository-receipts channel for REITs and InvITs on GIFT City's international exchanges, and the RBI quietly deleted a paperwork requirement for freelancers and small service exporters. On the other side, the enforcement arm arrived for the unsanctioned routes: the Enforcement Directorate raided crypto platforms over ₹5,000 crore of alleged unauthorised stablecoin remittances, and prepaid crypto cards that let Indians spend offshore stablecoin wallets at kirana stores started dying mid-transaction. Between those poles, UPI's tourist acceptance went digital — Burj Khalifa tickets, bookable with GPay — and Zimbabwe's October 31 deadline for a UPI backbone deal passed without a signature. Every story below is really the same question: when money crosses India's border, through which door, and who is watching it?

## 1. Razorpay's final GIFT City licence: the outbound plumbing consolidates

On November 2, Razorpay received its final Payment Service Provider (PSP) licence from the International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA), upgrading the in-principle clearance it received in April 2026 and allowing it to move money into and out of India from GIFT City under a single regulatory framework ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/funding/razorpay-gets-final-gift-city-licence-to-expand-cross-border-payments/articleshow/135448860.cms); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/razorpay-gets-final-ifsca-licence-cross-border-payments-gift-city-126110200734_1.html); [Fintech News Singapore](https://fintechnews.sg/140423/fintech-india/razorpay-gift-city-ifsca-psp-license)). The licence adds foreign-currency transfers and multi-currency accounts to a cross-border portfolio that already includes the RBI's Payment Aggregator–Cross Border (PA-CB) authorisation from December 2025, under which the company says Indian businesses can collect payments in over 130 currencies from platforms like Airbnb, Shopify and Hostinger ([Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/news/razorpay-secures-final-ifsca-psp-licence-to-move-money-into-and-out-of-india-from-gift-city-12775697)). Razorpay has completed integrations with RBL Bank and ICICI Bank, and says broker and wealth platforms will go live shortly — the intended traffic being Indians investing globally and NRIs remitting home ([Fintech News Singapore](https://fintechnews.sg/140423/fintech-india/razorpay-gift-city-ifsca-psp-license); [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/razorpay-secures-final-ifsca-psp-licence-for-gift-city-operations)).

Two pieces of context make this more than another licence announcement. First, it lands a week after Skydo picked up the identical final PSP licence (October 27), and in the same month Jio Payment Solutions crowded into exporter collections under PA-CB — the dual-stack of RBI authorisation for the domestic leg plus IFSCA licensing for the international leg is now the standard template, and the two GIFT City licences went to exactly the kind of platforms that already own the domestic rails ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/fintech/skydo-gets-final-ifsca-licence-to-operate-as-psp-at-gift-city/135233230)). Second, the demand side is softening: RBI data shows outward remittances under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme fell nearly 2% to $28.98 billion in FY26, with overseas education spending down 20.9% to $2.31 billion ([Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/news/razorpay-secures-final-ifsca-psp-licence-to-move-money-into-and-out-of-india-from-gift-city-12775697)). The corridor infrastructure is scaling into a market where the old volume driver — tuition payments — is shrinking, which explains why every platform is chasing the investing and wealth use case instead: regulators are increasingly directing cross-border investment flows through GIFT City, and whoever owns that plumbing owns the next decade of outbound flow ([Fintech News Singapore](https://fintechnews.sg/140423/fintech-india/razorpay-gift-city-ifsca-psp-license)).

The same regulator-plus-week arithmetic applied to capital coming in: on November 2 SEBI issued circulars creating a framework for depository receipts backed by REIT and InvIT units, listable on GIFT City's India INX and NSE International Exchange, with DR holders required to be non-residents, a 75% unitholder-approval norm for fresh issues, and a 24-hour mirror of international-exchange disclosures to Indian exchanges ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/nov-2026/framework-for-issuance-of-depository-receipts-on-units-of-reits-_105558.html); [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/sebi-allows-reits-invits-to-issue-depository-receipts-on-gift-city-exchanges-sets-75-percent-approval-norm-14066645.html)). Inbound real-estate and infrastructure capital, outbound retail investing, exporter collections, NRI remittances — GIFT City is becoming the single regulated doorway for cross-border money, which is efficient for compliance and concentrates consumer risk in the fine print of whichever platform holds your dollars.

## 2. The stablecoin shadow corridor meets the Enforcement Directorate

While the sanctioned doors expanded, the unauthorised ones got kicked in. On October 27, the Enforcement Directorate's Bengaluru Zonal Office searched four premises — three in Bengaluru, one in Madhya Pradesh — under Section 37 of FEMA, targeting RPFAS Technologies Private Limited (which operates investment platforms Mudrex and Saber.money) and entities linked to Instadapp Labs; the press release followed on October 29 ([ED on X](https://twitter.com/dir_ed); [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/bangalore/ed-raids-mudrex-saber-money-crypto-bengaluru-mp-fema-case-10942916); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/ed-searches-several-premises-in-bengaluru-and-madhya-pradesh-in-illegal-crypto-deals-enn26102905768)). The agency's preliminary account: more than ₹4,600 crore of unauthorised cross-border transfers and unauthorised crypto balances on foreign platforms, plus roughly $40 million in alleged violations at the Instadapp-linked entities — north of ₹5,000 crore in aggregate, moved by converting user rupees into stablecoins and paying out through OTC deals on Binance and Bybit via shell entities in tax havens ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/10/29/indias-ed-searches-mudrex-instadapp-over-inr-4600-crore-in-alleged-crypto-fema-violations); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/mudrex-instadapp-ed-searches-525-million-crypto-probe)). This is the second such action this year: on June 17 the same zonal office searched six premises across five crypto payment and remittance firms — Transak, Carret, Mokshagna Technologies, Buyhatke and Abhibha Technologies — alleging over ₹2,500 crore moved through a nearly identical stablecoin structure ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/10/29/indias-ed-searches-mudrex-instadapp-over-inr-4600-crore-in-alleged-crypto-fema-violations)).

Days later the same enforcement logic reached the consumer end of the corridor. The Economic Times reported on November 2 that the RBI and FIU-IND are scrutinising prepaid crypto cards — RedotPay being the named example — which let Indian residents load stablecoins into offshore wallets and spend them domestically on cards that look, to merchants, like ordinary Visa/Mastercard debit cards, with the stablecoin-to-rupee conversion happening invisibly at settlement ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/india/crypto-cards-enter-rbis-radar-as-indians-use-offshore-wallets-for-everyday-payments/articleshow/135436233.cms); [Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/11/02/crypto-cards-india-rbi-fiu-scrutinise-redotpay-visa-mastercard-stablecoin-spending)). Authorities are examining potential violations of payment-system regulations and FEMA, are in touch with Visa and Mastercard, and — notably — several Indian users' cards and provider websites have been blocked since around October 29 ([CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/india-rbi-stablecoin-cards-redotpay-shutdown-fears); [Coin Edition](https://coinedition.com/why-are-offshore-crypto-cards-facing-rbi-and-fiu-scrutiny-in-india)). The probe appears to have been triggered partly by Digital South Trust, a Bengaluru blockchain-research outfit that bought a virtual Visa-backed RedotPay card in August, ran domestic transactions, and handed the evidence to regulators ([Coin Edition](https://coinedition.com/why-are-offshore-crypto-cards-facing-rbi-and-fiu-scrutiny-in-india)). The global backdrop explains the urgency: Visa's CEO said on its October 27 earnings call that the network now carries more than 225 stablecoin-linked card programmes, up over 70% year on year, with volume up more than 150% across 5 million cards in over 50 countries ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/11/02/crypto-cards-india-rbi-fiu-scrutinise-redotpay-visa-mastercard-stablecoin-spending)).

For consumers the lesson is blunt. A ₹5,000-crore probe against a platform you trusted with your money is not a hypothetical; users of blocked cards this week discovered that a wallet balance with no Indian regulatory custody can become unreachable overnight, with no ombudsman, no chargeback scheme and no FEMA recourse. Using a crypto card for undisclosed cross-border spend is also, precisely, the trail the FIU is now reconstructing. The legitimate version of what these products promise — investing abroad, paying foreign merchants — exists at the sanctioned doors described above, at the cost of paperwork and disclosed FX spreads rather than the cost of an enforcement action.

## 3. RBI's paperwork amnesty for service exporters: the quiet, genuinely good one

Buried under the enforcement headlines, the RBI notified the Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2026 on November 2 — Notification No. FEMA 23(R)/(2)/2026-RB, effective retrospectively from October 1, 2026 ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-relaxes-fema-edf-idpms-reporting-rules-services-october-2026.html); [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/banks/rbi-eases-fema-reporting-norms-for-service-exporters-importers-sets-rs-10-lakh-threshold-14067952.html); [LiveMint](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/rbi-amends-edf-rules-for-service-exporters-freelancers-under-fema-10-lakh-threshold-relief-for-resident-individuals-11793665732487.html)). Three changes matter. Resident individuals exporting services no longer file a separate Export Declaration Form at all — sharing payment details with the authorised dealer bank for account credit counts as deemed submission. Other exporters get the same relief for inward remittances up to ₹10 lakh. And on the import side, IDPMS reporting is now required only where the importer is not a resident individual *and* the invoice or contract value exceeds ₹10 lakh ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/rbi-fema-rules-service-export-import-reporting-2026-10949208); [News On Air](https://newsonair.gov.in/rbi-simplifies-forex-reporting-rules-for-small-value-service-exports-and-imports)). The amendment revises the principal regulations of January 13, 2026, and follows a first amendment on September 22 ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/rbi-fema-rules-service-export-import-reporting-2026-10949208)).

This is the most consumer-positive cross-border development of the week, because its beneficiaries are the exact people the formal system serves worst: freelancers, designers, consultants and IT contractors billing foreign clients, for whom a missing EDF can freeze an inward remittance at the bank. The honest caveat is the eternal one — deemed submission is not exemption. FEMA obligations on the underlying transaction survive, and the payment trail your bank now holds is complete without the form; keep invoices and remittance records clean. But the direction is right, and worth noting as a pattern: in the same fortnight the regulator raided unlicensed remitters and licensed new ones, it also made the compliant path cheaper for the smallest users. Deregulation of paperwork and enforcement against bypass routes are two halves of one policy.

## 4. UPI acceptance goes e-commerce: Burj Khalifa and Sentosa, bookable from a UPI app

On October 30, the Finance Ministry announced that Indian travellers can now book two flagship attractions with UPI apps: At the Top, Burj Khalifa — the first UAE attraction to accept UPI for e-commerce, enabled by NIPL's partnership with NEOPAY and Emaar Entertainment — and Singapore's Resorts World Sentosa (Universal Studios Singapore, the Singapore Oceanarium, Adventure Cove Waterpark), via NIPL's tie-up with FOMO Pay ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/india/upi-payments-now-accepted-for-online-bookings-at-burj-khalifa-resorts-world-sentosa-14064767.html); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/uae-singapore-attractions-indians-can-now-book-tickets-using-upi-1.500730940); [DD India](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/10/upi-enabled-for-online-bookings-at-burj-khalifa-resorts-world-sentosa)). The underlying partnerships were announced earlier — UAE on July 29, Singapore on September 9 — and the integrations have now gone live ([DD India](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/10/upi-enabled-for-online-bookings-at-burj-khalifa-resorts-world-sentosa)).

The significance is in the word *online*. UPI's international acceptance story so far has been physical — scan the merchant's QR at the counter. E-commerce acceptance is a different integration class: the merchant's booking engine must handle a foreign real-time rail, currency conversion, and — crucially — the refund path, which for a cancelled Sentosa ticket does not run back through UPI. For consumers, booking in rupees with no card forex markup on a ₹12,000 theme-park day is a real saving, and paying through a familiar two-factor flow avoids foreign checkout fraud surfaces. But read the terms before assuming the markup is zero: the payer's own PSP applies cross-border transaction charges on UPI remittances abroad, and refund timelines will live in the merchant's policy, not in NPCI's dispute machinery. This is also where last week's plumbing note lands: FOMO Pay's adoption of BNY's 24/7 USD settlement is what keeps the Sentosa corridor instant beyond the payer's screen. Acceptance launches make headlines; settlement legs decide whether they survive contact with volume.

## 5. Zimbabwe's October 31 came and went — silently

Last week's lead story carried a date: Zimbabwe central bank governor John Mushayavanhu told Bloomberg that talks with NIPL on adopting UPI as the backbone of a new national payment system could conclude by October 31 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-05/zimbabwe-central-bank-seeks-india-s-npci-deal-to-boost-digital-payments); [Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/rbz-to-introduce-a-new-platform-for-digital-payments)). As of November 5, no signing has been announced by either side — no RBZ statement, no NIPL release, no follow-up in Zimbabwean or Indian coverage. The deadline, recall, was for concluding negotiations, not launching a service, and payment-infrastructure talks routinely overshoot their announcement dates; the Herald's Sunday explainer was still describing a *plan* for a shared platform to cut transaction costs ([Herald/Sunday Mail](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/our-plan-for-a-shared-payment-system-to-cut-transaction-costs)). The absence of news is itself the finding: sovereign UPI builds (Namibia, Peru, Trinidad & Tobago) take quarters, not weeks, between MOU and mobile wallet ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-payments-authority-inks-deal-develop-payments-system-trinidad-tobago-2024-09-27)). Watch for what goes live and under whose governance — fraud liability, dispute timelines and data rules will be written in Harare, and those clauses, not the press-release date, determine whether Zimbabweans actually get cheaper transfers.

## The pattern: build the sanctioned door, then lock the window

Set the week's five stories side by side and the policy architecture is unusually legible. India is expanding the compliant capacity of its cross-border plumbing — two GIFT City PSP licences in eight days, a REIT/InvIT depository-receipts channel, EDF relief for freelancers, e-commerce UPI acceptance in two more markets — while simultaneously raising the cost of the bypass: ₹5,000 crore in ED probes, prepaid crypto cards bricked mid-purchase. The bet is that if the sanctioned doors are good enough, users stop using windows. The evidence on that bet is mixed — LRS outflows fell in FY26 — but the direction is not. For consumers, the practical translation: the legitimate route to everything the stablecoin corridor promises now exists and is getting cheaper to use, and the illegitimate route is getting both expensive and traceable. The week told you which side of that line every product stands on; choose accordingly, and read the FX spread either way.
