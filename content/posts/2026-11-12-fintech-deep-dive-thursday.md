# Fintech Deep Dive — Thursday | November 12, 2026

**International & Cross-Border | Developments: November 5 – November 12, 2026**

Last week's deep dive described India's cross-border policy as one system pulling in two directions — sanctioned doors opening, unsanctioned windows getting raided. Week two confirms the pattern and adds something new: the consumer's own ledger. Dubai Duty Free started paying Indians to use UPI, SAIL and Zydus asked to move their treasury operations into the sanctioned zone, and the interest bill on the year's $133 billion diaspora deposit haul came due. On the other side of the border, the Enforcement Directorate's API forensics reached India's biggest crypto exchange and its tokenised US stocks, while — in the same week — investors holding *real* US stocks through a Bengaluru fintech discovered ₹100 crore of Apple and Nvidia had been swapped into a Chinese microcap without their consent. One week, both halves of the cross-border promise: cheaper sanctioned rails, and the full cost of everything else.

## 1. Dubai Duty Free pays you to use UPI: international acceptance enters its discount era

On November 5, NPCI International announced that Indian travellers paying through UPI at Dubai Duty Free get a 10% discount on eligible purchases, capped at AED 100 — roughly ₹2,400 — across the retailer's terminals at Dubai International and Al Maktoum International airports, with Network International as the acquiring partner ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/personal-finance/upi-payments-dubai-duty-free-what-indian-travellers-need-to-know-20027855.htm); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/dubai-duty-free-to-offer-discounts-on-upi-payments/articleshow/135564295.cms); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/indian-travellers-discount-upi-payments-dubai-duty-free)). Travellers must activate UPI International or UPI Global in their UPI app and link an eligible Indian bank account; NIPL has been working the UAE market through Network International, NeoPay and Magnati, and UPI is already accepted at Lulu Hypermarket, with rollouts across hospitality, retail and travel ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/indian-travellers-discount-upi-payments-dubai-duty-free); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/shopping-in-dubai-just-got-cheaper-for-indian-travellers-if-they-pay-by-upi-heres-how-565880-2026-11-05)).

The significant word is *discount*. UPI's international acceptance story so far has been an availability story — scan the QR, pay in rupees. Subsidising the transaction is phase two of the acceptance playbook, and it tells you about the economics: acceptance partnerships don't self-sustain on volume, so NIPL is buying habit formation in the single richest retail corridor for Indian travellers, where duty-free margins are fat enough to fund a 10% giveaway. AED 100 is comparable to the forex markup a card would have charged on a large purchase anyway, so the discount essentially refunds the consumer's cross-border cost — a genuinely good deal, with caveats. Your own PSP may levy cross-border charges on UPI international transactions, so read your app's terms before assuming the spread is zero; activation toggles and limits differ by app; and a duty-free refund does not travel back through UPI — it lives in the merchant's policy. Acceptance launches make headlines; the refund and dispute legs decide whether they survive contact with an actual shopping trip.

## 2. The ED kicks in the tokenised-stock window: CoinDCX and Delta Exchange under FEMA

Between November 4 and 5, the Enforcement Directorate's Bengaluru Zonal Office searched three premises under Section 37 of FEMA, targeting Neblio Technologies Private Limited (CoinDCX) and Excelium Technologies Private Limited (Delta Exchange India), alleging large-scale unauthorised cross-border transactions through stablecoins ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/ed-searches-coindcx-delta-exchange-offices-in-bengaluru-over-alleged-fema-violations-14073770.html); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-ed-coindcx-indian-users-trades-costa-rica-entity)). The alleged mechanics are now familiar from October's Mudrex and Instadapp searches, but at industrial scale: customer rupees converted into USDT, trades executed directly through APIs connecting to foreign exchanges — Binance, KuCoin, Gate.io — routing through accounts held by an entity registered in Costa Rica, described as a paper company used to circumvent cross-border transfer rules. The ED estimates spot volumes in the thousands of crores, alleges hundreds of crores paid as transaction fees to overseas exchanges in USDT, and flags crypto assets held in overseas enterprise accounts ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-ed-coindcx-indian-users-trades-costa-rica-entity); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/ed-raids-coindcx-delta-exchange-over-alleged-fema-violations-159980.html)). The product that stands out is tokenised foreign equity: CoinDCX marketed trading in "tokenised stocks" of Apple, Google and Tesla — one advertisement promised "Trade in US Stock Futures" starting at ₹100 — but the ED alleges these were derivative positions on Hyperliquid, a foreign decentralised exchange; customers never owned a share ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-ed-coindcx-indian-users-trades-costa-rica-entity); [TradingView/Coinpedia](https://www.tradingview.com/news/coinpedia:f989bf476094b:0-india-probes-coindcx-delta-exchange-over-alleged-offshore-crypto-trading-worth-thousands-of-crores)). The mandatory caveat: these are allegations under investigation, and Moneycontrol notes the agency has established no violations yet.

This is the third FEMA action against the stablecoin corridor in under five months — Transak and four others in June (₹2,500 crore alleged), Mudrex/Saber and Instadapp-linked entities in late October (₹4,600 crore plus $40 million) — and the new element is the product, not the plumbing. Tokenised US equity sold retail inside an Indian app sits in a regulatory crack by design: it is not a security settled in India, not a permissible overseas-investment route, and not a payment system. SEBI has no clean hook, RBI sees a FEMA breach, and the customer's "Apple share" is a derivative on a protocol with no Indian counterparty. For consumers the arithmetic is brutal: no demat statement, no ombudsman jurisdiction, no investor-protection fund, and — if the ED freezes platform assets — no immediate access to whatever you thought you owned. The ₹100 ticket size is what made this mass-market, and API receipts are what made it visible. The same week the state was decoding synthetic Apple shares, real ones were vanishing too — which brings us to Stockal.

## 3. Stockal: real US stocks, a real fraud, and an accountability gap with no address

While the ED probed synthetic US stocks, investors holding the genuine article were discovering the other failure mode. Nearly 30 retail investors have alleged losses exceeding ₹100 crore after their US portfolios — Apple, Tesla, Amazon, Alphabet, Nvidia, AMD — were liquidated without consent on October 15, with proceeds allegedly used to buy 300,000 shares of Zhibao Technology, a thinly traded Chinese microcap, at an average of $1.05; the stock closed at $0.42 on October 23 ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/money/stockal-scam-nearly-30-investors-allege-rs-100-crore-losses-after-us-stock-holdings-sold-without-authorisation-11793899651230.html); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/stockal-links-26-affected-customer-accounts-to-drivewealth-breach-says-no-evidence-of-its-own-platform-being-hacked/82148.html); [Firstpost](https://www.firstpost.com/vantage/investors-lose-10-mn-in-stockal-scam-as-apple-nvidia-shares-swapped-heist-decoded-vantage-vd2283624)). The platform, Bengaluru-headquartered Stockal Securities (parent: Borderless), first acknowledged unauthorised activity, then linked 26 affected customer accounts to a breach at its US brokerage partner DriveWealth, said there was no evidence its own platform was hacked, and denied any liability, wrongdoing or negligence ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/stockal-links-26-affected-customer-accounts-to-drivewealth-breach-says-no-evidence-of-its-own-platform-being-hacked/82148.html); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/stockal-trading-row-another-investor-flags-unauthorised-sale-of-us-stocks-claims-1-mn-loss-565918-2026-11-05)). Borderless put the affected population at 13 accounts and $6.26 million in unauthorised trades, blaming DriveWealth ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/stockal-scam-borderless-says-13-accounts-affected-in-6-26-million-unauthorised-us-stock-trades-blames-drivewealth-14073461.html)) — against investors' claims of ~30 accounts and ₹100 crore. One aggrieved investor added a detail that should worry every user of such platforms: the CEO had told him the firm was "SEBI-registered" ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/stockal-ceo-told-me-the-firm-was-sebi-registered-says-aggrieved-investor-14071546.html)).

This is the cross-border consumer-rights nightmare in a single case file, because the custody chain runs through jurisdictions that don't answer to the victim: Indian investor → Bengaluru fintech → US broker → US markets. When the chain breaks, SEBI's jurisdiction over an intermediary whose registration scope is itself disputed is unclear; DriveWealth answers to American regulators the investor never contracted with; and broker-failure insurance in the US covers custody failure, not necessarily fraud executed through the account stack. The investor's realistic remedies are a civil suit in India against a platform that says "not us," or arbitration in a country they've never visited. Set it beside story two and the lesson sharpens: the ED's tokenised-stock world is at least *legible* to Indian law — FEMA gives the state a hook — while the compliant-looking LRS route leaves the investor holding a claim on a broker chain they cannot audit. Every US-investing app built on this structure deserves two questions before funding: where do my shares actually sit, and what precisely happens to them if the broker layer is breached? Stockal's investors are learning the answers after the fact.

## 4. GIFT City gathers corporate tenants while the FCNR interest bill comes due

On November 11, Steel Authority of India and the Zydus Group applied to set up global treasury centres at GIFT City, joining Adani in centralising corporate treasury and FX operations at the international financial centre ([BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/sail-zydus-apply-to-set-up-global-treasury-centres-at-gift-city/article71688513.ece); [DeshGujarat](https://deshgujarat.com/2026/11/11/sail-zydus-apply-for-global-treasury-centres-at-gift-city-report)). The quiet corporatisation of the IFSC story continues: first payment fintechs (Razorpay, Skydo with IFSCA licences), then NRI deposits, now the treasury desks of India's largest corporates.

Meanwhile the bill for the year's biggest sanctioned cross-border mobilisation started arriving. The RBI's special concessional swap window pulled in a record ~$133 billion of FCNR(B) diaspora deposits; the Economic Times reports that at average interest rates of 6.5–7.0%, the annual interest outgo is $8.6–9.3 billion, and treasury heads at six banks say at least half of it is unhedged — more than $4 billion of dollar exposure sitting on bank balance sheets annually, compounded by the RBI's new reserve requirement on forex derivatives ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/forex/fcnrb-interest-bill-to-keep-dollar-hedging-costs-high/articleshow/135667135.cms)). The reserves ledger shows the same flow in reverse: India's forex reserves fell $2.69 billion to $717.06 billion in the week ended October 30, with the drawdown from September's $785.7 billion peak attributed partly to FCNR(B) fund deployment and RBI dollar sales supporting the rupee ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/economy/indicators/indias-forex-reserves-fall-2-6-billion-to-717-billion-as-of-october-30/articleshow/135606176.cms)). None of this is a scam — it is the sanctioned doorway's true cost structure. The state borrowed stability with a subsidised swap; banks now carry a multi-year interest liability whose hedging cost will surface in NRI loan pricing and corporate FX spreads. Consumers meeting "GIFT City" as a brand — in investing apps, in deposit pitches — should know the plumbing below the branding carries real, billable risk that someone will eventually pass through.

## 5. Project Faro: 21 banks, $270 million, and a dollar stablecoin with India's name nowhere on it

Bloomberg reported on November 10 that Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Banco Santander and Deutsche Bank are among 21 financial institutions that have committed about $270 million — funding five years of operations — to a US-incorporated stablecoin issuer known internally as Project Faro, targeting a dollar-pegged token in the first half of 2027, with other G7 currencies to follow and the euro as priority ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/goldman-citi-among-banks-in-270-million-stablecoin-backing); [CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/goldman-sachs-citi-270m-stablecoin-venture); [Blockhead](https://www.blockhead.co/2026/11/11/goldman-sachs-deutsche-among-banks-putting-270-million-behind-project-faro)). The coalitions are overlapping and crowded: Stripe-led Open USD, with Visa and Mastercard among founding supporters, went live in October; European banks are backing Qivalis; and Tether plus Circle still control roughly 85% of global stablecoin supply. Meanwhile the corridor where India's enforcement keeps finding the money keeps growing — TRON's USDT float hit $94.2 billion on November 9, up $16 billion in a year, precisely because cheap dollar transfers are what remittance-sized users want ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/tron-stablecoin-market-cap-16-billion-growth); [Cointelegraph via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:6bb225732094b:0-global-banks-commit-270m-to-stablecoin-venture-targeting-2027-launch)).

For India, Project Faro sharpens a dilemma the week's other stories already exposed. Global regulated-issuer coalitions will make dollar tokens bank-grade infrastructure in corridors India cannot police — while Indian agencies keep meeting the same USDT at every FEMA raid, from Mudrex to CoinDCX. India's answer so far is asymmetric: a retail CBDC pilot, UPI internationalisation, and FEMA enforcement sticks — but no stablecoin issuance framework, no admission route for foreign stablecoins, and a central bank historically hostile to private digital currency. The realistic forecast is that GENIUS-compliant bank coins will not be legally spendable in India, which guarantees the grey corridor stays busy, because the demand it serves — fast, cheap, dollar-denominated value transfer — is not going away. The policy question this week poses quietly: does India build a sanctioned stablecoin door, most plausibly in GIFT City under IFSCA, before the window trade becomes too entrenched to close?

## The pattern: two doors, one border, and the consumer's ledger

Set the week's five stories in a row and the architecture from last week holds, one level deeper. The sanctioned door got corporate tenants (SAIL, Zydus) and a marketing budget (Dubai Duty Free's 10%); its bill arrived too, in the shape of a $133 billion deposit base costing banks up to $9 billion a year to service. The windows got raided with better forensics — API-level tracing through a Costa Rican paper entity — and the consumer ledger recorded damage on *both* sides of the line: ₹100 crore allegedly gone from compliant-looking real-stock accounts, and a multi-billion-dollar contingent liability silently accruing under sanctioned deposits. The through-line for anyone moving money across India's border: every product is now either licensed-and-traceable or enforcement-bait, and the dangerous category is the middle one — global-sounding products whose custody chain nobody can explain in one sentence. Stockal's investors could not answer "where are my shares?" before October 15. Ask the custody question first; the discount comes second.
