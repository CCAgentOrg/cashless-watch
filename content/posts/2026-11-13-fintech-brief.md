# Fintech Brief — November 13, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### NPCI's Vyapaar Saksham: MDR money is already being budgeted — for small shops

NPCI has notified the [UPI Vyapaar Saksham Scheme](https://www.cnbctv18.com/business/finance/npci-notifies-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-push-small-merchant-acceptance-to-be-funded-from-upi-mdr-20032322.htm), a circular dated November 11 reported widely on Thursday, that will pay banks to put physical QR codes and soundboxes in front of small merchants. The scheme is funded by [a contribution from members' UPI MDR collections — 5% of their share — and runs for three years from the date UPI MDR is implemented](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/upi-vyapaar-saksham). That date, notably, still doesn't exist: the 0.4% MDR on P2M transactions above ₹2,000 was due October 15, deferred past Deepavali, and remains unnotified. Acquiring banks get [₹300 per physical QR code and ₹750 per soundbox](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/npci-introduces-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-boost-digital-payments-among-small-merchants-offers-incentives-for-qr-codes-and-soundboxes/135771249) deployed at eligible new small merchants — turnover up to ₹40 lakh, concentrated in Tier 3–6 centres, the North-East, J&K and Ladakh — who must log at least 25 eligible transactions (UPI payments up to ₹2,000) in any two months of a quarter with a minimum ₹5,000 monthly value. The fund splits 30/50/20 across onboarding, transaction growth and incremental growth in target geographies, and NPCI expects it to drive 6–10 million new QR codes and 0.5–1 million soundboxes a year, with 30–50% annual transaction growth among existing small merchants. A bank onboarding 1,000 merchants with 1,000 QRs and 200 soundboxes [could earn ₹4.5 lakh in incentives](https://thelivenagpur.com/2026/11/12/npci-introduces-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-boost-digital-payments-among-small-merchants). Claims can't double-dip with other institutional programmes such as NABARD's.

Consumer takeaway: the machinery for MDR is being quietly built even as the rollout date stays secret — and the small merchants this scheme targets are the same ones who stay outside the MDR charge. One caution cut both ways: the circular explicitly excludes [artificially split payments engineered to stay under ₹2,000](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/npci-introduces-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-boost-qr-code-soundbox-adoption-among-small-merchants-633372), so don't let a merchant talk you into splitting a bill "for the offer".

### RBI readies curbs on private equity controlling multiple NBFCs

The Reserve Bank of India is working on proposals that would [prevent private equity firms from holding a controlling stake in more than one non-bank lender](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/rbi-to-ban-private-equity-firms-from-controlling-multiple-non-bank-lenders-126111200692_1.html), Reuters reported Thursday citing two people with knowledge of the plans. The stated logic is contagion: stress at one lender shouldn't ripple through every other lender under the same investor. The framework would draw a cleaner line between control and investment — holdings of [15% or less would count as financial investment and attract less scrutiny](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/rbi-plans-to-ban-private-equity-firms-from-holding-controlling-stakes-in-multiple-nbfcs-what-will-change/articleshow/135761245.cms), while a 25% stake in a listed company (or a smaller one that buys board control) would be treated as control. A draft will go through public consultation and could be formally issued early next year; the RBI declined comment. The Indian Venture and Alternate Capital Association has lobbied for group entities to be allowed stakes above 20% in multiple NBFCs — [Siddarth Pai of 3one4 Capital](https://www.etnownews.com/markets/rbi-new-rules-private-equity-firms-multiple-nbfcs-ownership-restrictions-article-156498245) points out that PE and VC funds want fintech and financial-services exposure across their portfolios but keep hitting ownership thresholds.

Consumer takeaway: this targets investor structures, not borrowers — but ownership concentration in lending is a consumer issue too. When one sponsor's troubles cascade across a portfolio of lenders, it's deposit-free but loan-dependent households that feel the tightening first.

### Diwali by the numbers: 32% more UPI payments, barely any more money

UPI logged [916.9 million transactions on Diwali (November 8), up 32% from 695.7 million a year earlier](https://thefederal.com/category/business/upi-volume-jumps-32-per-cent-on-diwali-262459), per NPCI data. But value rose to ₹90,029 crore from ₹88,351 crore — [an increase of under 2%](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/upi-transactions-soar-32-on-diwali-to-916-9-million/57839920261110). The day before Diwali saw 975.8 million transactions worth ₹1,36,118 crore, and October closed at a record 25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore. UPI is now accepted in 11 countries, with Uzbekistan the latest entry.

Consumer takeaway: volumes are growing far faster than value, which means everyday payments are getting smaller and more frequent — more chai, fewer gold coins. It also quietly reshapes the MDR debate: if average tickets keep shrinking, a larger share of transactions sits below the ₹2,000 threshold where the fee won't apply.

### Norwest closes $4 billion fund — and India is a named target

Norwest has closed [Norwest 18 at $4 billion, the largest fund in its 65-year history](https://yourstory.com/2026/11/norwest-closes-4b-fund-to-back-ai-healthcare-and-financial-infra), backing tech, healthcare and consumer companies across the US, India and Israel, with [a sharpened India focus on manufacturing and mechatronics](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/norwest-raises-4-billion-for-18th-global-fund-sharpens-india-focus-on-manufacturing-mechatronics-14078350.html). The context matters: Indian AI startups raised $1.6 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up from $677 million a year earlier, but funding rounds fell 43% — four large deals accounted for 72% of the total.

Consumer takeaway: more dry powder is pointed at India, but it's concentrating into fewer, bigger cheques for infrastructure plays. Watch which consumer-facing categories get starved while the megafunds chase models and factories.
