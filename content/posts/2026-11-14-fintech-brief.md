# Fintech Brief — November 14, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### Paytm's wallet gets a second life: RBI grants in-principle approval

Two-and-a-half years after the RBI's crackdown on Paytm Payments Bank took India's once-largest wallet off the field, the central bank has granted [in-principle authorisation to Paytm Payments Services Ltd (PPSL)](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/indias-paytm-gets-preliminary-rbi-nod-for-wallet-licence-shares-hit-record-high/article71697554.ece) to set up and operate a payment system for issuing prepaid payment instruments — including digital wallets. Paytm's shares hit a record ₹1,960 on the announcement. The nod is conditional, and the conditions are telling: [an audit under the RBI's Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank Payment System Operators, dated November 5, 2026](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-economics/paytm-wallet-services-rbi-approval-conditions-explained-10962025), plus demonstrated compliance with the April 6, 2018 circular on storing payment system data in India. Wallets return only after final authorisation, which is not automatic.

Consumer takeaway: a fourth big wallet (alongside PhonePe, Google Pay and Amazon Pay) reviving adds competition — good for cashbacks and merchant offers. But the long pause and the audit-first conditions are a reminder that wallet money is float held on a company's books, not a deposit; treat wallet balances as spendable, not storable.

### RBI fines Mobikwik ₹9 lakh — for a missing feature and late fraud reports

The Reserve Bank has imposed a [₹9,00,000 penalty on One MobiKwik Systems](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/companies/rbi-imposes-9-lakh-penalty-mobikwik-compliance-violations/56119834), disclosed to exchanges on November 13, for two contraventions of the Master Direction on Prepaid Payment Instruments: the company failed to provide the mandated facility for pre-registered beneficiaries, and it delayed submissions to the Central Payments Fraud Information Registry (CPFIR) — the sector-wide database meant to surface recurring fraud patterns. The amount is small by corporate standards, but the violations point at two things consumers actually depend on: transaction safeguards on wallet transfers, and timely fraud reporting that lets other banks spot a scam pattern before it reaches them.

Consumer takeaway: CPFIR delays are invisible when they happen and expensive when they compound — fraud patterns travel precisely because everyone reports fast. And the pre-registered beneficiary mechanism exists to add friction before money leaves your wallet; if your wallet app doesn't offer beneficiary controls, that's a question worth asking the provider.

### A decade-old fraud, a fresh refund: Delhi consumer court enforces RBI's zero-liability rule

A Delhi District Consumer Commission (South) has ordered a private bank to [refund ₹70,084 lost by a customer to online fraud in July 2016](https://www.newindianexpress.com/cities/delhi/2026/Nov/13/online-fraud-consumer-court-orders-bank-to-pay-delhi-woman-rs-95000), plus ₹25,000 in compensation and litigation costs. The commission held that under the RBI circular on customer liability, in a third-party breach where neither bank nor customer is at fault, liability rests with the bank when the customer reports within three working days — which she did, by email the day after the ₹77,114 of unauthorised debits. The bank never reversed the transaction, levied penalty charges instead, and couldn't establish any negligence on her part. The bench also leaned on a Bombay High Court precedent: the burden of proving customer liability in unauthorised electronic transactions rests entirely on the bank.

Consumer takeaway: the RBI's limited-liability framework has teeth, even ten years late. The operational lesson is the three-working-day window — the moment you see an unauthorised debit, notify the bank in writing (email, not just a call), block the card, and file a police complaint. Paper trails are what win these cases.

On the payments-bank side, the RBI approved [Pratap Singh as MD & CEO of Fino Payments Bank](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/rbi-approves-appointment-of-pratap-singh-as-md-ceo-of-fino-payments-bank/article71696525.ece) for three years from November 27, 2026, ending the interim-CEO stint that began in February.

Consumer takeaway: celebrity capital keeps flowing into consumer-adjacent platforms rather than core fintech lending — a quieter week for funding overall, with no large rounds in payments or credit in the last 24 hours.
