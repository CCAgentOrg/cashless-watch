# Fintech Deep Dive — Sunday | November 15, 2026

*Weekly review | Developments reported November 8–15, 2026*

The week the fee didn't land — and everything built around it did. UPI's merchant discount rate was due "immediately after Diwali," Diwali came on November 8, and as of this Sunday morning no effective date has been notified. Yet in the same seven days, NPCI issued a scheme that spends the fee's proceeds before the fee exists, Paytm got its wallet licence back, Razorpay cleared its IPO, the Supreme Court moved to make banks pay for fraud-detection failures, and Blackstone wrote the largest primary cheque into a listed NBFC in the same week the RBI drafted rules to limit exactly that kind of cheque. Here are the five stories that will shape December.

## 1. The MDR date that keeps not existing — while its money is already budgeted

The 0.4% merchant fee on UPI person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000 (capped at ₹300 for payments of ₹75,000 and above) was originally due October 15, deferred past Deepavali with a promise of a date "immediately" after, and remains unnotified despite sources telling Zee Business a decision was likely by November 15 — today ([Zee Business](https://www.zeebiz.com/india/news-upi-mdr-decision-on-charges-for-payments-above-rs-2000-likely-by-november-15-say-sources-405479); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/business/Economy/two-days-past-deepavali-npci-yet-to-announce-a-date-for-upi-mdr-rollout/article71689098.ece/amp)). The Financial Express reported sources pointing to a possible slip to January 1, 2027 — unconfirmed, but the pattern of the rollout now looks like a deliberate glide path: notify the scheme that spends the money first, then announce the fee ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/upi-charges-after-diwali-will-customers-pay-extra-on-payments-above-2-000-npcis-mdr-rollout-likely-in-nov-11794289795387.html)).

That scheme is the week's most telling document. NPCI's November 11 circular establishes the **UPI Vyapaar Saksham Scheme**, funded by a contribution of 5% of every participating bank's UPI MDR collections and running three years from the date MDR is implemented — a date that does not exist yet ([NPCI via CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/business/finance/npci-notifies-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-push-small-merchant-acceptance-to-be-funded-from-upi-mdr-20032322.htm); [Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/upi-vyapaar-saksham)). Acquiring banks earn ₹300 per new physical QR code and ₹750 per soundbox deployed at new small merchants with turnover up to ₹40 lakh, concentrated in Tier 3–6 centres, the North-East, J&K and Ladakh, plus volume-linked incentives that run to 0.2% on incremental growth ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/npci-introduces-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-boost-digital-payments-among-small-merchants-offers-incentives-for-qr-codes-and-soundboxes/135771249)). NPCI expects 6–10 million new QR codes and 0.5–1 million soundboxes a year from it.

Read the scheme as an admission. Nearly seven years of zero-MDR was supposed to prove acceptance infrastructure could grow without merchant fees; the programme that funds soundboxes in small-town India is explicitly conditioned on the fee arriving. The industry has done the arithmetic publicly: Pine Labs CEO Amrish Rau told analysts MDR could unlock ₹80–230 crore of annual revenue and committed to deploying roughly 10 lakh soundboxes to monetise merchant UPI it had written off; Paytm has pegged its own windfall near ₹700 crore, with analysts running figures as high as ₹1,160 crore ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-upi-mdr-annual-revenue-pine-labs); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/paytm-gets-rbi-prelim-authorisation-to-operate-wallets-shares-surge-to-52-week-high/articleshow/135798025.cms)). The Supreme Court side of the fight resumes November 23, when the bench in the digital-arrest suo motu case has also asked RBI to file its list of authorised institutions — and NPCI's counter-affidavit stands on the argument that ~96% of merchant payments are unaffected by the fee ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/digital-arrest-scams-sc-seeks-prevention-plan-from-whatsapp-others-sends-notice-to-rbi/articleshow/135708591.cms)).

**Consumer stake:** until a date is notified, nothing changes at checkout — and even after it does, the fee sits on the merchant's side of the counter. NPCI has told the Supreme Court that pass-through is a "mandatory and binding direction" enforceable up to termination. If a shop adds "UPI charges" to a bill above ₹2,000 today, that is a business choice, not a rule — and the complaint route is the RBI Integrated Ombudsman.

## 2. The record month that reframes the fee: volume is small, value is person-to-person

UPI closed October at 25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore — the first month past ₹30 lakh crore, average daily value crossing ₹1 lakh crore for the first time, 758 live banks, average ticket ₹1,238 ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-upi-p2m-p2p-transactions-october-2026); [NPCI](https://www.npci.org.in/product/upi/product-statistics)). Then Diwali landed inside the MDR's own news cycle: 916.9 million transactions on November 8, up 32% year-on-year, but value up under 2% to ₹90,029 crore; the day before Diwali set a volume record at 975.8 million worth ₹1,36,118 crore; Dhanteras logged ₹1.37 lakh crore, the second-highest day ever; and the four-day festive window moved 3.75 billion transactions worth ₹4.99 lakh crore, up 28% in volume and 38% in value ([The Hindu BusinessLine/PTI](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/upi-transactions-rise-32-on-diwali-2026-to-9169-million/article71686144.ece); [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/startups/upi-volume-value-post-strong-growth-during-diwali-as-festive-spending-lights-up-digital-payments/articleshow/135688534.cms); [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/business/upi-volume-jumps-32-per-cent-on-diwali-262459)).

The composition is the argument. Merchant payments were 63.2% of October's volume but only 30.8% of value; person-to-person transfers carried 69.2% of all money moved — and P2P stays free at any amount under the framework. Meanwhile the festive pattern shows tickets shrinking: 32% more payments, barely any more money. Both facts cut toward the consumer side of the MDR debate — the transactions most of India actually uses sit below ₹2,000 or outside the merchant rail entirely, which is precisely the band the fee framework leaves alone. The caution flag sits in reliability: technical declines among the top-20 remitter banks rose to 0.24% in September, and at 25 billion transactions a month even that rate means tens of millions of stuck payments a month ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-upi-technical-decline-rate-rises-september-2026)). A fee meant to fund infrastructure only works if the infrastructure fails less.

## 3. Paytm's wallet second life and Razorpay's IPO clearance: the regulatory-reconciliation trade

Two years after the action against Paytm Payments Bank took India's once-largest wallet off the field, RBI granted **Paytm Payments Services Ltd in-principle authorisation** on November 13 to set up a prepaid payment instrument business — including wallets — sending the stock to a record ₹1,960, up 49% year-to-date though still below its ₹2,150 IPO price ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/indias-paytm-gets-preliminary-rbi-nod-for-wallet-licence-shares-hit-record-high/article71697554.ece); [BW Disrupt](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/paytm-gets-rbi-in-principle-nod-digital-wallet-licence-moves-closer-to-wallet-revival-633559)). The conditions are the story: the authorisation requires an audit under RBI's Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank payment system operators dated November 5, 2026, and demonstrated compliance with the 2018 data-localisation circular — the same cyber rulebook that anchored Monday's developer deep dive ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-economics/paytm-wallet-services-rbi-approval-conditions-explained-10962025)). Final authorisation is not automatic. The commercial logic is visible in the recovery metrics: monthly transacting users rebuilt from ~7 crore during the crisis to 8.5 crore, UPI share back to 8.1%, and management signalling wallet-to-Postpaid integration on its Q2 call ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/paytm-s-upi-market-share-grows-by-one-percentage-point-to-8-percent-in-two-years-article-14078212.html)).

Razorpay, meanwhile, cleared the public-market gate: SEBI issued final observations on its confidentially-filed draft papers on November 3, disclosed November 13, clearing an IPO pegged at ₹5,000–6,000 crore targeting roughly $5 billion — a step down from the $7.5 billion of its 2021 private round, and part of this year's pattern of fintech listings repricing 2021 marks ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/razorpay-moves-closer-to-public-market-debut-as-sebi-gives-green-signal-to-ipo/articleshow/135809192.cms); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/razorpay-gets-sebi-approval-for-rs-6000-cr-ipo-in-india/articleshow/135810458.cms); [BW Disrupt](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/razorpay-gets-sebi-nod-for-rs-5-000-6-000-cr-ipo-listing-likely-in-q1-2027-633553)). Shareholders approved a fresh issue of up to ₹2,700 crore in May; Axis Capital, Kotak, JPMorgan and Citi are the bankers; listing is eyed for January–March 2027 after a 21-day public-comment window on the updated draft.

Together these are the same trade from opposite directions: Paytm's valuation is recovering because the regulator that shut its bank is now licensing its subsidiary, and Razorpay is approaching the market at a haircut because public investors now price 2021 fintech marks skeptically. Both suggest the sector's reckoning is settling into something mundane — compliance as a growth input, and honest pricing — rather than a collapse.

## 4. Stress tests for the fraud-recovery machine, at every joint

The week's fraud stories formed one connected system, and each joint leaked. At the top, the Supreme Court's order in the digital-arrest suo motu case (uploaded November 10; next hearing November 23) converts compensation from an RBI suggestion into a liability: financial institutions must compensate victims where fraud succeeds because of a due-diligence failure, and the RBI must file enforceable provisions — not advisories — plus a list of all authorised institutions ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/digital-arrest-scams-sc-seeks-prevention-plan-from-whatsapp-others-sends-notice-to-rbi/articleshow/135708591.cms); [New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Nov/10/supreme-court-tells-banks-to-pay-victims-for-cyber-fraud-losses-if-found-negligent)). The court noted cybercrime reports have fallen roughly half this year, but victims still lost about ₹800 crore.

Below that, Mumbai Mirror reported the Enforcement Directorate is investigating **12 officials — police, GST and income-tax, many posted to cyber-fraud units — for unfreezing fraudster mule accounts** in a network worth an estimated ₹28,000 crore, with bribes routed through angadias. The recovery infrastructure itself was for sale. At the layer above money movement, Telangana's cyber bureau convened NBFCs and banks on November 12 after AI-assisted attacks that study an NBFC's payment plumbing for 10–12 days before exploiting whitelisted IDs and payment API credentials — and RBI's November 3 advisory now mandates bank inventories of every NBFC API client, CERT-In-empanelled audits (new NBFCs before API access, existing ones by February 3, 2027) and beneficiary whitelisting or maker-checker on API debits by December 3, 2026 ([The Head & Tale](https://theheadandtale.com/deepdives/exclusive-rbi-tightens-api-security-rules-for-banks-after-nbfc-cyberattacks-mandates-audits-beneficiary-checks/); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-rbi-cyber-security-rules-payment-companies)). ET Now separately reported RBI is weighing a one-hour hold on person-to-person transfers above ₹10,000 — a design that trades immediacy for catchability, and whose final shape depends on feedback to the March 2026 discussion paper still under review.

Enforcement bit at the edges: RBI fined MobiKwik ₹9 lakh — small money, but the violations (no pre-registered beneficiary facility; delayed CPFIR fraud reporting) are the two controls that let pattern-fraud spread between banks ([scanX](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/companies/rbi-imposes-9-lakh-penalty-mobikwik-compliance-violations/56119834)). And a Delhi consumer commission ordered a bank to refund ₹70,084 from a **2016** fraud plus ₹25,000 compensation — proof the zero-liability framework has teeth even a decade late, and that the three-working-day written-report window is what wins these cases ([New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/cities/delhi/2026/Nov/13/online-fraud-consumer-court-orders-bank-to-pay-delhi-woman-rs-95000)). The through-line for consumers: reporting speed and paper trails remain the difference between the ₹800 crore the system lost and the refunds a handful of courts recovered.

## 5. Capital rotates into gold loans and NBFCs — just as RBI drafts rules to cap its owners

Blackstone-managed funds committed ₹5,000 crore (~$535 million) to IIFL Finance on November 10 via compulsorily convertible preference shares converting at ₹602 within 18 months for roughly 16% fully diluted, anchoring a ₹6,414 crore all-primary raise alongside ₹1,264 crore from co-promoters and ₹150 crore from Anchorage — lifting the lender's capital adequacy from about 17% toward 30% pro forma, aimed at gold-backed lending, and billed as one of the largest primary PE investments in a listed Indian NBFC ([The Hindu BusinessLine/Reuters](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/blackstone-to-buy-about-16-stake-in-iifl-finance-for-5000-crore/article71685978.ece); [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/companies/blackstone-iifl-finance-promoters-to-invest-rs-6-414-crore-in-nbfc-14076344.html)).

In the same week, Reuters reported RBI is drafting rules to **bar private equity from controlling more than one NBFC**, treating stakes of 15% or less as passive financial investments and drawing control at 25% or board dominance, with public consultation and possible issuance early next year ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/rbi-to-ban-private-equity-firms-from-controlling-multiple-non-bank-lenders-126111200692_1.html); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/rbi-plans-to-ban-private-equity-firms-from-holding-controlling-stakes-in-multiple-nbfcs-what-will-change/articleshow/135761245.cms)). February's conditional clearance of Bain-linked holdings up to 41.66% in Manappuram is the obvious template case. The timing is the analysis: PE is writing its biggest cheques into exactly the lenders RBI now worries about concentrating, and the ProposedOne-investor-one-NBFC rule — pitched as contagion containment — will define how the next IIFL-style raise can be structured. The broader funding backdrop sharpened the point: October fintech funding was just $76 million across six deals, the January–September total of $2.2 billion is up 14% year-on-year but two-thirds late-stage, Norwest closed a $4 billion fund with India named, and the week's largest cheque of all — Bodhi Tree's $180 million majority stake in Adyar Ananda Bhavan — went to restaurants, not fintech ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/fintech-funding-october-2026-indian-fintechs-raise-usd-76-million-across-6-deals/135737055); [BW Disrupt](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/how-india-s-fintech-growth-hinges-on-bridging-the-series-a-gap-632694); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/11/norwest-closes-4b-fund-to-back-ai-healthcare-and-financial-infra); [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-nov-09-nov-14-12808553)).

**Consumer stake:** when one sponsor's capital sits behind multiple lenders, a sponsor's bad quarter becomes every portfolio borrower's tighter terms. And the asset choice matters — gold loans pledge the household's jewellery, so competition among well-capitalized lenders is welcome, but aggressive doorstep solicitation is the failure mode to watch.

## The week ahead

November 23 brings the Supreme Court's next hearing, where the digital-arrest compensation framework, RBI's authorised-institutions list and WhatsApp's prevention proposals converge. Somewhere in between, NPCI must finally notify an MDR date — Zee Business's sources pointed at today — and when it does, Vyapaar Saksham's soundbox incentives switch on the same day. Razorpay's updated draft hit the public comment window next, giving merchants their first unfiltered look at gateway economics before a Q1 2027 listing. And RBI's NBFC-ownership draft, when published for consultation, will test whether the industry that just received the sector's largest PE cheque gets to keep the structure that received it.
