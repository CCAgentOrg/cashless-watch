# Fintech Brief — November 16, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### RBI's new cyber rulebook: payment companies must report incidents within 6 hours

The weekend's expert verdict is in on the RBI's consolidated [Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank payment system operators](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-rbi-cyber-security-rules-payment-companies), issued November 5: [analysts say the mandatory six-hour incident reporting window will force real investment in monitoring and incident response](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/rbis-6-hour-reporting-rule-to-help-curb-cyber-threats-fraud-in-digital-payments-experts/articleshow/135859708.cms). The rules cover everyone who runs payment rails without a banking licence — NPCI, card networks, payment aggregators, prepaid wallet issuers and white-label ATM operators. Operators must report cyberattacks, critical outages, internal fraud and settlement delays to the RBI within six hours of detection, alongside parallel reporting to CERT-In. Boards stay accountable for cyber risk, and disaster recovery must sit in a different seismic zone from primary data centres, with drills to prove recovery actually works. The direction consolidates and tightens the July 2024 framework whose compliance deadlines — April 2025 for large companies, April 2026 for medium ones — have just run out.

Consumer takeaway: when a payment app goes down or a fraud wave hits, the regulator now hears about it in hours, not weeks. And keep this rulebook in mind — it's the same one Paytm must now audit against.

### SEBI to write AI guardrails for the markets, with humans kept in the loop

Speaking at the India International Trade Fair in Delhi on Sunday, SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said the regulator [will soon issue detailed guidelines for responsible AI use in capital markets](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/sebi-plans-ai-guardrails-for-capital-markets-stresses-human-oversight-in-critical-decisions/articleshow/135852140.cms), centred on investor protection, disclosure requirements and accountability of regulated entities — with a "human-in-the-loop" approach for critical decisions. SEBI has also set up a working group to [make the Innovators Growth Platform more attractive to deep-tech and biotech startups](https://legal.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/regulators/sebi-to-issue-ai-usage-guidelines-for-capital-markets-focus-on-investor-protection-pandey/135857713) that need public money before profits. Separately, [Business Standard reports the regulator may revisit its promoter-classification framework](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-may-revisit-promoter-classification-framework-to-address-concerns-126111500769_1.html), responding to concern that the definition is too broad for today's ownership patterns.

Consumer takeaway: when your broker's "AI research" picks your stock, accountability should land on the regulated entity, not the algorithm — these guidelines are meant to make that explicit.

### Paytm's wallet comeback comes with real conditions attached

After Friday's record share high (₹1,960, still [about 9.8% below the ₹2,150 IPO price](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/paytm-wallet-likely-to-see-revival-following-rbi-s-in-principle-nod-126111301606_1.html)) on news of RBI's in-principle authorisation for [Paytm Payments Services to issue prepaid instruments](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indias-paytm-gets-rbi-preliminary-nod-pre-paid-instruments-including-wallet-2026-11-13), the fine print is now public. PPSL must complete a system and cybersecurity audit through a CERT-In empanelled auditor, ISACA-registered CISA, or ICAI-DISA holder — [scoped to the new November 5 cyber Master Directions](https://www.financialexpress.com/business/industry-paytm-gets-rbis-in-principle-approval-for-wallet-business-audit-due-in-6-months-4383635) — and file the report within six months, or the approval lapses automatically. Any shareholding change needs prior RBI approval. Wallet issuance begins only after final authorisation. The commercial stakes: [Paytm's UPI market share has slid to 8.1% (September 2026) from 11.8% in January 2024](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/paytms-wallet-comeback-can-it-reclaim-lost-ground/135820529), even as monthly transacting users hit 8.5 crore and quarterly GMV rose 26% to ₹7.1 lakh crore — all while posting a sixth consecutive profitable quarter (Q2 revenue up 28% to ₹2,648 crore).

Consumer takeaway: no wallet returns tomorrow — the audit gate stands between announcement and app store. When it does come back, compare what a prepaid wallet offers against plain UPI before loading money in; prepaid balances carry their own risks.

### RBI Deputy Governor: microfinance lenders must not squeeze vulnerable borrowers

Addressing the industry over the weekend, RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu [warned MFIs to lend responsibly as climate risks and global uncertainty batter low-income households](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/mfis-should-lend-responsibly-as-climate-risks-could-impact-borrowers-rbi-deputy-governor/article71705221.ece). He flagged that MFIs may be over-focused on existing customers at the expense of first-time borrowers — limiting financial inclusion rather than deepening it — and said self-regulatory organisations "should refrain from actions that could be construed as interpretations or clarifications of existing regulatory provisions." His remarks land as the industry lobbies to dilute the 50% loan-to-income cap and raise income thresholds for MFI loans.

Consumer takeaway: the loan-to-income cap is the main brake against repeat-loan spirals in microfinance. If it moves, watch who benefits — it won't be the borrower in a climate-stressed district.

### Funding wrap: institutional money picks digital lenders

It was a thin weekend for venture rounds, but digital lending drew steady institutional capital. Personal lending platform Olyv (formerly SmartCoin) raised [₹57.62 crore in an extended Series C led by Nippon India Digital (₹50 crore) with Fundamentum adding ₹7.62 crore](https://entrackr.com/exclusive/exclusive-olyv-raises-rs-58-cr-in-extended-series-c-round-led-by-nippon-india-digital-12803184) — a flat ₹1,104 crore valuation for a company that posted FY25 revenue of ₹338 crore and net profit of ₹26 crore. Listed lender Kissht's parent OnEMI completed a [₹832.2 crore preferential issue to 34 investors](https://m.economictimes.com/tech/funding/kissht-parent-onemi-completes-832-crore-preferential-issue-24-new-investors-join-existing-shareholders-add-stakes/articleshow/135728271.cms) — new names include Axis Mutual Fund schemes, 360 ONE, Bandhan Small Cap Fund, Citigroup Global Markets Hong Kong and MIT — adding roughly 13% to its equity, on the heels of crossing ₹10,000 crore in assets under management with Q2 net profit doubling to ₹125 crore.

Consumer takeaway: mutual funds and institutions are funding the digital-lending consolidation, so expect aggressive "instant loan" marketing to follow the money. Compare the total cost of credit, not just the EMI.
