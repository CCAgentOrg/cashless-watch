# Fintech Brief — November 19, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### AI debt collector's flaws exposed 6.6 million borrower accounts and 315,000 call recordings

Two security vulnerabilities in [CreditNirvana, the AI-driven debt-collection platform owned by fintech firm Perfios](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-debt-collection-service-exposed-66-million-accounts-and-315000-call-recordings), exposed roughly 6.6 million borrower accounts and about 315,000 recorded collection calls, according to research published November 18 by independent security researcher Eaton Zveare. The first flaw — a [hardcoded API key embedded in the client-side code of CreditNirvana's Agentic AI Call Center](https://receivablesinfo.com/2026/11/18/creditnirvana-ai-debt-collection-security-flaws) — gave anyone who found it access to call recordings, transcripts and AI-generated recommendations belonging to customers of Aditya Birla Capital Finance. The second, rated critical with a severity score of 9.3 and tracked as [CVE-2026-110475](https://mlq.ai/news/flaws-in-ai-debt-collector-creditnirvana-exposed-66-million-accounts-and-315000-call-recordings), was an authentication failure that permitted administrator-level logins to the main collections platform, exposing 3.4 million accounts at Aditya Birla Capital Finance and another 3.2 million across BharatPe and International Asset Reconstruction Company — complete with ID cards, credit reports, legal documents and emails and texts sent to borrowers. CERT-In rated one flaw high and the other critical; both were fixed in June after Zveare reported them, with public disclosure in October. Notably, the exposure is confirmed access — researchers did not establish that attackers actually exploited the flaws.

Consumer takeaway: debt collection is where your most embarrassing financial moments live — unpaid EMIs, recovery calls, court notices. If you borrowed from a lender that outsourced collections to AI platforms, your data's security now depends on a vendor chain you never signed up with. Ask lenders who processes your collections data and whether they audit those vendors.

### Wise's CEO challenges RBI's ₹25 lakh cap on cross-border fintech payments

Wise co-founder and CEO Kristo Käärmann has [publicly questioned the Reserve Bank of India's ₹25 lakh per-transaction cap on payments processed by cross-border fintech companies](https://www.livemint.com/companies/news/wise-ceo-questions-rbi-s-25-lakh-cap-on-cross-border-fintech-payments-11795003830944.html), saying "it's hard for Indian businesses to understand why the government is stopping them from getting paid." The cap applies to payment aggregator-cross border (PA-CB) companies, a category the RBI [created via a circular on 31 October 2023](https://www.livemint.com/companies/news/wise-ceo-questions-rbi-s-25-lakh-cap-on-cross-border-fintech-payments-11795003830944.html), replacing the older $10,000 export-receipt ceiling; the September 2025 master direction retained ₹25 lakh for both inward and outward flows. Any single export payment above the cap must route through a bank instead. Wise holds the licence for inward payments only — final authorisation on 12 March — and now moves roughly $15 billion a year into India, about 10% of the country's [FY26 inward remittances of $151.8 billion, up from $135.46 billion in FY25](https://www.livemint.com/companies/news/wise-ceo-questions-rbi-s-25-lakh-cap-on-cross-border-fintech-payments-11795003830944.html). Wise's India country manager Navin Jain conceded the regulator is being deliberately cautious with a new class of players, and that limits tend to loosen once AML and KYC behaviour is proven.

Consumer takeaway: if you're a freelancer or small exporter billing overseas clients, the practical effect today is simple — invoices above ₹25 lakh in one shot need a bank wire, with its paperwork and markups, while smaller invoices can ride fintech rails. Watch this space: the cap is the difference between "get paid like a local" and a branch visit.

### Government's banking reform panel opens consultations — fintechs on the witness list

The High-Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat has [begun stakeholder consultations and circulated questionnaires](https://www.bignewsnetwork.com/news/279441019/high-level-committee-on-banking-for-viksit-bharat-begins-stakeholder-consultations-on-banking-sector-reforms) across banks, NBFCs, development finance institutions, regulators, industry associations and fintech organisations, per an official statement on November 18. The panel — chaired by former Central Vigilance Commissioner Suresh N Patel, with [RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu and former RBI Deputy Governor N.S. Vishwanathan among its members](https://m.economictimes.com/industry/banking/finance/banking/govts-banking-reform-panel-seeks-stakeholder-inputs-on-making-lenders-future-ready/articleshow/135962830.cms) — was announced in the Union Budget 2026-27 and has six months to report. Its mandate covers an AI and digital innovation roadmap for banking "along with safeguards against cybersecurity risks," plus a review of the Priority Sector Lending framework and the Lead Bank Scheme. For the fintech sector, this is the rare structural chance to argue for clearer licensing, data-sharing rules and partnership norms directly into a reform blueprint — and the cybersecurity safeguards language lands days after the CreditNirvana exposure above demonstrated exactly the kind of third-party AI risk banking now carries.

Consumer takeaway: reform committees mostly hear from institutions, not borrowers. The PSL and Lead Bank reviews, though, decide how credit reaches small towns and priority sectors — outcomes worth tracking when the recommendations land next year.

### Funding note: Olyv extends Series C as PB Fintech plans a board transition

Digital lending platform Olyv has raised [₹57.62 crore in an extended Series C round led by new investor Nippon India Digital with ₹50 crore, and existing backer Fundamentum adding ₹7.62 crore](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/https-entrackr.com-_exclusive-funding-olyv-activity-7526551516657238016-fP1F), at a flat valuation of ₹1,104 crore per Entrackr's read of the regulatory filings. The Bengaluru-based lender — founded 2015, small-ticket unsecured personal loans — grew FY25 operating revenue 36% to ₹338 crore while [net profit more than doubled to ₹26 crore](https://www.vccircle.com/olyvcountai-lemonpeel-others-pocket-early-stage-cheques), and says the fresh capital funds an expansion beyond lending into broader financial services. Meanwhile, PB Fintech told the exchanges on November 18 that co-founder Alok Bansal will step down as Executive Vice Chairman and Whole Time Director on 31 March 2027, [continuing on the Policybazaar-Paisabazaar parent's board as a Non-Executive Director](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/POLICYBZR_18112026233346_PBFintechLimitedDisclosureReg3018112026.pdf) after 18 years building the company.

Consumer takeaway: profitable small-ticket lenders raising growth capital usually means more loan offers in your feed. The fee to watch is processing charges and effective interest on those "instant" personal loans — not the headline rate.
