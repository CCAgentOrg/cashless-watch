# Fintech Brief — November 21, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### CERT-In flags AI-driven payment fraud as bank security upgrades stall fintech payouts

India's cybersecurity watchdog has warned that attackers are now using AI to push fraudulent transactions through legitimate payment workflows — and the fallout is already visible in delayed payouts across the fintech stack. In an [advisory dated November 21](https://inc42.com/buzz/fintechs-bear-the-brunt-as-yes-bank-upgrades-ai-safeguards), CERT-In flagged unauthorised transactions at technology service providers handling government-linked payment processes and vendor-payout platforms: attackers altered business records or approval relationships, then used legitimate payment processes to approve fraudulent transfers, exploiting gaps in verification and reconciliation rather than payment APIs alone. The advisory catalogues the AI-assisted playbook — automated reconnaissance for exposed systems, rapid exploit preparation from software-update analysis, multiple agents working intrusion tasks in parallel, and misuse of valid credentials and trust relationships — and directs entities to restrict accounts that can approve payments, require independent certification for high-risk changes to payment records, and reconcile service-provider data against authoritative records. Meanwhile, [Inc42 reports Yes Bank's own AI-safeguard upgrades are delaying payouts at payment service providers](https://inc42.com/buzz/fintechs-bear-the-brunt-as-yes-bank-upgrades-ai-safeguards), with industry sources linking the tightening to RBI directions issued after the October data leak at SBI. The ripple effects this week: Razorpay temporarily delayed merchant settlements on November 20 over a partner-bank technical issue (restored within hours), while CoinDCX, ZebPay and Coinbase India have all flagged INR withdrawal delays traced to bank-side disruptions.

Consumer takeaway: a delayed settlement or withdrawal is more likely a bank security upgrade than theft — check the platform's status page before panicking, and never "verify" a stuck payment by sharing an OTP with anyone who calls you about it.

### SEBI promises five-day FPI onboarding and no-visit KYC for NRIs

At the CII-DFS Financing Summit in Mumbai on November 20, [SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said the regulator is working to cut foreign portfolio investor onboarding to as little as five business days](https://www.thehindu.com/business/markets/sebi-eyes-faster-fpi-onboarding-plans-fresh-reforms-to-attract-foreign-capital/article71723707.ece) — some FPIs under the Swagat framework have already been onboarded in five days. Eligible NRIs, OCI cardholders and foreign nationals can now [complete KYC and re-KYC digitally, without visiting India](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/sebi-plans-further-easing-of-fpi-rules-to-attract-foreign-capital-consultation-paper-soon/article71723454.ece), and from December 1, KYC review cycles for applicable FPIs stretch from one and three years to two and eight years, cutting repeat paperwork. Pandey also promised a consultation paper "very soon" to remove India-specific FPI constraints that other major markets don't impose, and flagged a coming market-making framework for corporate bonds. [SEBI is also exploring a domestically-governed AI model for the securities market](https://m.economictimes.com/news/india/sebi-explores-its-own-ai-model-plans-framework-to-boost-bond-trading-simplifies-nri-kyc/videoshow/136024533.cms), developed collaboratively through digital public infrastructure so that smaller regulated entities get affordable AI-based regulatory tools.

Consumer takeaway: if your KYC lapsed because you moved abroad, the fix may no longer require a flight to India — video verification is now the sanctioned route for re-KYC.

### NPCI rolls out UPI Vyapaar Saksham — ₹300 per QR code, ₹750 per soundbox for small merchants

NPCI has introduced the [UPI Vyapaar Saksham Scheme](https://www.livemint.com/money/npcis-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-boost-digital-payments-help-small-merchants-with-incentives-all-we-know-11795165732819.html) (circular dated November 11, detailed by Mint/ANI on November 20) to push UPI acceptance into Tier 3-6 towns, the north-eastern states, Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh, and merchants covered by notified central government schemes. The scheme pays ₹300 per physical QR code and ₹750 per soundbox deployed, with an incentive pool split 30% for new merchant onboarding, 50% for transaction growth (0.03% of eligible transaction value, with a 50% uplift in targeted geographies) and 20% for incremental growth (0.20% of transaction value above the previous quarter). Read it alongside the stalled UPI MDR rollout and the scheme's purpose is clear: the merchant discount rate proceeds are meant to bankroll exactly this kind of acceptance infrastructure, so small-town shopkeepers absorb MDR only in exchange for getting QR codes and soundboxes they currently lack.

Consumer takeaway: the trade being struck is that merchants' future MDR payments buy digital acceptance in small towns — whether the money actually reaches the last-mile store is what regulators should be made to prove.

### RBI desk: Fusion Finance fined ₹7.5 lakh, states get a bond-switch first, exporters get an EDF clarification

Three RBI actions from November 20. First, the central bank [imposed a ₹7.50 lakh penalty on micro-lender Fusion Finance](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=64015) (order dated November 17) for failing to obtain PAN or Form 60 from customers while opening account-based relationships, and for not escalating partly or wholly rejected complaints to its internal ombudsman within the prescribed time. Second, RBI notified its [first-ever switch auction of state government securities — ₹10,800 crore across five states on November 26](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/banks/rbi-to-conduct-first-state-govt-securities-switch-auction-worth-rs-10-800-crore-on-nov-26-14085877.html), letting Madhya Pradesh, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh swap bonds maturing in 2027-29 for longer-dated paper maturing 2034-44, smoothing near-term repayment obligations. Third, under this month's FEMA (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Second Amendment Regulations, RBI [clarified that the ₹10 lakh Export Declaration Form threshold for non-individual service exporters applies to each inward remittance](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/rbi-clarifies-10-lakh-edf-threshold-applies-to-each-inward-remittance-126112000851_1.html), not to aggregate receipts — a genuine simplification for freelancers and small exporters who invoice clients in instalments.

Consumer takeaway: the EDF clarification matters more than it sounds — it means a service exporter collecting ten remittances of ₹2 lakh each stays under the simplified reporting route instead of being dragged into full documentation because of the total.
