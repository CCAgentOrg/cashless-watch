# The Museum of "This Call May Be Recorded"

*Recovered from the National Museum of "This Call May Be Recorded," founded 2051. The future headlines and testimonials are fictional; the events described in the fact box are real.*

## Future headlines from the week the disclaimers came true

- **Museum's Founding Collection: 315,000 Recordings of People Begging; Donor Confirms Files Were "Perfectly Safe Until June"**
- **Fee Enters Third Rescheduling; Investors Lose 9% Waiting for It, Merchants Promise to Wait Even Longer**
- **Lenders Asked to Squeeze Gently; Industry Proposes Removing the Ruler**
- **National Fraud Strategy Finalised: Letter Sent to Mountain View, Very Polite Tone, Hopeful Closing**

I am the founding curator of the National Museum of "This Call May Be Recorded." Our name is a disclaimer. For decades, every debt-collection call began with it: *this call may be recorded for quality and training purposes.* In November 2026, we learned the sentence had been accurate all along. The quality was exposed. The training was someone else's.

The student arrives, as they always do, at Gallery One.

"What is in the cases?"

"Voices. Three hundred and fifteen thousand of them."

The founding collection came from CreditNirvana, an AI debt-collection platform owned by the fintech firm Perfios — the machine that phoned borrowers about missed EMIs, politely, at scale. A researcher named Eaton Zveare found two doors. The first was a hardcoded API key sitting in the client-side code of the Agentic AI Call Center — "sitting," in the past tense, is the museum's favourite tense — which gave anyone who found it access to call recordings, transcripts and AI-generated recommendations belonging to customers of Aditya Birla Capital Finance. The second door, CVE-2026-110475, scored 9.3 out of 10 and required a login of approximately nobody: administrator access to the main collections platform, opening roughly 3.4 million borrower accounts at Aditya Birla Capital Finance and another 3.2 million across BharatPe and the International Asset Reconstruction Company — ID cards, credit reports, legal notices, emails and texts sent to borrowers.[^1][^2][^3]

"So someone stole six point six million financial lives?"

"We don't know. The researcher confirmed the doors opened. Nobody counted afterwards."

"That seems important."

"In 2026, 'no evidence of exploitation' was considered a comforting sentence rather than an inventory problem."

"When were the doors fixed?"

"June. The public was told in October. The labelling committee rejected 'Prompt Disclosure' for this case as too ironic even for us."

Gallery Two contains a single object: a fee. Not an active fee — a fee in a jar, labelled ANY DAY NOW since October 15. The merchant discount rate on UPI payments above ₹2,000 — 0.4%, capped at ₹300 — was to begin in October, then "immediately after Diwali," and by November 19 was reportedly drifting toward the one date more sacred than Diwali in Indian public finance: the Uttar Pradesh assembly elections of 2027. The stock market, asked to price a fee that would not say when, produced poetry — Paytm fell 7%, Pine Labs 5.5% and MobiKwik 9% in a single morning. Jefferies calculated the fee was worth 8–12% of Paytm's FY27 earnings and about 28% of its FY28 profit before tax: the most precisely imagined money in the country, attached to a date that did not exist.[^4][^5]

"So nobody paid it?"

"Nobody paid it. Nine per cent of a wallet company's share price evaporated waiting for it. In 2026, a fee could be so thoroughly anticipated that it cost money before existing."

"Did the small merchants get anything?"

They did, and it is my favourite case. The same fortnight, NPCI announced UPI Vyapaar Saksham: ₹300 per QR code and ₹750 per soundbox for small merchants in small towns — acceptance infrastructure meant to be bankrolled, eventually, by the very MDR proceeds that had not arrived.[^6] The state had, in effect, sold the same fee twice: to investors as future earnings, and to shopkeepers as future hardware. The QR code in the display still works. The fee inside it is still coming.

Gallery Three is quieter. A brass plaque reads: WE SQUEEZE GENTLY. It is a paraphrase; the original words belonged to RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu, who warned microfinance lenders to lend responsibly as climate risks battered low-income households, and observed that the industry had grown oddly devoted to the borrowers it already had over the ones it had never met.[^7] Behind the plaque hangs the 50% loan-to-income cap — the brake that stops a fifth loan from being sold to pay the fourth. That very season, the industry was lobbying to dilute it and raise the income thresholds. Beside it, the year's macro backdrop: wholesale inflation at 12.29%, retail inflation at 6.09% — through RBI's tolerance band for the first time in two years — and economists expecting 50 to 75 basis points more of rate hikes, landing, as such things reliably do, on floating-rate EMIs.[^8][^9][^10]

"So the borrower on the recordings was squeezed by inflation, by the EMI, and by the collector — and the industry's proposed reform was to remove the cap?"

"You have understood Gallery Three. Most visitors require a moment."

Gallery Four we call the Six-Hour Apology. On November 5, RBI ordered every non-bank payment operator to report cyber incidents within six hours of detection, with boards personally accountable and disaster recovery in a different seismic zone from primary data centres — the regulator's insight being that disasters, like families, should not live on the same fault line.[^11] By November 21, CERT-In was warning that attackers were using AI to push fraudulent transfers through legitimate payment workflows, altering business records and approval relationships so that the fraud looked like work.[^12] The same week, Yes Bank's security upgrades stalled payouts across the fintech stack — Razorpay delayed merchant settlements, and crypto exchanges flagged INR withdrawal delays. The nation acquired a new genre of anxiety: the money was not stolen, it was being protected, slowly.

"In 2026, security meant waiting?"

"Security meant being asked to distinguish a firewall from a failure. The museum keeps both under glass; from the app, they looked identical."

One more case, near the exit. In November 2026, the National Payments Corporation of India — custodian of the world's largest real-time payment system — formally asked Google to make Android harder to misuse: restrict permissions for apps installed outside vetted stores, gate accessibility and overlay access behind multi-step safeguards.[^13] The most advanced payment rail on Earth had arrived at the strategy of writing a letter to Mountain View.

"Did it work?"

"The letter was very polite. So were the collection calls."

## What actually happened this week (November 14 – 21, 2026)

- **November 16:** October's wholesale inflation printed at 12.29%, driven by fuel and power costs, while retail inflation had already breached RBI's 6% upper band at 6.09% — the first breach in two years. Economists expect another 50–75 bps of repo hikes from 5.5% toward 6–6.25%, with the MPC meeting December 2–4; floating-rate home loan EMIs are set to rise.[^8][^9][^10]
- **Weekend, reported November 16:** RBI Deputy Governor Shirish Chandra Murmu warned MFIs to lend responsibly as climate risks batter low-income households, flagged over-focus on existing customers, and said self-regulatory bodies should not reinterpret regulations — all as the industry lobbies to dilute the 50% loan-to-income cap and raise income thresholds.[^7] Analysts also judged that RBI's November 5 cyber Master Directions — six-hour incident reporting, board accountability, seismic-zone-separated disaster recovery — would force real investment in monitoring across payment aggregators, wallet issuers and white-label ATM operators.[^11]
- **November 17, reported:** NPCI began discussions with Google on Android-level safeguards against device-exploiting UPI fraud — restricting critical permissions for sideloaded APKs and blocking apps outside vetted stores from using accessibility, device-admin or overlay permissions without multi-step checks.[^13]
- **November 18:** Researcher Eaton Zveare's findings on CreditNirvana (Perfios) were published — a hardcoded API key exposing call recordings, transcripts and AI recommendations for Aditya Birla Capital Finance customers, plus CVE-2026-110475 (critical, 9.3), an authentication failure permitting admin-level logins that exposed ~3.4 million accounts at Aditya Birla and ~3.2 million across BharatPe and IARC, with ID cards, credit reports, legal documents and borrower communications. CERT-In rated one flaw high and one critical; both were fixed in June after responsible disclosure, publicised in October; researchers confirmed access but not attacker exploitation.[^1][^2][^3] The same day, NPCI's UPI and Services Steering Committee cleared a flat ₹5 MDR on merchant-to-merchant (B2B) UPI transfers, far below the ₹80–300 the 0.4% framework would have charged.[^14]
- **November 19:** Financial Express reported the UPI MDR rollout — already deferred from October 15 — could slip past assembly elections in five states in early 2027, with no timeline agreed between government, RBI and NPCI. Paytm fell up to 7%, Pine Labs ~5.5% and MobiKwik ~9% intraday; Jefferies pegged the MDR at 8–12% of Paytm's FY27 EPS and ~28% of its FY28 PBT.[^4][^5]
- **November 20:** Details emerged of NPCI's UPI Vyapaar Saksham scheme (circular dated November 11): ₹300 per physical QR code and ₹750 per soundbox, with an incentive pool for onboarding and transaction growth in Tier 3–6 towns, the north-east, J&K and Ladakh — the acceptance infrastructure MDR proceeds are meant to bankroll.[^6] Razorpay also temporarily delayed merchant settlements over a partner-bank technical issue, restored within hours.[^12]
- **November 21:** CERT-In flagged attackers using AI to push fraudulent transactions through legitimate payment workflows — altering business records and approval relationships at government-linked payment service providers and vendor-payout platforms — and directed entities to restrict payment-approval accounts and reconcile provider data. The same reporting linked Yes Bank's AI-safeguard upgrades to delayed payouts at payment service providers, with CoinDCX, ZebPay and Coinbase India flagging INR withdrawal delays.[^12]

## What you can do

1. **If you ever borrowed from a lender that outsources collections, assume your file was in the pile.** Aditya Birla Capital Finance and BharatPe borrowers were among those exposed — expect phishing that quotes your real loan details. Hang up on "recovery agents" who call you; call the lender's official number instead, and never settle a loan over a UPI ID sent on WhatsApp.
2. **At the checkout, nothing has changed yet — hold that line.** No UPI fee is live for consumers, and merchants are barred from passing MDR to customers. If a shop adds a "UPI charge" or steers you to cards, push back. Do expect the flat ₹5 B2B fee to seep quietly into the price of everything a small business buys.
3. **If you're on a floating rate, budget for the December policy.** With 50–75 bps of hikes expected after the December 2–4 MPC, rework your EMI math now. And if you're a small-ticket borrower: count all your instalments against your income before taking one more loan — the cap that stops the spiral is the very thing under lobbying pressure.
4. **Treat a delayed payout as a security upgrade until proven otherwise — then verify.** Check the platform's status page before panicking, and never "release" a stuck payment by sharing an OTP or screen with anyone who calls about it.
5. **Keep your phone boring.** Install only from official stores, and treat any app demanding accessibility or overlay permissions as a fraud tool in waiting — today's UPI scams exploit the operating system, not your carelessness. If fraud does strike: call 1930 and file at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in/) immediately. The reporting clocks now run in hours; so should you.

In 2046, people asked whether machines could be trusted with money. By 2051 we had learned to ask something smaller and harder: when the machine said it was keeping a record *for your benefit*, whose benefit was the record for? The museum was built from the answer. It has three hundred and fifteen thousand wings.
