# Fintech Brief — November 22, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### SEBI moves to scrap the FPI look-through framework born of the Adani-Hindenburg era

Two years after it forced foreign funds to open up their ownership books, SEBI is asking whether the rule should simply go. A [consultation paper issued November 20](https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/reports/nov-2026/consultation-paper-on-proposal-to-review-the-additional-disclosure-framework-for-foreign-portfolio-investors-_105926.html) proposes removing the Additional Disclosure Framework (ADF) entirely — the 2023 rule requiring FPIs that hold over 50% of their India equity AUM in a single corporate group, or over ₹50,000 crore of equity AUM, to disclose every entity holding ownership, economic interest or control in the fund, on a full look-through basis down to natural persons, with no threshold. SEBI's justification, per [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/business/markets/sebi-mulls-review-of-additional-disclosure-norms-for-fpis-floats-consultation-paper/article71724977.ece) and [CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/sebi-says-india-fpi-disclosure-rules-go-beyond-global-norms-proposes-scrapping-adf-20038836.htm): the granular disclosure requirement exists nowhere else among major jurisdictions, and it arrives against the backdrop of FPIs pulling over ₹5.61 lakh crore ($62.24 billion) out of Indian equities between October 2024 and October 2026. [Business Standard notes](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-proposes-removing-additional-disclosure-framework-for-fpis-126112001177_1.html) the framework was introduced months after Hindenburg Research's Adani allegations, which hinged on opaque foreign-fund ownership structures the Adani group denied. The proposal covers offshore derivative instrument subscribers too, and comments are open until December 11. The same day, SEBI floated a [second consultation paper on market infrastructure institution governance](https://www.corplawupdates.in/updates/sebi-consultation-paper-mii-governance-tenure-caps): a managing director would be capped at 12 years across all exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories combined (maximum 8 in any one institution), public interest directors would face a retirement age of 70 with a two-term limit, down from 75.

Consumer takeaway: beneficial-owner identification under anti-money-laundering rules survives, but the threshold-free look-through — created precisely because opaque structures hid concentrated bets in Indian equities — is on the table. If you care about knowing who ultimately owns the market your money sits in, December 11 is the deadline to tell SEBI.

### The NBFC hack wave: ₹300 crore gone, with AI on both sides of the attack

Non-banking financial companies have quietly become India's most-robbed lenders. [The Indian Express reports](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/delhi/12-nbfcs-lose-over-rs-65-crore-in-three-months-to-cyber-fraud-in-delhi-ncr-10964516) that 12 NBFCs in Delhi-NCR lost over ₹65 crore in three months, FIRs that are part of more than 150 complaints filed nationwide in the same period with losses exceeding ₹300 crore. The method: malware enters through the front end of the lender's loan app, then fraudulent payouts are [sent from cloud servers the NBFC's firewall already trusts](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/delhi-ncr-nbfc-cyber-fraud-hackers-rs-65-crore-loan-scam), so the disbursement sails through as routine — [Financial Express adds](https://www.financialexpress.com/india-news/nbfcs-lose-over-rs-300-cr-in-cyberattacks-ai-used-to-cover-tracks/4385665) investigators found at least one breach driven by "CyberStrikeAI", a Chinese AI platform used to generate command scripts, and that a government advisory last month rated the threat critical with AI also used to cover tracks. Delhi Police have arrested 20 people and seized ₹18 crore before it could be converted to cryptocurrency; Hero FinCorp was among the first to report a major breach. Small lenders detect these heists late because loan reconciliation often runs weekly or fortnightly — by the time anyone checks, the money is gone. The pattern lands a day after CERT-In's advisory on AI-assisted payment fraud ([covered here yesterday](/posts/2026-11-21-fintech-brief/)): exploit trusted workflows and verification gaps, then push fraudulent transfers through legitimate processes.

Consumer takeaway: a "loan disbursed" notification inside an app is not proof money reached your account — reconcile every disbursal against your bank statement, and report mismatches immediately, because these frauds are typically discovered weeks late.

### Funding note: a ~$250-million week, and an NBFC buys into its own insurtech plumbing

Indian startups raised roughly $243-291 million across about 22 deals in the week to November 21, with AI taking the largest share — [Entrackr's weekly tally](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-nov-16-nov-21-12829487) has growth-stage deals at $290 million across six rounds, led by AI-native CRM startup Dreamteam's $53 million round with Together Fund, Accel and Premji Invest, alongside Nexon Omniverse's ₹485 crore (~$52 million) Series E for value-fashion brand Style Union. The fintech item worth watching: [Mufin Green Finance will pay ₹65.75 crore in cash for up to 23% of BimaPay Finsure](https://inc42.com/buzz/mufin-green-finance-to-acquire-23-stake-in-bimapay-for-%E2%82%B966-cr), the Delhi insurtech that finances insurance premiums in EMIs and already powers Mufin's mediclaim lending — [board approval came November 13](https://dealroom.co/news/174053-mufin-green-finance-to-buy-23-of-fintech-partner-bimapay-for-65-75-crore), with closing expected in three to four months pending regulatory clearances. Also this week: Urban Company opened a ₹2,500 crore qualified institutional placement that drew demand exceeding ₹15,000 crore.

Consumer takeaway: when a lender buys the fintech that finances its customers' premiums, expect smoother bundling of loans and insurance — and watch that the convenience doesn't come with you losing the ability to shop for the two separately.
