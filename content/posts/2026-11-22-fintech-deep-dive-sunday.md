# Fintech Deep Dive — Sunday | November 22, 2026

*Weekly Review | The top stories of the week, November 15–22, 2026*

India's fintech week ended where it began: with the merchant fee that keeps refusing to arrive. Between that, a regulator's new six-hour cyber clock meeting its first live breaches, an AI debt-collection platform's exposure of 6.6 million borrower accounts, Razorpay's march toward the public markets, and UPI's record October pushing fraud into the phone itself, this was a week about systems being stress-tested in public — a payment fee revised by press leak, a bank's payment gateway switched off mid-incident, and a vendor's AI agents briefly reachable by anyone on the internet. Here are the five stories that mattered, and what each means for the consumer.

## 1. UPI MDR's long retreat: an election-year timetable and a stock-market tantrum

The week's dominant story was the continued political shrinking of the UPI merchant discount rate. On November 19, the Financial Express reported that the rollout of MDR on select UPI transactions could be delayed until after the 2027 assembly elections in five states — Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Goa and Manipur — as the government stays cautious about merchant and trade-association backlash, with the government, RBI and NPCI yet to agree on any implementation date ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/upi-mdr-rollout-may-be-delayed-until-after-2027-assembly-elections-report-14084125.html); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/upi-mdr-rollout-may-be-pushed-beyond-2027-state-polls-report)). The framework itself — 0.4% on person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 for transactions of ₹75,000 and more — has already been through two revisions: announced in September with an October 15 start, deferred until after Diwali, then re-scoped this week to a flat ₹5 for business-to-business payments ([NDTV Profit](https://www.ndtv.com/business-news/upi-mdr-rollout-delay-hits-payment-stocks-should-you-buy-paytm-mobikwik-pine-labs-stocks-now-12348466)).

Markets repriced the drift instantly. On November 19, Paytm's parent One 97 Communications fell as much as 9.1% to ₹1,715, Mobikwik dropped 8.7% to ₹206.80, and Pine Labs slid 5.5% to ₹159.90 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/upi-mdr-rollout-may-be-pushed-beyond-2027-state-polls-report)). Reuters reported Jefferies' estimate that the fees would have accounted for roughly 14% of Paytm's earnings ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/paytm-pine-labs-shares-plunge-upi-transaction-fee-rollout-india-delayed-2026-11-19)) — a reminder that "free UPI," now six years old, is also an industry whose monetisation lives or dies in committee rooms rather than in product.

The quieter story underneath is NPCI's UPI Vyapaar Saksham scheme, notified by circular on November 11: ₹300 per physical QR code and ₹750 per soundbox deployed for small merchants, funded from MDR collections, with the fund split 30% for onboarding new merchants, 50% for transaction growth and 20% for incremental growth, targeting 60 lakh to 1 crore new QR codes and 5–10 lakh soundboxes a year, priority to Tier III–VI towns and merchants with turnover up to ₹40 lakh ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/money/npcis-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-boost-digital-payments-help-small-merchants-with-incentives-all-we-know-11795165732819.html); [ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/npci-introduces-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme-to-boost-digital-payments-among-small-merchants-offers-incentives-for-qr-codes-and-soundboxes/135771249); [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/business-news/npci-launches-upi-vyapaar-saksham-scheme)). The scheme runs for three years from MDR's implementation ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/upi-mdr-rollout-may-be-pushed-beyond-2027-state-polls-report)) — which means acceptance infrastructure for the smallest merchants is now hostage to the same election calendar that delayed the fee. For consumers, the takeaway is procedural: three structural revisions in two months, each discovered through press leaks rather than a published consultation. Whatever the final charge, that is no way to build consensus for the first price tag UPI has ever carried.

## 2. The six-hour clock meets its first live tests

The RBI's Master Directions on Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank payment system operators, issued November 5 (RBI/DPSS/2026-27/475), consolidated the July 2024 directions into a single framework: serious cyber incidents must be reported to the regulator within six hours of detection, with board-level governance, fourteen baseline security controls, device binding, a 12-hour cooling period on new devices, and annual audits across twenty areas. Large and medium operators — payment aggregators, card networks, TReDS platforms, Bharat Connect units, large and medium prepaid issuers — are bound now; smaller prepaid issuers and instant money transfer operators get until April 1, 2028 ([ETBFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/rbis-6-hour-reporting-rule-to-help-curb-cyber-threats-fraud-in-digital-payments-experts/135852490); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/news/rbi-consolidates-cyber-resilience)).

The same week supplied the test cases. City Union Bank disclosed to exchanges on November 18 that it had detected an unauthorised transaction through NEFT in one customer account on November 16, and had shut down that account's NEFT and RTGS gateways while working with external experts on root cause; it reported no material impact and did not disclose the amount ([CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/stocks/city-union-bank-share-price-shuts-neft-rtgs-gateways-after-unauthorised-customer-account-transaction-20037220.htm); [Mint](https://www.livemint.com/money/city-union-bank-shuts-neft-rtgs-gateway-customer-following-unauthorised-transaction-what-mean-changes-for-you-11795063791014.html)). Separately, Chennai-based fintech Bulkpe reported a theft of roughly ₹1 crore on November 17, with unauthorised transfers executed within a single hour.

The consumer-relevant detail is timing. Moneycontrol's explainer noted that the RBI's revised customer-protection framework — liability caps scaled to how fast a fraud is reported and provisional credit within ten working days — takes effect January 1, 2027, and does not apply to November incidents like this one ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/banking/city-union-bank-flags-unauthorised-neft-transfer-shuts-payment-access-are-other-customers-affected-article-14084240.html)). So the week produced a regulator-facing six-hour clock and a bank-facing two-day exchange disclosure, while the customer-facing clock that actually refunds victims is still weeks from starting. Watch how the two clocks align: a regime where operators must tell the RBI in six hours but customers learn nothing for days would be resilience theatre.

## 3. The AI debt collector that knew 6.6 million borrowers

The week's most consequential data story broke on November 18, when WIRED published researcher Eaton Zveare's disclosure of two flaws in CreditNirvana, an AI-driven debt-collection platform owned by fintech firm Perfios ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-debt-collection-service-exposed-66-million-accounts-and-315000-call-recordings)). The first, rated high severity by CERT-In, was a hardcoded API key in the client-side code of CreditNirvana's Agentic AI Call Center, exposing some 315,000 call recordings, transcripts and AI-generated recommendations belonging to Aditya Birla Capital Finance customers — and letting anyone who found it manage the platform's AI agents and run their own call campaigns. The second, rated critical (CVE-2026-110475, severity 9.3), was an authentication bypass in the main collections platform that allowed administrator login, exposing about 6.6 million borrower accounts across Aditya Birla Capital Finance (over 3.4 million), BharatPe and International Asset Reconstruction Company (together about 3.2 million) — credit reports, ID cards, case files, legal documents, and millions of emails, texts and call transcripts ([MLQ.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/flaws-in-ai-debt-collector-creditnirvana-exposed-66-million-accounts-and-315000-call-recordings); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/two-flaws-in-creditnirvanas-ai-collections-platform-exposed-66-million-accounts)).

The remediation timeline is half-reassuring: Zveare reported the flaws to CERT-In on May 30, both were patched by June 2, and CVEs followed on October 26. The unreassuring half is the silence since — neither CreditNirvana nor Perfios has said whether the 6.6 million borrowers were ever told, and neither has WIRED's requests for comment been answered ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/ai-debt-collection-service-exposed-66-million-accounts-and-315000-call-recordings)). Collections data is uniquely sensitive: debt amounts, court records, and behavioural transcripts of people at their most financially distressed. And the exposure was not just of data but of control — an attacker who can steer an AI call centre inherits its calling capacity at scale, in an industry where RBI digital-lending rules already struggle to police human recovery agents' contact hours. This is also a third-party-risk story: the borrower's relationship is with the lender, but the data sat with the lender's vendor's AI platform. India's data protection law will require breach notification once its duties commence in 2027; this incident is an argument for that timeline being treated as a floor, not a target.

## 4. Razorpay heads for the public markets while SEBI runs a twin track

Razorpay, the Tiger Global-backed payments major, received SEBI's observations on its confidentially filed IPO papers on November 3 and is preparing to publicly file its updated draft red herring prospectus as early as this coming week, with the offering — up to $600 million (about ₹5,000–6,000 crore) in fresh shares and offer-for-sale — targeting a valuation around $5 billion and a listing that could come as early as February 2027 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/razorpay-to-file-udrhp-by-next-week-eyes-ipo-by-february-2027-report); [News18](https://www.news18.com/business/ipo/upcoming-ipos-razorpay-sembcorp-green-infra-receive-sebi-nod-heres-all-you-need-to-know-10432533.html)). Note the direction: $5 billion is roughly a third below the $7.5 billion the company commanded in its 2021 private round. The company reported ₹3,783 crore in FY25 operating revenue, up 65% year-on-year, with losses weighed down by ₹1,209 crore of ESOP expense. It lands in an Indian IPO market that has raised about $18.6 billion in 2026 to date ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/razorpay-to-file-udrhp-by-next-week-eyes-ipo-by-february-2027-report)).

Two SEBI threads frame the listing. The tightening one: the market regulator confirmed detailed AI guardrails for capital markets — disclosure of AI use, named accountability, and a mandatory human-in-the-loop so no regulated entity can blame "the model's output" for mis-selling. The easing one: a reported proposal to raise the FPI beneficial-ownership look-through threshold to 25%, plus five-day FPI onboarding and no-visit KYC for NRIs announced late in the week. Razorpay's public risk factors will be the first honest public document of what it costs to run payments infrastructure under a policy regime that holds the consumer-facing price of UPI at zero — read them alongside the MDR delay in story one, which is a direct regulatory-risk line item. And when the listing lands, quarterly disclosure of complaint volumes and fraud numbers arrives with it, a transparency upgrade the private markets never demanded.

## 5. UPI's record October — and fraud moving from the user into the phone

UPI processed a record 25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore in October, up 16% year-on-year ([Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/npci-google-android-upi-fraud)). The scale is the backdrop for the week's most interesting technical development: NPCI has opened talks with Google on Android-level defences against UPI fraud, per an Economic Times report on November 17. The concern is a class of attacks that targets the handset itself — sideloaded apps and overlay malware that silently capture UPI PINs — rather than the user's judgment, and security researchers are pushing for server-side device-integrity checks alongside any OS-level fix ([Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/npci-google-android-upi-fraud)).

The significance is a shift of burden. For years, payment security messaging has leaned on customer awareness — don't share the PIN, don't click the link. Overlay attacks make awareness insufficient: the fraud happens inside a phone that behaves correctly on the surface. That is why the RBI's new directions lean on device binding and cooling periods, and why NPCI's own hardening trajectory — from the UPI Common Library to the consolidated circular route — keeps moving toward attestation. For consumers the practical guidance is newly concrete: sideloading apps is now a payment-security decision, not a convenience choice. The open question is whether Google will ship India-specific protections for a market that generates most of the world's real-time payments volume — and whether NPCI mandates device-attestation checks the way it eventually mandated the common library.

## Also on the radar

- **RBI enforcement**: a ₹9 lakh penalty on Mobikwik for PPI transfer-limit violations and delayed fraud reporting, and the cancellation of 49 NBFC registrations announced November 16 — 43 of them in West Bengal, 41 in Kolkata, nine sharing a single Poddar Court address — alongside 13 NBFCs and one housing finance company surrendering their licences. Sixty-three entities left the register in one announcement.
- **Cross-border pressure**: Wise's CEO publicly challenged the RBI's ₹25 lakh cap on cross-border payments through non-bank fintechs, keeping the limits debate alive ahead of any policy response.
- **Banking reform**: the government's banking reform panel opened consultations, with fintechs on the witness list.
- **Funding notes**: Paytm bought a Singapore entry via a stake in Funding Societies; InPrime raised ₹101 crore; Olyv extended its Series C; PB Fintech is planning a board transition.
- **RuPay on UPI**: the two biggest payment apps are still trailing on RuPay credit cards linked to UPI, a gap that shapes which consumers can actually use credit on the rail.
- **Fraud watch**: CERT-In flagged AI-driven payment fraud even as bank security upgrades stalled some fintech payouts during the week.

The pattern of the week: India's payment systems are being hardened and monetised at the same time, and each move is now tested in public — by markets, by researchers, and by attackers. Consumers should note which clock protects them: the fee clock keeps slipping, the reporting clock starts at six hours, and the refund clock begins January 1.
