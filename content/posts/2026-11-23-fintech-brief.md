# Fintech Brief — November 23, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### RBI's FAQ reset: export declarations go near-automatic for freelancers and small service exporters

The week's most consumer-relevant RBI action closed out on Sunday with the news cycle absorbing the central bank's [FAQs published November 20](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/rbi-issues-faqs-on-fema-amendment-regulations/article71725477.ece) (Press Release 2026-2027/1531) on the new Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 — the January-notified framework that took effect October 1, superseded the 2015 export regulations, consolidated [more than 150 circulars](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moneycontrol:775b929a6094b:0-rbi-eases-forex-reporting-norms-for-small-service-exporters-importers-sets-rs-10-lakh-threshold-for-edf-filing), and quietly killed the SOFTEX softex form for software exporters. The FAQs, read with the [Second Amendment notified November 2](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/rbi-clarifies-new-fema-rules-who-needs-to-file-export-declaration-form-with-bank-check-faqs-on-exemptions-thresholds-11795220937310.html) with retrospective effect from October 1, settle the anxieties the new regime created: a resident individual — freelancer, consultant, gig worker receiving foreign payments — is **deemed** to have filed the Export Declaration Form the moment they hand payment details to their bank for crediting the remittance; no separate EDF ever. Companies and other non-individual exporters file an EDF only when a single inward remittance exceeds ₹10 lakh, and invoices raised before October 1 stay under the old rules even if paid later. Software exporters file within 30 days of the invoice month's end; other service exporters within 30 days of month-end or on receipt of payment, with banks able to extend for reasonable cause. [ANI reported the per-payment interpretation](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/rbi-clarifies-rs-10-lakh-export-declaration-threshold-applies-to-each-service-payment20261121140005) on November 21: the ₹10 lakh threshold applies to each remittance, not an annual aggregate.

Consumer takeaway: if your bank asks you for an EDF on a ₹3 lakh foreign transfer, that's a compliance error — individuals are exempt outright, and the FAQs are your citation. But exemption from the form is not exemption from reporting: banks still log every remittance into RBI's E/IDPMS system, so the paper trail behind your foreign income now lives with your AD bank whether you file anything or not.

### Razorpay files for its IPO this week — at a third less than its 2021 valuation

The payments unicorn is set to file its updated draft red herring prospectus with SEBI [as early as this week](https://inc42.com/buzz/razorpay-to-file-udrhp-by-next-week-eyes-ipo-by-february-2027-report), targeting a February 2027 listing with a ₹5,000-6,000 crore issue (~$600 million) comprising fresh shares and an offer for sale. The listing would value Razorpay at roughly ₹47,000 crore (~$5 billion) — a 33% markdown from the $7.5 billion it commanded in its last funding round in 2021, though SEBI has already issued observations on its June 2026 confidential filing (November 3), clearing the path to roadshows. The company closed FY25 with ₹3,783 crore in revenue, and its investment-banking, payroll and lending lines now sit alongside the core gateway business. The IPO timing keeps Razorpay in the same January-March 2027 window as PhonePe, which Inc42 sources say is [eyeing a ~$10 billion February-March listing](https://inc42.com/features/fintech-stocks-put-mdr-dreams-on-hold) — meaning India's two largest payments processors will pitch public investors in the same quarter, both with the unresolved UPI monetisation question hanging over them. Razorpay has reportedly modelled a 0.4% MDR on eligible UPI transactions above ₹2,000 into its monetisation story, but that framework's rollout — deferred twice already — could slip again past the 2027 state elections.

Consumer takeaway: a markdown-to-list is the market repricing payments for what they are without free-UPI subsidies — watch how much of the prospectus is payments revenue versus lending and software, because that's the honest answer to whether UPI dominance actually pays.

### The funding ledger rebounds: InPrime's ₹101 crore Series B leads a fintech-heavy week

After an 88% collapse the previous week, Indian startup funding rebounded to [$290 million across 23 deals](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-nov-16-nov-21-12829487) in the week to November 21, and the fintech names deserve a closer look. The largest: [InPrime Finserv's ₹101 crore (~$10.7 million) Series B](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/funding/weekly-funding-roundup-the-startup-deals-you-may-have-missed-in-week-of-november-22-14085961.html) led by Dutch development financier FMO alongside Z47 and Pravega Ventures — an AI-first NBFC lending to India's emerging middle class with ₹333 crore in AUM, a 0.80% gross NPA, ~₹517 crore disbursed across six states, and plans to enter micro-mortgages. Also in the ledger: digital lender [Olyv raised $6 million (~₹56 crore) in a Series B extension](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/funding/weekly-funding-roundup-the-startup-deals-you-may-have-missed-in-week-of-november-22-14085961.html) from Nippon Life India AIF to expand beyond personal loans into health insurance and credit-health management, and AI-trust startup Disseqt took in $8 million.

Consumer takeaway: development-finance money leading an NBFC round is a bet that informal-income borrowers — shopkeepers, micro-entrepreneurs — can be underwriten profitably with AI. If it works, credit reaches people banks decline; the failure mode is AI underwriting that quietly prices them out. Watch which one shows up in the GNPA numbers two years out.
