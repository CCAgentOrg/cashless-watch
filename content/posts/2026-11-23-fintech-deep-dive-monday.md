# Fintech Deep Dive — Monday | November 23, 2026

*Developer & Technical | Developments reported November 16–23, 2026*

This was the week the plumbing moved. India's payment stack is being re-engineered at three layers at once — the conversation layer, where AI agents started collecting payments inside WhatsApp chats; the device layer, where NPCI and UPI apps took Android's security gaps straight to Google; and the settlement machinery, where a URCS dispute-management rewrite lands on November 25 and merchant-to-merchant payments got their own fee line. None of this changes the two-second tap a consumer sees. All of it changes what happens underneath that tap, on a rail that just processed a record 25.47 billion transactions in October. Here is what each move means for the people building on the rail — and the people paying on it.

## 1. Agentic commerce ships in the chat window: Razorpay and Cashfree go live on Meta Business Agent

The week's most concrete agentic-commerce deployment came on November 17, when Razorpay went live as the payments layer on Meta's Business Agent for WhatsApp in India — the AI agent answers customer queries, recommends products, and when the shopper commits, sends a Razorpay payment link inside the chat, payable by UPI, cards or netbanking without leaving the conversation ([Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/razorpay-meta-business-agent)). Cashfree went live the same week as a Meta launch partner on the WhatsApp Business App, with its AI agent answering questions, suggesting products and dropping a Cashfree payment link the moment a customer says "I'll take it" — payment collected while the shop owner keeps working ([Cashfree on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OXRw4ITlhic)). Elets also notes Cashfree opened its hosted checkout to AI shopping agents earlier this month, and that Razorpay received SEBI approval for its IPO earlier in November — meaning India's two largest private payment platforms are both racing into agentic commerce ahead of public scrutiny ([Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/razorpay-meta-business-agent)).

The architectural significance is easy to miss: the checkout has moved out of the web page and into the conversation. For fifteen years, "accepting payments online" meant integrating a gateway into a website or app. Now the merchant's integration surface is an agent-to-gateway link, and for the millions of Indian small businesses that run on WhatsApp chats alone, the AI agent *is* the storefront. This is a genuinely Indian shape for agentic commerce — not a browser extension orchestrating a checkout flow, but an agent embedded in the chat app where the buying conversation already happens, with the payment link as the conversion event.

The consumer caveat is liability, and the honest answer is that it is still undefined. Every flow live today keeps the human in the loop — the customer taps the link and approves the payment themselves, so these are agent-assisted payments, not autonomous ones. The rails' existing delegation primitives hint at what comes next: UPI Circle's full delegation caps payments at ₹5,000 each and ₹15,000 a month, and Reserve Pay carries a ₹10,000 cap ([Appbrew analysis](https://www.appbrew.com/blogs/ai-agents-and-upi)). Reuters reported in September that NPCI plans to let agents make small payments without per-transaction approval, but the liability framework has not been published. When an agent buys the wrong thing — or a fraudster impersonates one — the dispute machinery being rewritten this week (story 3) becomes the safety net of record. Merchants adopting these flows should write return and cancellation policies as if disputes will lean on them, because they will.

## 2. The device is the new attack surface: NPCI and UPI apps take Android's chinks to Google

Also on November 17, The Economic Times reported that NPCI and UPI players are pressing Google to harden Android itself against fraud that targets the device rather than the user's mistake — sideloaded apps and overlay malware that can compromise phones and steal UPI credentials ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/banking/finance/fight-against-cyber-frauds-npci-upi-apps-tap-google-to-fix-android-chinks/articleshow/135900825.cms)). The fraud pattern is specific: malicious APKs distributed through messaging links, fake customer-support conversations and spoofed "update" prompts; once installed, overlay malware of the Ghost Overlay/GhostBat family uses Android's "display over other apps" permission to draw an invisible layer on top of a legitimate UPI app and capture the PIN as it is entered ([The 420](https://the420.in/npci-google-android-upi-fraud-security/); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-npci-upi-google-sideloading-apks/)). Google's own analysis reportedly found over 95% of suspicious installs came from browsers, messaging apps or file managers rather than vetted stores.

The industry's ask, routed through the Payments Council of India, is a two-gate model. Gate one is Android: OS-level blocks that stop sideloaded apps from requesting accessibility, device-administration or overlay permissions without multi-step safeguards — a notable gap, since Google's India protection pilot running since October 2024 covers four permissions (accessibility, SMS and notification access) but not the overlay permission that Ghost-family malware actually exploits. Gate two is the bank's server: device attestation checks against Google's revoked-key list, an extended Play Integrity API that flags devices using leaked keys, and transaction limits or extra verification on devices with outdated security patches or suspicious behaviour ([The Terminal](https://theterminal.space/security/npci-google-sideloading-overlay-permission-gap-upi/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/npci-upi-apps-seek-googles-intervention-to-curb-cyber-fraud-report-567919-2026-11-17)). Google's newer defences — restricted settings on recent Android versions and an advanced sideload flow, as of August 2026, that requires multi-step verification and imposes a seven-day-plus waiting window — help only users on patched, recent devices, which is not most of India. As Mint's report put it, strengthening Android alone is not enough; payment providers must harden their own fraud-detection and authorization layers in parallel ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/news/india/npci-turns-to-google-to-fix-android-security-gaps-as-upi-fraudsters-find-new-ways-to-target-users-11794911187938.html)).

The regulatory backdrop sharpens the urgency. RBI data showed digital payment frauds at 3.58 lakh cases in September, the RBI's compensation framework for small-value electronic fraud takes effect in January 2027, and the central bank's November 5 Master Directions on cyber resilience now require non-bank payment operators to report serious incidents within six hours of detection ([Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/npci-google-android-upi-fraud); [Rediff](https://m.rediff.com/amp/business/report/rbis-6-hour-reporting-rule-to-curb-cyber-threats-digital-fraud/20261115.htm)). Six-hour reporting speeds the regulator's response; it does not stop a single overlay capture. Device integrity might, and it is why the Android conversation matters more than any awareness campaign: the fraud has moved from "you were deceived" to "your phone was compromised," and defences that live only in the app layer cannot see it. Consumers should read the practical message directly — sideloaded APKs remain the delivery mechanism for nearly all of this, and a phone that cannot be patched is a payment instrument with a broken tamper seal.

## 3. Circular 240: the dispute machinery gets rewritten on November 25

Two days after this column, on November 25, 2026, NPCI's Circular 240/2026-27 rewrites UPI dispute management under URCS — and it is the most consumer-consequential piece of engineering to land this week. The update standardises the reason-code vocabulary across fraud chargebacks and wrong-payment reversals: 1063 covers duplicate or multiple transactions and payments settled by alternate means (absorbing the discontinued code 1084), 1085 and the new U011 cover amounts charged above the transaction value, 1061 covers credit not processed for returned goods, 1062 covers defective or misdescribed goods, 1064 covers non-delivery, and 1066 covers excess charges ([Ajay Kumar on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/authorajay_upi-dispute-management-rules-activity-7529458099208773632-AYEi); [Sayantan Guha Roy on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/sayantanguharoy/posts/upi-added-new-dispute-chargeback-reason-codeshere-are-the-important-reason-codes/1471635399363192)). "Wrong Credit Chargeback" is renamed "Wrong Payment – Reversal Request" under flags WC, WA and WR, with two new rejection codes — WC6, where the customer confirms the credit received was correct, and WC7, where the customer confirms the credit was returned by other means. Fraud chargebacks and wrong-payment reversals will be permitted for deemed-approved transactions after Transaction Complaint Corner updates, and evidence requirements are standardised across categories: UPI payment receipts, purchase invoices, customer complaints and, where applicable, FIR or NCRP records.

Why this belongs on a developer desk: chargeback maturity has always been the missing consumer protection in instant payments. Card networks spent decades building code-based dispute taxonomies with evidence rules and arbitration; UPI's instant irrevocability left victims of fraud and error with complaint tickets rather than structured claims. This circular imports card-network discipline into the URCS back-office — named reason codes, rejection codes that bind the receiving side, and a documented evidence standard. For engineers at banks, PSPs and UPI apps, the change set is small but immovable: reason-code tables, rejection-code handling, and evidence upload flows all change on November 25, with no runway extension. For consumers, cleaner dispute categories mean less "no applicable code" friction at the complaint desk — but the evidence standardisation cuts both ways: your refund case is now only as strong as your documentation, so save the receipt, and where fraud is involved, file the NCRP complaint, because the circular explicitly names those records as supporting evidence. One caveat: these details come via detailed industry summaries of the circular; the circular text itself governs.

## 4. Merchant-to-merchant gets a flat ₹5 — but the MDR clock still isn't running

The missing piece of the MDR framework's fee schedule surfaced on November 19: merchant-to-merchant UPI payments — wholesalers paying retailers, B2B marketplaces, B2B collections — will carry a flat ₹5 per transaction, Business Standard reported, citing people aware of the NPCI decision taken by its UPI and Services Steering Committee ([Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/upi-mdr-m2m-flat-fee)). That is far below the ₹300-per-transaction charge traders had feared under the 0.4% MDR on UPI transactions above ₹2,000 announced in September, and person-to-person transfers remain exempt entirely. What has not arrived is the date: the rollout was deferred two days before its scheduled October 15 start, five weeks have passed since Diwali with no new implementation date, and reporting suggests the wait could stretch well into next year — possibly past the 2027 state elections.

The engineering read is that pricing is being compiled into the rail as per-counterpart fee logic: P2P free, P2M up to ₹2,000 free, mid and large merchants at 0.4% capped at ₹300, essential sectors at a flat ₹5, and now M2M at a flat ₹5. Classifying a transaction as merchant-to-merchant is a harder technical problem than applying a value threshold — it requires the rail to know that both ends are businesses, which means onboarding data quality becomes a pricing input. The scheme's purpose is already programmed too: under the UPI Vyapaar Saksham Scheme (circular dated November 11), each issuing bank, acquiring bank, payer PSP and UPI app contributes 5% of its MDR share to a fund that pays acquiring banks ₹300 per physical QR code and ₹750 per soundbox deployed at eligible new small merchants — turnover up to ₹40 lakh, concentrated in tier 3–6 towns and the northeastern states — targeting 60 lakh to 1 crore new QRs and 5–10 lakh soundboxes annually over a three-year term ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-npci-upi-mdr-vyapaar-saksham-scheme); [CIOL](https://www.ciol.com/news/npci-unveils-mdr-funded-plan-to-expand-upi-among-small-merchants-12816121)). Public opinion has not moved: NPCI told the government on November 3 that MDR is a commercial charge among participants rather than a tax, with pass-through to customers prohibited, and LocalCircles reported on November 19 that over three in four UPI users oppose the flat 0.4% levy outright ([LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/upi-mdr-roll-out)). Until an implementation circular names a date, checkout economics stay unchanged — but every payments engineer in the country should be building the MDR fee-code plumbing now, because the day it lands, it lands everywhere at once.

## 5. The rest of the technical week

October's data gave every story above its scale: UPI processed a record 25.47 billion transactions worth ₹31.53 lakh crore in October — up 23% and 16% year-on-year, the first month past both 25 billion transactions and ₹30 lakh crore in value — with average daily value crossing ₹1 lakh crore for the first time and daily volume averaging 822 million ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/upi-transactions-rise-5-8-mom-to-record-25-47-bn-in-october); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/finance-news-upi-average-daily-transaction-value-crosses-rs-1-trillion-october-2026-126110100275_1.html); [BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/avgerage-daily-value-of-upi-transactions-crosses-1-lakh-crorein-october-2026/article71652439.ece)). PhonePe and Google Pay's combined grip on roughly 79% of volume remains the concentration problem no fee scheme solves ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/upi-transactions-rise-5-8-mom-to-record-25-47-bn-in-october)).

Elsewhere on the infrastructure desk: Veefin launched its AI OS at Singapore FinTech Festival on November 18 — an agentic platform where banks deploy role-specific "AI employees" for lending, trade finance, payments and treasury, connected to core banking through APIs, with every action logged and human-approval gates configurable, opening an early access programme with 12 institutions ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/veefin-ai-os-banks-singapore-fintech-festival-2026)) — the bank-ops counterpart to the checkout-side agentic story. Cashfree added Google Pay to its international payment gateway for Indian exporters on November 16, widening the foreign-methods shelf a single integration can offer ([Express Computer](https://www.facebook.com/ExpressComputerOnline/posts/1826074839524647)). Decentro signed a letter of intent with Kubar Labs at SFF on November 19 to build an institutional exchange for cross-border trade finance — insured Indian export receivables a foreign financier can buy repeatedly — on Decentro's GIFT City stack of multi-currency collections and escrow ([Decentro](https://www.facebook.com/decentrotech/posts/signed-singapore-fintech-festival-2026day-2decentro-and-kubar-labs-have-signed-a/1806906861441481)). And the RBI has summoned fintechs to its virtual "Finquiry" interaction on November 27 with the theme "Emerging Cybersecurity trends: Implications for Fintechs" — a fair sign that the device-integrity conversation of story 2 is heading for a regulatory agenda near you ([RBI on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/Df98nRdBDsv)).

The pattern across all of it: the visible product is still a two-second payment, but the invisible stack under it — conversation, device, dispute code, fee schedule — is being rebuilt in public, one circular and one integration at a time. The week of November 16–23 was the week you could watch all four layers move at once.
