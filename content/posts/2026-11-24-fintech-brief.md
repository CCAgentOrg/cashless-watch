# Fintech Brief — November 24, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### RBI's "Stop Switch": draft directions would hand customers the account kill-switch

The most consequential consumer-protection proposal of the quarter landed Monday, when RBI placed [seven draft amendment directions](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/rbi-proposes-greater-customer-control-over-digital-payments-to-curb-fraud-126112301054_1.html) for public comment — one each for commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, urban co-operative banks and rural co-operative banks — that would shift fraud defence from compensation-after-the-fact to control-before-it. The draft's [centrepiece mechanics](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/rbi-proposes-new-rules-to-protect-bank-accounts-from-fraud-heres-how-stop-switch-and-trusted-person-approval-work-11795441662320.html): a "Stop Switch" to instantly block all customer-initiated outgoing debits; per-channel switches so customers can disable net banking, cards or other digital rails individually; customer-set limits at transaction, beneficiary, daily-per-channel and overall-daily levels, plus time windows during which chosen channels stay off; and default-off digital channels at account onboarding, with new or reissued cards restricted to domestic ATMs and PoS until the customer opts in. A voluntary "Trusted Person Mechanism" lets customers nominate an adult who gets one hour to approve or reject transfers above a self-set threshold, [Informist reports](https://www.informistmedia.com/MoneyWire/65332/combat-frauds-RBI-proposes-customer-set-digital-payment-limits) — with the draft explicit that the trusted person is not a joint holder, nominee or signatory, and sees no balance or account details.

The carve-outs deserve scrutiny. Per [Mint](https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/rbi-proposes-new-rules-to-protect-bank-accounts-from-fraud-heres-how-stop-switch-and-trusted-person-approval-work-11795441662320.html), the trusted-person check will not apply to UPI transfers, merchant payments, or recurring instructions — meaning the P2P UPI scam, India's largest retail fraud vector, sits outside its protection. Bank-negligence provisions (failed MFA, missing alerts, internal breaches, staff collusion) take effect [January 1, 2027](https://legal.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/regulators/rbi-proposes-trusted-person-sign-off-on-electronic-transfers-to-curb-fraud/136112294); the rest from July 1, 2027, or earlier if banks move voluntarily. Comments are open until December 14 via RBI's Connect 2 Regulate portal.

Consumer takeaway: this draft finally treats the customer as the first line of defence rather than the last victim — but the exclusion of UPI P2P from the trusted-person check is the gap fraudsters will exploit first. Comment period is three weeks; use it.

### The UPI MDR that wasn't: no date, no incentives, six quarters of waiting

India's digital payment companies are stuck in a double bind, per an [ETBFSI report published Monday](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/upi-mdr-delay-fintech-incentives): the merchant discount rate on select UPI transactions — announced in September 2026 with an October 15 start, then deferred past Diwali "to spare merchants extra costs" — still has no fresh date, and government incentives haven't been paid out for six quarters. The Payments Council of India has raised both with the government even as transaction volumes, compliance requirements and cybersecurity spending climb. The market already voted: Paytm and Pine Labs shares fell earlier this month when the scale of the limbo became clear.

This is the unresolved question underneath every payments IPO story we've covered this month — Razorpay's ₹5,000-6,000 crore issue and PhonePe's rumoured February-March listing both depend on monetisation math that keeps getting postponed. When the two largest processors pitch public investors in the same quarter on a fee that "exists everywhere except checkout," the deferral isn't a payment-policy footnote; it's the difference between the prospectus working and not.

Consumer takeaway: the MDR applies only to large-merchant P2M transactions above ₹2,000 on select rails — small merchants and P2P stay free — but watch whether a third deferral quietly becomes indefinite. ### Funding ledger: Angel One doubles down on wealthtech, data-security startup banks ₹110 crore

Monday's filings-based funding news skews fintech-adjacent. Wealthtech platform [Ionic Wealth raised ₹177 crore](https://entrackr.com/news/ionic-wealth-raises-rs-177-cr-from-angel-one-marquee-families-12833453) in a strategic round led by its backer Angel One alongside marquee family offices — a vertical-integration play by the listed broker as managed-account wealth platforms compete with direct mutual fund apps. Data-security startup [Matters.AI is raising ₹110 crore (~$11.5 million)](https://entrackr.com/exclusive/exclusive-mattersai-to-raise-rs-110-cr-in-pre-series-a-led-by-tanglin-ventures-12833226) in a pre-Series A led by Singapore's Tanglin Ventures at a ~₹447 crore post-money valuation, with IIFL Fintech Fund among existing investors re-upping — note the investor: a fintech fund buying into security for financial data is itself a signal about where the sector's risk sits. Context for the week: Indian startup funding rebounded to ~$290 million last week after an 88% collapse, and a [Tracxn report out Monday](https://m.economictimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/india-ai-funding-nearly-doubles-to-usd-1-6-billion-in-jan-sept-2026-tracxn/articleshow/136102647.cms) puts India AI funding at a record $1.6 billion for January-September 2026, with Mumbai taking a startling 48% share.

Consumer takeaway: when brokerages and fintech funds lead security rounds, the industry is admitting the breach surface — not the product feature list — is the competitive moat. Watch whether these tools ever get pointed at customer-side fraud, not just enterprise perimeter.
