# Fintech Deep Dive — Tuesday | November 24, 2026

*Buzz & Funding | Developments reported November 17–24, 2026*

This was the week Indian fintech's money story became openly policy-contingent. Razorpay cleared its IPO and now waits on market windows alongside PhonePe — both priced against a merchant fee that still has no start date. A single report that the UPI MDR rollout could slip past the 2027 assembly elections knocked ₹36,000 crore of listed payments market value around in days. Ribbit Capital booked ₹1,905 crore selling Groww at a record high. Paytm got its wallet licence back and wrote its first overseas equity cheque. And in the quieter corners, Dutch and Japanese development money found India's small-borrower lenders. Here is what each move means — for the companies, the investors, and the consumers these markets claim to serve.

## 1. Razorpay clears SEBI: the Q1 2027 fintech listing queue is now two deep

SEBI issued its final observations on Razorpay's draft IPO papers on November 3, and the clearance surfaced publicly on November 13 when Reuters cited the regulator's processing-status update ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/ipos/fpos/razorpay-moves-closer-to-public-market-debut-as-sebi-gives-green-signal-to-ipo/articleshow/135804850.cms); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/razorpay-gets-sebi-approval-for-rs-6000-cr-ipo-in-india/articleshow/135810458.cms)). The Bengaluru payments company filed confidentially in June and is now working toward a January–March 2027 listing ([BW Disrupt](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/razorpay-gets-sebi-nod-for-rs-5-000-6-000-cr-ipo-listing-likely-in-q1-2027-633553)). The shape of the deal: a raise of ₹5,000–6,500 crore, split between a fresh issue of up to ₹2,700 crore (approved by shareholders in May 2026) and an offer-for-sale of roughly similar size, with Axis Capital, JPMorgan, Citi, Kotak Mahindra Capital and Goldman Sachs on the ticket ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/ipos/fpos/razorpay-moves-closer-to-public-market-debut-as-sebi-gives-green-signal-to-ipo/articleshow/135804850.cms); [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/razorpay-ipo-sebi-approval)).

The more revealing number is the valuation. Razorpay is targeting roughly $5 billion — about 13 times its FY25 revenue and 39 times its FY25 gross profit, on analyst back-of-envelope math — well below the $7.5 billion it commanded at its December 2021 peak ([INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/razorpay-ipo-sebi-approval)). A founder taking a down-round valuation to the public market is a signal worth reading: it prices the business on what it actually earns today, not on 2021's fintech euphoria. Razorpay's own numbers hint at why the mix matters — revenue from card machines and prepaid instruments jumped from ₹21 crore to ₹442 crore, and direct costs rose about 80% to ₹2,506 crore, as the company pivoted hard into offline acceptance hardware ([INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/razorpay-ipo-sebi-approval)).

Razorpay will not be alone in that window. Inc42 reports PhonePe is targeting a February–March 2027 listing at around a $10 billion valuation, with roughly 45% of UPI merchant payments — the single largest claim on the merchant fee that hasn't arrived ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fintech-stocks-put-mdr-dreams-on-hold)). Which leads directly to the week's repricing.

## 2. The MDR delay knocks the promised fee out of fintech stock prices

On November 19, shares of Paytm's parent One97 Communications fell 6% to ₹1,777 — the day's biggest loser in the derivatives segment — while Pine Labs dropped 3.8% to ₹163 and MobiKwik tumbled as much as 7% ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/stocks/news/paytm-mobikwik-pine-labs-shares-tumble-up-to-7-on-further-reports-of-a-upi-mdr-rollout-delay-what-to-expect-now/articleshow/135977413.cms)). The trigger was a Financial Express report that the 0.4% UPI merchant discount rate — announced in September for P2M transactions above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 — could now be deferred until after the 2027 assembly elections in five states, with no official date offered. By Friday November 20, the week's damage read: One97 down 10.8%, Pine Labs down 9.4%, MobiKwik down 6.6% ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fintech-stocks-put-mdr-dreams-on-hold)).

What makes this week instructive is how much of the fee had already been booked in analysts' models. Goldman Sachs had estimated up to ₹1,400 crore of incremental FY28 EBITDA for Paytm in a high-end scenario — 43–70% of its entire FY28 EBITDA projection. Jefferies valued Pine Labs' FY28 MDR revenue at ₹160 crore, roughly 20% of its EBIT and profit estimates. MobiKwik's own co-founder Upasana Taku told investors in the November earnings call that MDR would add at least ₹100 crore to FY28 payments revenue ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fintech-stocks-put-mdr-dreams-on-hold)). None of these companies is unprofitable without the fee — Paytm's financial services revenue grew 49% year-on-year to ₹912 crore in Q2 FY27; Pine Labs earns across issuing, affordability and online payments. But as Anand Rathi's Atul Thakkar put it, "this is MDR euphoria unwinding, not a change in the businesses" — the September announcement had lifted Paytm 11%, MobiKwik 12% and Pine Labs 21% before any fee existed ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fintech-stocks-put-mdr-dreams-on-hold)).

The consumer stake in this is direct. The fee was designed as a merchant-side charge, with pass-through to customers prohibited — yet a market that prices payments companies on the promise of it is a market that will celebrate every rupee eventually extracted from the checkout. Watch the gap between what regulators permit and what investors reward.

## 3. Ribbit Capital books ₹1,905 crore on Groww — the post-IPO liquidity cycle at full speed

On November 17, Groww's earliest institutional backer Ribbit Capital sold 8.7 crore shares — a 1.38% stake in parent Billionbrains Garage Ventures — at ₹219 apiece for ₹1,905.3 crore via two block deals on the NSE ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/block-deals-ribbit-capital-sells-groww-stake-worth-rs-1-905-crore-happy-forgings-promoters-offload-1-7-stake-14082803.html)). The buyers read like a roll call of institutional India and global money: Kotak Mahindra Mutual Fund took 2.05 crore shares for ₹448.95 crore, seven BlackRock schemes bought ₹274.9 crore worth, Société Générale ₹237.61 crore, with Morgan Stanley Asia Singapore, BNP Paribas Arbitrage, Fidelity funds, HDFC Life, Norges Bank and the NPS Trust filling out the tape ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/stocks/news/groww-block-deal-ribbit-capital-entities-sell-shares-worth-rs-1905-crore-morgan-stanley-asia-blackrock-bnp-paribas-among-buyers/articleshow/135931013.cms)).

The arithmetic of the exit is the story. Ribbit sold at ₹219, some 119% above Groww's IPO price of the equivalent of $1.36 from November 2025, into a stock that closed at a record ₹227.53 the same day and has returned 46% in 2026 ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/173764-ribbit-capital-sells-203m-groww-stake-as-shares-hit-record-high); [Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/stocks/news/groww-block-deal-ribbit-capital-entities-sell-shares-worth-rs-1905-crore-morgan-stanley-asia-blackrock-bnp-paribas-among-buyers/articleshow/135931013.cms)). Across the IPO and three subsequent sell-downs, the firm has now harvested roughly ₹7,871 crore while still holding about 6.9% ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fintech-stocks-put-mdr-dreams-on-hold); [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/block-deals-ribbit-capital-sells-groww-stake-worth-rs-1-905-crore-happy-forgings-promoters-offload-1-7-stake-14082803.html)). This is not a fund fleeing — it is the designed rhythm of venture returns meeting retail-market demand, with mutual funds and pension money on the other side. The fundamentals under the tape are real, at least: Groww reported Q2 FY27 revenue of ₹1,501 crore, up 47%, and profit of ₹725.6 crore, up 54% ([The CEO Magazine](https://www.instagram.com/p/Df4e69EiMHV)). But note the choreography for every IPO that follows — Razorpay and PhonePe's marketing materials will be landing into a market that has just watched a peak-2021 fund monetise a third of its stake at an all-time high.

## 4. Paytm's two-front week: the wallet comes home, the stack goes abroad

Two Paytm transactions defined the week. The first was regulatory: on November 13, the RBI granted Paytm Payments Services — the subsidiary that kept the company's payments business alive after its banking affiliate's licence was cancelled in April 2026 — in-principle authorisation to issue prepaid payment instruments, i.e. wallets. Paytm's stock touched an all-time intraday high of ₹1,960 on the news ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/companies/news/paytm-returns-to-wallet-business-will-it-help-regain-some-of-its-lost-markets-11794989806605.html); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/paytm-snaps-2-day-losing-run-on-singapore-investment-plan-nomura-retains-buy-126112000373_1.html)). It is a second chance with a clock on it: the company must complete a system and cybersecurity audit and file the report within six months, and final authorisation remains pending ([billcut explainer](https://www.billcut.com/blogs/paytm-wallet)). The regulatory subtext is notable — the RBI cancelled one entity's licence in April and admitted its sibling's application in November, distinguishing between promoter and entity with unusual speed.

The second was strategic: on November 19, Paytm Singapore's board approved investing up to ₹200 crore in the subsidiaries of Funding Societies, Southeast Asia's largest SME lending platform — about ₹162 crore (up to SGD 21.8 million) in cash for a 19.1% stake in its payments subsidiary CardUp, with roughly ₹38 crore more into an affiliate, targeting completion by May 20, 2027 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/paytm-to-invest-up-to-rs200-crore-in-funding-societies-subsidiaries-acquire-19-1-stake-in-cardup-568639-2026-11-19); [TradingView/Moody's](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:eba938cc48cab:0-paytm-to-acquire-19-1-stake-in-cardup-for-rs-162-cr)). Paytm supplies the payments-and-lending technology stack; the local partner keeps the sales force — the same "partner-operated" template it has trialled via an Indonesian entity since April and a Luxembourg incorporation late last year ([Asia Tech Review](https://www.asiatechreview.com/p/dayones-5-billion-us-ipo-is-reportedly)). Nomura kept its buy rating; the stock rose 2.5% on November 20 to snap a two-day slide ([Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/markets/stocks/news/paytm-shares-in-focus-as-firm-set-to-invest-rs-200-crore-in-singapore-fintech-partnership-what-nomura-says-on-expansion/articleshow/136012270.cms)).

Read together, the two moves say something plain: Paytm is re-arming the businesses where a fee can actually be charged. Wallets carry float and higher blended margins than UPI — MobiKwik's wallet business runs above 16 basis points ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-mobikwik-100-crore-upi-mdr-revenue)) — and an overseas stack licensed for revenue sidesteps India's zero-fee rails entirely. The consumer question is who pays for that re-arming: wallet convenience fees, wallet pricing on add-money, and card-up-style paid rails are exactly where a margin-hungry Paytm will test users' tolerance. Competition helps — MobiKwik itself got an RBI nod for an NBFC arm the same week, even as it absorbed a ₹9 lakh penalty for PPI limit violations and late fraud reporting ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-mobikwik-100-crore-upi-mdr-revenue)).

## 5. The quiet desk: development finance finds India's small-borrower lenders

The startup funding market snapped back this week — 23 deals worth $290 million, up from a dire $31.3 million the previous week, with fintech deals behind only AI's six ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-nov-16-nov-21-12829487)). The fintech cheques shared a signature: institutional, patient, credit-focused money.

InPrime Finserv, an "AI-first" NBFC lending to India's emerging middle class, raised ₹101 crore (about $10.7 million) in Series B funding led by the Dutch development bank FMO, with existing investors Z47 and Pravega Ventures participating; the company has disbursed ₹516.92 crore to over 27,000 households, holds ₹332.8 crore in assets under management, and will enter micro-mortgages ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/inprime-finserv-raises-%E2%82%B9101-cr-to-expand-lending-enter-micro-mortgages)). Olyv — formerly SmartCoin, the Bengaluru credit-led platform serving customers across 19,000 pin codes — added about $6 million (₹57 crore) from Nippon Life India AIF Management in a Series B extension, lifting the round to roughly $30 million against lifetime equity of $50 million and debt of $150 million; the money goes to AI underwriting, health insurance and credit-health products, in service of a stated target of 100 million users and $1 billion AUM by FY29 ([BW Disrupt](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/lending-fintech-startup-olyv-raises-6-mn-takes-series-b-to-30-mn-633916); [Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/funding/bengalurus-lending-app-olyv-raises-6-million-as-it-chases-1-billion-aum-by-fy29-12819724)). Bengaluru wealthtech finny.club added ₹5.3 crore in an angel round for its AI financial-planning product ([Medium — Akhil Mishra's fintech weekly](https://medium.com/@itsAkhilMishra/last-weeks-11-fintech-updates-you-must-know-in-india-november-22nd-d49e1942e5d6)).

The pattern deserves a consumer lens. When development banks and Japanese insurance money fund small-ticket lenders, the stated mission is formalising credit for borrowers the banking system declines. That is genuinely valuable — and it is also the segment where effective interest rates, collections practices and data consent matter most, because the customer has the least negotiating power. FMO and Nippon India's involvement brings environmental-and-social underwriting disciplines that pure-play VC does not. Hold these lenders to them.

## The rest of the funding week

Urban Company raised ₹2,500 crore through a QIP that attracted bids worth over ₹15,000 crore, earmarked for its InstaHelp services push — even as Q2 FY27 losses more than tripled to ₹182.5 crore ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fintech-stocks-put-mdr-dreams-on-hold)). PB Fintech co-founder Alok Bansal will step down as Executive Vice Chairman on March 31, 2027, remaining on the Policybazaar parent's board in a non-executive role ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/report/weekly-funding-report-weekly-funding-report/funding-and-acquisitions-in-indian-startups-this-week-nov-16-nov-21-12829487)). And in a sign of how global markets now reach Indian retail portfolios, Anthropic's Nasdaq debut at a $1.5 trillion valuation drove 40% of INDmoney's international investment activity for the period ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/fintech-stocks-put-mdr-dreams-on-hold)).

## The takeaway

Strip the week to its frame: India's fintech valuations — public and private — are now visibly priced off a regulatory fee that does not exist yet, on rails that remain free, for companies whose customers pay nothing to pay. Razorpay taking a haircut to $5 billion, Paytm losing 10.8% in a week on a delay rumour, and Ribbit selling into a record high are three faces of the same bet. When the MDR date finally arrives — or fails to — the repricing will not distinguish between the exchanges and the checkout. Consumers should read this week's market choreography as the clearest signal yet of where the industry intends to find its next margin: the wallet, the merchant counter, and the small-ticket loan.
