# Fintech Brief — November 25, 2026

## Today's Top Stories

### EPFO puts your PF on UPI: final trials running on BHIM

The Employees' Provident Fund Organisation is running final validation trials with 100 shortlisted subscribers across India before launching provident fund withdrawals through a UPI interface on the BHIM app, [The Hindu BusinessLine reported Monday](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/epfo-upi-withdrawal-bhim). No launch date has been fixed yet. Once live, the facility would let EPFO's [7.48 crore active subscribers](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/epfo-upi-withdrawal-bhim) check their balance and withdraw up to 75% of their provident fund directly to Aadhaar-linked bank accounts — putting what would be one of the largest government-to-citizen payout use cases yet on NPCI's rails, and widening BHIM's role beyond everyday payments.

The convenience case is obvious: no EPFO portal login, no claim-form queue. The stakes are just as clear. As [Elets BFSI notes](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/epfo-upi-withdrawal-bhim), payment firms have already had to ask Google to harden Android against UPI PIN-stealing malware, and NPCI has been consolidating safety tools in its MyUPI hub — retirement savings moving over a rail that fraudsters actively target raises the cost of every security gap. There is also a process question worth asking before launch: what happens when a withdrawal fails mid-flight, and who answers the grievance call — EPFO or the member's bank?

Consumer takeaway: watch the trials closely. A failed PF withdrawal is not a failed ₹200 UPI payment — the redressal design matters more than the feature.

### Paytm's wallet comeback inches closer — in-principle is not in your pocket

Paytm's wallet revival moved from ambition to paperwork. Its subsidiary PPSL has received [in-principle RBI authorisation](https://inc42.com/buzz/paytm-set-to-revive-wallet-business-with-rbi-in-principle-nod) to issue and operate prepaid payment instruments, including digital wallets — the first formal step back after the regulator barred Paytm Payments Bank from accepting fresh deposits and top-ups in [January 2024](https://inc42.com/buzz/paytm-set-to-revive-wallet-business-with-rbi-in-principle-nod), which took the wind out of the wallet business. PPSL applied for the fresh licence on December 31, 2025, per the company's own disclosures, and the approval comes with conditions: a system and cybersecurity audit within six months, and final authorisation from RBI before wallet operations can actually resume.

The market has already priced in the optimism. Paytm shares reclaimed the ₹1,900 mark — and their [November 2021 listing price — for the first time in nearly five years](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/paytm-shares-reclaim-listing-price-for-first-time-in-five-years-rbi-nod-for-wallet-business-boosts-sentiment-14079592.html) after the announcement, and Jefferies raised its price target to ₹2,300 on Monday, calling the PPI nod ["an avenue for monetisation"](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/markets/jefferies-sees-up-to-19-upside-in-paytm-says-rbi-nod-for-ppi-services-avenue-for-monetisation-14081133.html); the stock slipped 2.5% to ₹1,888.90 the same day on profit booking. Note what the brokerages are actually excited about: a wallet is an earnings lever, not a consumer giveaway. And an in-principle nod is RBI's way of saying the paperwork is acceptable — the audit and final authorisation remain the real gates.

Consumer takeaway: your Paytm wallet does not work as a wallet again yet. Nothing changes until final authorisation lands; treat "wallet is back" headlines as a share-price story until then.

### SEBI to intermediaries: stop feeding the grey-market beast

SEBI issued an [advisory on November 23](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/nov-2026/advisory-regarding-unregulated-pre-listing-premium-often-called-as-grey-market-premium-gmp-for-upcoming-initial-public-offers-ipos-_105942.html) (PR No. 75/2026) asking all registered intermediaries and market participants to refrain from publishing, displaying or broadcasting grey market premium figures for upcoming IPOs — on websites, platforms, email, SMS and social media alike. The regulator's framing: GMP figures are ["unofficial, unverified, and unregulated"](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-asks-market-players-to-stop-publishing-grey-market-premiums-for-ipos-126112301145_1.html) — shares supposedly traded before formal listing, outside recognised exchanges, with no settlement guarantee.

The advisory also warns investors directly: don't treat GMP as an indicator of listing price or expected returns, and avoid GMP-linked transactions — because anyone trading in the grey market has [no access to SEBI's investor protection framework](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/sebi-advises-market-intermediaries-to-stop-publishing-ipo-grey-market-premiums-cautions-investors), exchange grievance redressal or the ODR mechanism if things go wrong.

Consumer takeaway: the advisory binds registered intermediaries, but most GMP numbers circulate via unregistered sites and Telegram channels that SEBI's word alone won't reach. The enforcement test is whether regulators go after the publishers, not just the practice. Until then, an IPO should be judged on its prospectus — not a number some anonymous channel is quoting.

### Funding ledger: Prosus leads ₹38 crore into lending infrastructure

B2B fintech Vegapay is raising [₹38.4 crore (~$4 million) in a Series A led by Prosus](https://yourstory.com/2026/11/vegapay-to-raise-rs-384-cr-series-a-funding-led-by-prosus), with existing investors Elevation Capital, Vertex Ventures and Eximius Ventures participating — [Entrackr first reported](https://entrackr.com/) the share issuance from regulatory filings. The Mumbai-based digital lending and card-management platform raised ₹37.9 crore from existing investors led by Vertex just months ago, and [The Economic Times notes](https://m.economictimes.com/tech/startups/prosus-to-lead-rs-38-crore-funding-in-vegapay-as-fintech-bets-gather-pace/articleshow/136122830.cms) the deal marks Prosus expanding beyond consumer-facing fintech into the plumbing — consistent with its stated over-indexing on financial services (Navi at $100 million, Bachatt at $10 million).

Consumer takeaway: credit infrastructure is where the smart money is going this quarter. Better rails for issuing cards and loans mean faster credit products downstream — the underwriting standards those products carry are set elsewhere, which is where consumer risk will live.
